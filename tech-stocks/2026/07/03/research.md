# Research Notes — July 3, 2026

## Memory Glut Debate — Deeper Context

The selloff priced in a memory glut. On every published construction timeline, one does not exist yet. Key data points:

- **SanDisk:** Closed at $2,273.73 on June 30, fell 23.3% in two sessions. Still +635% YTD from $237.38 starting point. +858% in H1 — best of any S&P 500 stock. Micron's +304% was runner-up.

- **Korea's expansion:** 800 trillion won (~$518B) for four new Samsung/SK Hynix fabs. Timeline: 2027-2028. Construction schedules often slip. Historically, semiconductor capacity booms precede price peaks by 2-4 quarters.

- **HBM supply-demand:** HBM shipments growing at triple-digit rates. Only three HBM3E suppliers (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron). Market tight through at least calendar 2027 per Micron guidance.

- **Key signal to watch:** Next DRAM contract-price data. Continued deceleration keeps the shortage thesis intact. An outright price decline would genuinely change the story.

### Australia Friday Session
The KOSPI rebounded 5.76% on Friday morning (local time) after the Thursday crash. Whether US memory names follow the bounce or extend losses is the first test next week.

## The Meta Compute Thesis — Both Sides

### Bullish for NVDA
If Meta becomes a cloud provider, it buys more Nvidia GPUs, not fewer. More AI compute demand, regardless of seller, flows through NVDA's data center stack. Nvidia's 1.25% dip amid a sector meltdown supports this.

### Bearish for AI infra specialists
- CoreWeave fell 14% — Meta is a $21B customer turning into a competitor.
- Nebius fell 17% — same dynamic.
- AMD fell 6.9% — Meta is the anchor customer of AMD's AI story with a multi-year, multi-gigawatt GPU commitment and ~10% warrant. If Meta has excess compute, does it need more AMD Instinct GPUs?

### Bearish for MU, SNDK, memory
"Excess" + "compute" = "overcapacity" = pricing power erosion. Memory stocks were the most extended winners in H1 2026.

### Bullish for software/cloud
Cheaper compute = better margins for AI-native applications. Explains why NOW, CRM, PLTR caught bids during the hardware selloff.

## OpenAI Efficiency Gains — Another Layer
The Information reported that OpenAI engineers found software optimizations cutting inference costs by more than half, reducing the number of Nvidia GPUs needed for some ChatGPT traffic. If true, this structurally reduces GPU demand growth. Only a single-source report; needs confirmation.

## Apple's China Memory Sourcing Dilemma
Apple is negotiating to buy memory chips from CXMT and YMTC, both on the Pentagon blacklist. Tim Cook contacted Treasury Secretary Bessent directly. Rep. Brian Mast (House committee chair) advised against permission. This could become a political issue given the current administration.

## European Friction for Palantir
Spain advised state-backed companies to avoid new contracts with Palantir citing national security concerns. France and Britain uneasy about tech dependency on US defense infrastructure. Palantir is the de facto OS for NATO, creating tension between sovereignty and alliance obligations.

## Risk: Circular AI Investment
Nvidia offering fast-growing startups access to computing power in exchange for future profits. Already done with Nebius, CoreWeave, OpenAI. This creates circular revenue structures that could unwind in a downturn.

## Consumer Weakness Signal
Nike revenue -1.1%, Greater China -17% currency-neutral. Lululemon flagged North American weakness. If consumer spending softens further, the ultimate funder of all this AI compute (enterprise customers) may tighten budgets.
