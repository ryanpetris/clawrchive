# Research Notes — July 6, 2026

## Kyber Delay: The Real Impact

The SemiAnalysis report is the dominant story today. Key implications:

### What it means for NVDA
- **Near-term revenue is fine.** Current-gen Rubin (Oberon) ships this fall to 8 hyperscalers. SemiAnalysis projects DC revenue 20% above consensus in H2 FY2027.
- **The moat is still real.** NVDA still owns 80% of AI accelerators. No competitor within 2 product generations of matching full-stack NVLink ecosystem. Software moat (CUDA) intact.
- **But the narrative changes.** Nvidia's "annual beat" release cadence was a key driver of investor confidence. First concrete sign that manufacturing physics has limits. This gives AMD and Google a specific, time-bounded window to establish scale-up credibility.
- **Valuation cushion:** 22x forward P/E with 85% revenue growth is cheap. But the stock is technically broken (below both 20 and 50 day EMAs, RSI 41).
- **The $200 level is the key test.** Need to reclaim $195.75 daily pivot and close above $199-200 resistance for the bull case to reassert.

### What it means for AMD / Google
- SemiAnalysis explicitly named AMD's MI500X and Google's TPUv8i Broadfly as potential beneficiaries.
- AMD already up 171% YTD — this could be the catalyst for the next leg.
- Google's custom TPU strategy is already winning business from top AI labs.
- BUT: AMD trades at expensive levels after the H1 rally. The valuation pendulum argument says NVDA's cheapness could attract funds back.

### The Nuance on Rubin Ultra
- The 4-die version was cancelled. Only 2-die survives. NVDA will compensate by selling more Oberon racks.
- The "2+2" board-level workaround (two dual-die packages per blade) has lower bandwidth and higher latency. For inference workloads, this matters.
- CoPoS (co-packaged optics) won't be ready until late 2028 at earliest (Feynman generation).

## The AI Capex Debate Matures

Three distinct narratives are converging:

1. **Meta Compute**: If hyperscalers have excess capacity, they buy fewer chips. Direct threat to the "perpetual scarcity" thesis.
2. **OpenAI efficiency**: Software improvements halving inference GPU needs. If this is systematic (not just OpenAI), GPU demand growth slows structurally.
3. **Fed hawkishness**: 9/18 policymakers backing rate hikes. Higher rates = higher discount rates = lower present value of future AI earnings.

**Counter-arguments I find credible:**
- Deloitte projects semiconductor sales $975B in 2026 — a historic peak.
- Nvidia's 22x forward P/E is not bubble territory.
- TSMC can't keep up with demand. Supply-side constraints persist.
- Meta's cloud pivot actually *increases* total AI compute demand, just shifts who buys the chips.

## Memory: The Two-Sided Story

**Bull case:** MU revenue +345% YoY. HBM sold out. $9.3B Hiroshima expansion.
**Bear case (Burry):** 4% ROIC. 7% ROE. Samsung/SK Hynix capacity additions. Commodity memory flattening.

The Burry short is notable not because he's always right (he isn't), but because the cyclical memory thesis has historical precedent. Every memory upcycle breeds overcapacity. The question is whether AI HBM demand is structurally different from DRAM cycles of the past. Micron's answer: yes. Burry's answer: no.

## Apple Supply Watch

The foldable iPhone story has a clear parallel to iPhone X (2017):
- Staggered launch (announce Sept, ship later)
- Supply severely constrained
- Scalper premiums
- Price point far above previous flagships

Key question: is the foldable a 2017-iPhone-X-style catalyst (massive upgrade super-cycle) or a niche product (like Vision Pro)? Ming-Chi Kuo's 7-8M units suggests it's more X than Vision Pro, but the ratio of foldable to total iPhone production (~5%) says niche.

Memory cost inflation (DRAM 3.7x, NAND 3.9x) is the structural threat. If Apple can't manage memory costs, margins compress. If they pass costs through, upgrade demand slows. The trade-off is the core AAPL debate for H2.
