## 2. White House And President

### U.S.-Iran Deal Announcement

**What happened:** President Trump posted on social media Saturday that a memorandum of understanding with Iran would be signed Sunday, June 14, with immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif confirmed a final text has been agreed. The framework — reportedly called the "Islamabad Agreement" — would extend the ceasefire by 60 days, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and launch new nuclear negotiations. ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/politics/middle-east/261965737-trump-pushes-for-june-14-signing-but-iran-throws-cold-water-tradingkey), [ABC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xG_j4WcTAPc))

**Why it matters:** A deal would end more than three months of active hostilities that began with Operation Epic Fury on Feb. 28, 2026, reopen a waterway through which 20% of global oil and gas passes, and begin addressing Iran's nuclear program. It's Trump's most consequential foreign policy moment of his second term.

**Iranian pushback:** Iran Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei explicitly denied Sunday signing, saying completion is possible "in the coming days." Disagreements remain on Strait of Hormuz navigation fees and disposal of enriched uranium (Iran insists on dilution within its borders). ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/politics/middle-east/261965737-trump-pushes-for-june-14-signing-but-iran-throws-cold-water-tradingkey))

**Israel concerns:** Israeli officials express growing alarm. PM Netanyahu was reportedly not briefed on negotiations and sought details from Trump administration contacts. Critics warn the deal leaves Iran's ballistic missile program and regional proxy support unresolved. ([ILTV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3cR1Bpnu_tI))

**What to watch:** Whether signing occurs Sunday as Trump claims, or in the coming days as Iran suggests. The 60-day negotiation window on nuclear issues will be the next critical phase.

## 3. Congress: Senate

### Reconciliation 2.0 Moves to Floor

**What happened:** Senate voted 53-46 on June 3 to bring the FY2027 budget reconciliation bill to the floor for debate. The bill stalled over the Senate Parliamentarian's Byrd Rule ruling on White House ballroom funding and disagreements over the anti-weaponization fund (which was ultimately dropped after GOP pushback). ([Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/06/08/senate-moves-reconciliation-2-0-to-the-floor-for-debate-after-stalled-negotiations/))

**Why it matters:** This is the second reconciliation package (following the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025). It faces amendment battles, particularly Democratic attempts to permanently block the anti-weaponization fund.

### SASC Passes $1.14T Defense Policy Bill

**What happened:** Senate Armed Services Committee voted 18-9 to advance the FY27 NDAA. Key provisions: creates a Robotic and Autonomous Systems Combatant Command, codifies restrictions on defense contractor stock buybacks/dividends, recommends renaming the Department of Defense to the Department of War. Kaine and Duckworth voted no. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/06/sascs-1-14t-defense-policy-bill-creates-combatant-command-for-drones/))

**Why it matters:** The vote was unusually bipartisan-split. The Pentagon name change is notable symbolically. The new drone command reflects Ukraine war lessons.

### McConnell: No Reconciliation 3.0

**What happened:** Chairman McConnell stated "there will not be another reconciliation bill," echoed by Appropriations Chair Collins. Thune remains noncommittal. ([Vinyl Institute](https://www.vinylinfo.org/news/congress-faces-delays-and-uncertainty-in-the-federal-appropriations-process/))

**Why it matters:** This casts doubt on Trump's $1.5T defense funding strategy that depended on a third reconciliation package.

### Senate Passed ICE Funding Bill (S.2)

**What happened:** Senate passed $70B bill to fund ICE and Border Patrol for remainder of Trump's term. House passed 214-212 on party lines. Sent to Trump. ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4601751/republicans-trump-settlement-fund-fallout/))

### Sen. Mike Lee's Grand Staircase-Escalante CRA Effort Fails

**What happened:** Senator Lee's attempt to use the Congressional Review Act to block the BLM management plan for Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument failed. The 60-session-day deadline passed June 11. ([Earthjustice](https://earthjustice.org/press/2026/senator-lees-attempt-to-fast-track-attack-on-grand-staircase-escalante-national-monument-management-plan-fails))

## 4. Congress: House Of Representatives

### House Fails to Extend FISA Section 702

**What happened:** The House rejected a short-term extension of Section 702 of FISA in bipartisan fashion. Democrats conditioned support on Trump withdrawing acting DNI Bill Pulte's appointment and nominating a permanent replacement. The law was set to expire at midnight Friday (June 12). Senate may attempt its own vote. ([PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-house-expected-to-vote-on-short-term-fisa-extension))

**Key players:** Sen. Warner (D-VA), Sen. Cotton (R-AR), Sen. Grassley (R-IA), acting DNI Bill Pulte.

**Why it matters:** A lapse in Section 702 would be unprecedented and would affect US intelligence-gathering capabilities. The dispute is fundamentally about Trump's refusal to nominate a permanent DNI — his pick Bill Pulte (current FHFA director) has little intelligence experience and has drawn bipartisan criticism.

### Immigration Enforcement Bill Passes House

**What happened:** House passed S.2 (ICE funding bill) 214-212 after the anti-weaponization fund controversy was resolved by DOJ shelving the fund. Rep. Fitzpatrick (R-PA) is gathering signatures for a discharge petition to force a vote on blocking the fund permanently. ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4601751/republicans-trump-settlement-fund-fallout/))

### House-Senate Tax Bill Divide

**What happened:** House passed "One Big Beautiful Bill" ($3.8T) in late May, permanently extending TCJA, cutting Medicaid, boosting defense/border. Senate is assembling a leaner framework to keep CBO score under $3T. Key divergence: Senate may include 12% corporate minimum tax; different SALT cap treatment; more clean energy credit retention. Conference expected mid-July. ([USA Business Times](https://usabusinesstimes.com/house-vs-senate-tax-bill-who-wins-the-2026-fiscal-war/))

## 6. Federal Agencies: Official Announcements

### Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

- **Title V Affirmative Defense Reinstated:** EPA issued final rule June 1 withdrawing the 2023 rule that removed emergency affirmative defense provisions from Title V operating permits. Effective immediately. Effect: permittees can again assert emergency affirmative defense against enforcement actions for exceedances of technology-based emission limitations during emergencies. ([JDSupra/Mayer Brown](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/epa-restores-title-v-affirmative-8542879/))
- **EPA Asks 9th Circuit for Rehearing:** EPA petitioned for rehearing of ruling requiring ESA Section 7 consultation before issuing CWA water quality criteria for cadmium. Filed May 28 petition. ([InsideEPA](https://insideepa.com/daily-news/epa-asks-court-rehear-ruling-requiring-esa-review-water-criteria))

### Internal Revenue Service (IRS)

- **IRS Notice 2025-42 Vacated:** U.S. District Court vacated IRS Notice 2025-42 which set "beginning of construction" requirements for wind/solar tax credits, finding it arbitrary and capricious under the APA. Remanded to agency. ([Court ruling via Novoco](https://www.novoco.com/public-media/documents/court-ruling-oec-irs-notice-2025-42-06062026.pdf))

### Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) / CMS

- HHS administrative penalty authority for nursing homes upheld by 4th Circuit — Seventh Amendment does not guarantee jury trial in Medicare administrative penalty proceedings. ([4th Circuit opinion](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/251669.P.pdf))
