## 2. White House And President

### Iran Deal Announcement

**What happened**: President Trump announced on Thursday, June 11, that a "great settlement" had been reached with Iran to end the three-month-old war. He said the Strait of Hormuz would reopen upon signing, which could happen "over the weekend in Europe" with Vice President Vance representing the U.S. The announcement came hours after Trump threatened "very hard" strikes against Tehran. [Source](https://political.org/2026/06/11/trump-says-settlement-reached-with-iran-signing-possible-this-weekend/)

**Why it matters**: The war with Iran, which began on February 28 with U.S.-Israeli strikes, has killed thousands and sent global energy prices sharply higher. Approximately 20% of global petroleum passes through the Strait of Hormuz, which has been effectively blocked during the conflict. Gasoline prices and the war's unpopularity have driven Trump's approval ratings down, and Republicans have openly worried about midterm consequences. [Source](https://quews.news/iran-says-no-final-2/)

**Policy/political implications**: If the deal holds, it would be the most significant diplomatic achievement of Trump's second term. If it collapses or proves to be premature, it could further damage U.S. credibility and Trump's political standing. The use of "settlement" rather than "treaty" may carry specific legal implications regarding congressional oversight requirements.

**What to watch next**: Whether a deal is actually signed this weekend. Iran's foreign ministry has stated no final decision has been reached. Israel has said it is not a party to the memorandum of understanding but expressed appreciation for Trump's commitment to certain terms. [Source](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/june-11-2026-pbs-news-hour-full-episode)

**Key uncertainties**: Specific terms of the deal have not been disclosed. Key questions include what concessions Iran made on uranium enrichment and inspections, and what sanctions relief or security guarantees the U.S. offered.

### Jay Clayton DNI Nomination

**What happened**: Trump announced on social media that he plans to nominate Jay Clayton — former SEC chairman and current U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York — as director of national intelligence. This comes amid intense pushback over naming FHFA head Bill Pulte as acting DNI. [Source](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2026/jun/12/trump-to-name-clayton-as-pick-for-intel-director/)

**Why it matters**: The Pulte appointment triggered a Democratic blockade of FISA Section 702 renewal. Clayton, who has significantly more national security credentials and bipartisan respect, may unlock the impasse. However, Trump stated he still plans to keep Pulte in the role "for a little while."

**What to watch next**: The Senate Intelligence Committee has scheduled a confirmation hearing for Wednesday. Senate Majority Leader Thune says the Senate will "move quickly." It is unclear whether Clayton can be confirmed before Pulte is set to take over on June 19.

### Kennedy Center Name Appeal

**What happened**: The Trump administration filed a notice of appeal Thursday challenging Judge Christopher Cooper's May 29 order requiring Trump's name be removed from the Kennedy Center. The handpicked board voted to seek a stay, which will be formally filed Friday. [Source](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/white-house-appeal-judge-order-remove-trump-name-kennedy-center/)

**Why it matters**: The case tests the limits of executive authority over congressionally chartered institutions. Judge Cooper ruled only Congress can change the Kennedy Center's name. The board's shift from compliance to appeal represents a reversal from a June 4 memo instructing staff to drop Trump's name.

### Third Reconciliation Bill Push

**What happened**: Trump called on congressional Republicans to "immediately" pass a third budget reconciliation package including $350 billion in defense spending and the stalled SAVE America Act (voter ID/citizenship verification). [Source](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trumps-push-350-billion-arsenal-freedom-hits-gop-skepticism)

**Why it matters**: This request came just hours after signing the $70B immigration enforcement reconciliation bill. Republicans are deeply skeptical, with Sen. John Kennedy calling it a "very, very long shot." Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins said "reconciliation is not the best approach."

## 3. Congress: Senate

### FISA Standoff

**What happened**: A fast-track Senate attempt to extend Section 702 FISA powers collapsed Wednesday after Democrats indicated they would object. Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA) said the only way he'd support an extension is if Principal Deputy DNI Aaron Lukas serves as acting DNI during the extension period — effectively demanding Pulte be removed. [Source](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/mo/st-louis/politics/2026/06/11/house-fisa-section-702-vote)

**Why it matters**: Section 702 expires at midnight Friday, June 12. The lapse would put the U.S. in a legal gray area regarding foreign intelligence surveillance. Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) and Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) have warned the administration to prepare for "a potential significant gap in foreign intelligence collection."

### Senate GOP Divided on Tax Bill

**What happened**: Senate Republicans led by fiscal hawks including Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI) are questioning the CBO math on the $3.8 trillion American Prosperity and Competitiveness Act. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.87%, its highest since February. [Source](https://usabusinesstimes.com/senate-republicans-break-with-white-house-over-big-tax-bills-deficit-math/)

**Why it matters**: The bill extends Trump-era tax cuts, slashes corporate rate to 18%, and includes $700B in Medicaid reductions. Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) said "a lot of races that shouldn't be in play, are in play."

### Senate Democratic Unity
