## Iran Deal — Deep Dive

### Timeline of Events (June 10-11)
- **June 10 (Wed)**: US launches airstrikes on Iran after Trump blames Tehran for downing a US Army helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's IRGC claims it struck 21 US military sites in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan.
- **June 11 (Thu AM)**: Trump threatens to strike Iran "very hard tonight" and hints at seizing Kharg Island oil infrastructure.
- **June 11 (Thu PM)**: Trump announces he has cancelled strikes and a "great settlement" has been reached. Says signing could happen "over the weekend in Europe."
- **Iran response**: Foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei says Tehran "has not reached a final conclusion."

### Key Context
- War began Feb 28 with US-Israeli strikes on Iran
- Ceasefire declared April 8 but repeatedly strained
- The conflict has killed thousands and sent global energy prices sharply higher
- ~20% of global petroleum supply passes through the Strait of Hormuz
- Trump's approval ratings have been sinking amid voter anger over high gasoline prices
- Some Republicans have worried the war could cost them control of Congress

### Israel's Position
- PM Netanyahu's office said Israel is "not a party to the memorandum of understanding with Iran"
- Netanyahu expressed appreciation for Trump's commitment to terms including removing enriched material, dismantling enrichment infrastructure, limiting missile output, and ending support for regional proxies

### Open Questions
- What are the specific terms? (nuclear, oil, sanctions)
- Did Supreme Leader Khamenei actually approve? (Trump says yes, no confirmation)
- Will this hold given past failed claims of imminent deals?
- What happens to the Strait of Hormuz blockade?
- Congressional oversight - will this require Senate ratification?

### Analysis
Trump's use of "settlement" rather than "treaty" or "agreement" is legally significant. The term may carry specific implications for whether congressional approval is required. Given Republican control of Congress, this is less of an obstacle than it would be under divided government, but some GOP hawks may demand scrutiny.

The whiplash from "bomb them tonight" to "great settlement" in a single day is notable even by Trump's standards. This pattern — threatening maximum force, then declaring victory/diplomatic breakthrough — has been a hallmark of his approach to international negotiations.
