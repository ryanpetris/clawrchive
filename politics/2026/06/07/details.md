## 3. Congress: Senate

### $70B Immigration Enforcement Bill Passes
- **What happened:** The Senate voted 52-47 early Friday to pass legislation providing $70 billion to ICE ($38.2B) and CBP/Border Patrol ($26B) through the rest of Trump's term. The bill now goes to the House.
- **Why it matters:** This is the largest single immigration enforcement funding package in recent history. It funds Trump's mass deportation program through 2028 and protects the agencies from shutdown fights.
- **Key players:** Senate GOP leaders overcame a rebellion from Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) and other holdouts who demanded language to kill Trump's "anti-weaponization" DOJ settlement fund. Democrats led by Chuck Schumer attempted to strip the fund but failed.
- **Expected next step:** House consideration. The bill is likely to pass the Republican-controlled House.
- **Source:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-settlement-fund-ice-border-patrol-vote-93b9f5b487997b629d87bf59a046d7ec), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/04/politics/senate-trump-weaponization-fund-immigration), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/senate-passes-70-billion-immigration-enforcement-bill-what-included-12036271)

### Senators Warn on Surveillance Program Expiration
- **What happened:** Sens. Tom Cotton (R-AR) and Chuck Grassley (R-IA) urged the Trump administration to prepare for the possible expiration of a contentious intelligence-gathering authority (FISA Section 702-related).
- **Why it matters:** The program is a key counterterrorism and foreign intelligence tool. Its expiration could create intelligence gaps.
- **Source:** [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/06/us/politics/cotton-grassley-surveillance-program.html)

### Bob Packwood Dies at 93

## 4. Congress: House of Representatives

### House Passes SAVE Act
- **What happened:** The House passed a new version of the SAVE Act (S. 1383), requiring American citizens to show a birth certificate or passport to register to vote. The bill also requires photo ID to vote.
- **Why it matters:** Critics — including the Brennan Center for Justice — say it would block 21M eligible voters who lack such documents. It would eliminate mail, online, and voter registration drive options.
- **Key players:** House GOP leadership pushed the bill through. Senate Minority Leader Schumer has previously declared it "dead on arrival" in the Senate.
- **Expected next step:** The Senate will take it up, where its fate is uncertain. Previous versions died without a Senate vote.
- **Status:** Confirmed. House passed; Senate consideration pending.
- **Source:** [Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/house-passes-new-version-save-act-brennan-center-responds), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/02/12/what-is-the-save-act-voter-id-rules/88642716007/)

### House Iran War Powers Resolution
- **What happened:** The House voted 215-208 to adopt a war powers resolution directing Trump to withdraw U.S. forces from Iran or seek congressional approval.
- **Why it matters:** This was the fourth House attempt this year and the first since the conflict crossed the 90-day War Powers Act threshold. The war began Feb. 28 without congressional authorization.
- **Key players:** Four Republicans crossed party lines — Reps. Thomas Massie (KY), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA), Tom Barrett (MI), and Warren Davidson (OH). Speaker Mike Johnson opposed it, arguing it would weaken Trump's negotiating hand. Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY) co-sponsored.
- **Expected next step:** The Senate advanced a similar resolution in May but has not held a floor vote. Even if passed by both chambers, concurrent resolutions lack force of law and would face certain veto.
- **Source:** [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cj6pldg39deo), [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/06/04/trump-iran-war-powers-resolution-house-republicans/), [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/03/us/politics/house-vote-trump-iran-war-powers.html)

### Republican Defections Growing
- **What happened:** Multiple stories highlight increasingly frequent GOP defections from Trump positions, including on Iran, immigration fund structure, and SAVE Act details.
- **Analysis:** Vulnerable Republican members are positioning for a tough midterm environment. Democrats argue the GOP majority is "unraveling," but Republicans may be strategically distancing themselves to retain seats rather than breaking on principle.
- **Source:** [CNN](https://us.cnn.com/2026/06/06/politics/republicans-defy-trump-agenda-midterms), [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/06/06/republicans-trump-midterms)

## 7. Federal Agencies: News and Reported Developments

### FBI
- **What is being reported:** FBI Director Kash Patel fired at least five analysts (four intelligence analysts and one supervisory analyst) involved in the 2023 "Richmond memo" on Radical Traditionalist Catholic ideology.
- **Status:** Confirmed by AP, CBS, LA Times, multiple sources. The analysts were fired Friday.
- **Background:** The January 2023 memo from the FBI's Richmond field office explored links between racially motivated violent extremists and radical Catholic ideology. It was withdrawn under former Director Chris Wray. A 2024 DOJ Inspector General report found departures from proper tradecraft but no evidence of malicious intent or anti-Catholic bias.
- **Why it matters:** The firings are the latest in a series of personnel actions under Patel targeting FBI employees involved in Trump-related investigations or politically sensitive work. Critics say it creates a "culture of fear" at the bureau.
- **What to watch:** Potential legal challenges from the fired analysts, whose lawyer David Laufman called the action "manifestly unjust."
- **Source:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/fbi-kash-patel-firings-e9793d06e6310bfcd848b55bf8c47cc6), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fbi-fires-analysts-richmond-memo-catholic-extremist-ideology/), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-06-06/fbi-fires-several-analysts-tied-to-disputed-catholic-ideology-memo)

- **Key cases pending:**
