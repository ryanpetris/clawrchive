# Research Notes — Daily Political Outlook, June 7, 2026

## Immigration Enforcement Bill — Additional Context

- The $70B bill provides funding through the end of Trump's term (2028), effectively locking in deportation infrastructure regardless of which party controls Congress after the 2026 midterms.
- The DOJ "anti-weaponization" settlement fund ($1.776B) was the flashpoint. Sen. Tillis (R-NC) demanded it be killed in writing. Leadership arm-twisting overcame the rebellion.
- Democrats argued ICE and Border Patrol already have ~$100B in unspent funds from a larger DHS package enacted last year.
- The 52-47 vote was almost entirely along party lines. One Republican voted no (Collins? Murkowski? — not fully confirmed by available snippets).

## SAVE Act

- The Brennan Center is the primary source opposing the bill. Their figure of 21M citizens lacking documents has been cited by multiple outlets.
- The bill prohibits student IDs (even from state universities) and accepts tribal IDs only with expiration dates (many tribal IDs don't have them).
- Previous version (H.R. 22) passed House in February 2026. This newest version is S. 1383.
- Original version would have required document presentation *every time* someone voted. This was replaced with a photo ID mandate.

## Iran War Powers Resolution

- Four House Republicans have now crossed party lines on Iran war powers measures. The defectors vary by vote — Massie has been the most consistent.
- The 90-day War Powers Act deadline was a key legal point. The House had previously failed to pass this resolution (212-212 tie on May 14).
- The war began February 28, 2026. Trump labeled it a "skirmish" and "short-term excursion."
- Concurrent resolutions do not require presidential signature and cannot be vetoed in the traditional sense, but they also carry no force of law.

## FBI Richmond Memo Firings

- Previous DOJ IG report (2024) concluded: "no evidence of malicious intent or an improper purpose."
- The memo was titled: "Interest of Racially or Ethnically Motivated Violent Extremists in Radical-Traditionalist Catholic Ideology Almost Certainly Presents New Mitigation Opportunities"
- It originated from a domestic terrorism investigation of a Virginia extremist who joined a Catholic sect not recognized by the Vatican.
- The January 2023 memo led then-Director Wray to apologize in congressional hearings.
- Task Force to Eradicate Anti-Christian Bias (created by Trump EO) cited the memo in its report.
- The Southern Poverty Law Center, which provided some information for the memo, is now under a controversial fraud indictment.
- This is the second major round of political firings under Patel — the first targeted counterintelligence agents who worked on the Mar-a-Lago classified documents investigation.

## Supreme Court Term

- *Trump v. Barbara* challenges 14th Amendment birthright citizenship as applied to children of undocumented immigrants and visa holders. Could be the most consequential citizenship ruling since *Wong Kim Ark* (1898).
- The independent agency cases ask Court to overturn *Humphrey's Executor* (1935), which has protected agency independence for 90+ years.
- FTC case: conservatives signaled willingness to abandon protections for multi-member commission members.
- Fed case: Justices have signaled they see the Fed differently — described as "uniquely structured, quasi-private entity."
- Campaign finance case could expand *Citizens United* logic to party coordinated expenditures.
