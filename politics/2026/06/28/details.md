## 3. Congress: Senate

### Iran War Powers Reversal
- **What happened:** The Senate voted 50–48 on June 23 to approve a war powers resolution directing Trump to remove US forces from Iran hostilities — the first time both chambers had passed such a measure. But the next day, Trump stormed a closed-door Senate Republican lunch, refusing to sign a bipartisan housing bill and engaging in a shouting match with senators. Four Republicans had crossed party lines; Trump singled out Sens. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), Rand Paul (R-KY), Susan Collins (R-ME), and Todd Young (R-IN).
- **The reversal:** On June 24 late night, the Senate voted 47–50–1 to block a separate Kaine-led war powers resolution. Cassidy and Paul shifted their votes. Paul voted "present," saying Trump's remarks affected his vote.
- **Cassidy's explanation:** On Face the Nation (June 28), Cassidy said he received a briefing from VP Vance and Special Envoy Witkoff in the Situation Room. He acknowledged the White House has seen polling showing the American people understand the importance of vaccines — referencing measles concerns.
- **Why it matters:** The episode reveals deep GOP unease with the war but an unwillingness to break with Trump. The initial resolution's passage was historic; its effective reversal within 24 hours demonstrates Trump's continued grip over congressional Republicans.
- **Next:** Sen. Tim Kaine (D-VA) may reintroduce or modify the resolution. Democratic leadership is expected to keep pressure on vulnerable Republican incumbents.
- **Source:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/senate-republicans-reject-war-powers-resolution-trump-cassidy-clash-rcna351703), [AP](https://apnews.com/article/senate-iran-war-powers-resolution-trump-7462a9a561103f531d995aac91f9fc96), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-rejects-measure-restrict-trump-iran-war-powers-as-key-republicans-shift-votes/), [CBS Face the Nation transcript](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bill-cassidy-louisiana-senator-face-the-nation-transcript-06-28-2026/)

- **Iran claims:** Much of the reporting on Iranian military actions comes from IRGC statements, which carry propaganda risk. Cross-referenced with CENTCOM, Bahrain, and Kuwait official statements where possible.
- **SCOTUS speculation:** All predictions about pending decisions are inherently uncertain. The Court's internal dynamics and compromise processes are opaque.
- **Midterm polling:** Early primary results are not necessarily predictive of general election outcomes. The political environment could shift significantly by November.
- **Economic data:** PCE data is subject to revision. The 4.1% figure is the initial estimate.
- **War powers legal status:** The Senate-rejected Kaine resolution was non-binding. The House-passed version is also non-binding. Congress has not formally authorized or declared war.
