## Deeper Analysis / Discarded Items

### US-Iran: The Ceasefire Architecture
The June 17 US-Iran framework agreement (MOU) was reportedly a 14-point document aimed at halting the war that began February 28, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and beginning talks on Iran's nuclear program. The current exchanges represent the first major test of that framework. Iran's attack on the M/V Ever Lovely (June 25) was described by Iran's parliamentary national security chair Ebrahim Azizi as "ceasefire management" rather than a breach — suggesting Iran believes it can calibrate pressure below the threshold of full ceasefire collapse. Trump's "complete the job" rhetoric suggests the US may have a higher threshold for what constitutes a violation.

### The Mamdani Factor
Shahana Mamdani's victory in the NYC mayoral race (2025) is being treated by Republican strategists as a gift — they see an opportunity to tie every Democratic candidate to the left-most flank of the party. The New York primaries (June 23) saw Mamdani-backed candidates win several House primaries. The Guardian noted Republican strategists "believe rise of Mamdani could present opportunity to tag Democrats with most extreme views of the left." This could backfire if Mamdani-style candidates appeal to swing voters on economic populism.

### The Cassidy Flip — Deeper Context
Sen. Bill Cassidy's reversal on the Iran war powers vote is the most telling individual moment of the week. Cassidy:
1. Initially voted for the resolution (June 23)
2. Was shouted at by Trump at the June 24 closed-door lunch
3. Received a classified Situation Room briefing from Vance and Witkoff
4. Changed his vote (June 24)
5. Went on Face the Nation (June 28) and explained the briefing helped

The implication is that intelligence briefings are being used as a whip tool — providing information to wavering senators that is used to justify pro-administration votes.

### Stephen Miller's Birthright Citizenship Demand
Stephen Miller's call for a "unanimous" 9-0 ruling overturning birthright citizenship is notable because it signals the White House knows this is a tough case. If the administration expected to win 6-3, Miller wouldn't need to demand unanimity. This suggests internal concern the Court may rule against Trump or split in an unexpected way.
