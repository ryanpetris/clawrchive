# Research Notes - June 21, 2026

## US-Iran Nuclear Talks - Deep Analysis

### Key Players on the Ground in Switzerland
- **US Delegation:** VP JD Vance (leading), Steve Witkoff (special envoy), Jared Kushner
- **Iranian Delegation:** Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf (leading), Foreign Ministry officials
- **Mediators:** Qatar and Pakistan
- **Location:** Bürgenstock resort, near Lucerne, Switzerland

### The Interim Deal (Signed June 17)
- 14-point memorandum of understanding
- 60-day ceasefire extension for negotiations
- Allows Iran to sell oil freely
- Paves way for release of frozen assets (~$100B estimated worldwide)
- Calls for Iran to dilute highly enriched uranium stockpile
- Commercial vessels can pass Strait of Hormuz without charge for 60 days

### Tensions Threatening the Deal
1. **Lebanon:** Israeli strikes continuing despite ceasefire calls. 83 killed in 24 hours before Saturday's renewed ceasefire. Israel says responding to Hezbollah attacks. Neither Israel nor Hezbollah are parties to the US-Iran deal.
2. **Strait of Hormuz:** IRGC announced closure citing Israeli attacks in Lebanon. US military says commercial vessels still operating. Trump threatened US tolls if no deal in 60 days.
3. **Domestic Criticism:** Bipartisan pushback. Sen. Cassidy (R-LA) called it "worst foreign policy blunder in decades." Sen. Schumer (D-NY) called it "surrender." Sen. Kennedy (R-LA) urged giving peace a chance.
4. **Iranian Politics:** Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei criticized the deal, said Trump sought it from weakness. Iran pressing for $24B in initial asset releases vs. $6B proposed by US via Qatar.

### Israeli Public Opinion (Agam Institute-Hebrew University Poll)
- 92.1% believe Iran emerged as the winner
- 82.9% say Israel's long-term security weakened
- 72.5% reject Netanyahu's claim of strategic gains
- 87.8% say Israel failed or only partially met objectives
- Nearly half would support renewed large-scale action against Hezbollah even at risk of confrontation with Trump

### Market Implications
- Oil futures dropped ~8% after deal announcement
- Markets expected to closely track Switzerland talks when trading opens Sunday evening
- Gasoline prices remain a political vulnerability for Trump ahead of summer travel
- Strait of Hormuz transits one-fifth of global traded oil and natural gas

## Education Department Restructuring - Deeper Context

### Timeline of Dismantling
- **Jan 2025:** Trump signs EO directing McMahon to facilitate closure
- **Throughout 2025:** Staff cuts, 7 of 12 regional offices closed
- **Nov 2025:** First interagency agreements (IAAs) for K-12 programs to Labor Dept.
- **Sept 2025:** $30M+ in competitive IDEA grants canceled
- **June 16, 2026:** IAAs signed for special education (to HHS) and civil rights (to DOJ)
- **Total:** 14 IAAs with 6 federal agencies

### Agency Staffing Reductions
- OSERS: shrunk ~1/3 since 2024
- OCR: ~40% smaller
- DOJ Education Opportunities Section: shrunk by half
- During government shutdown: admin fired nearly all special education workers, later reversed

### Bipartisan Congressional Resistance
- Senate HELP Committee: Chairman Cassidy (R-LA) opposes moving special ed to HHS
- Cassidy committed to Sen. Kaine (D-VA) for July committee vote on blocking measure
- Cassidy suggested Labor Dept. instead of HHS for special education
- Several moderate Republicans on the panel could provide momentum

### RFK Jr. Controversies Relevant to HHS Role

- Claimed children with autism should be "re-parented"
