**Confirmed: War powers resolution passes both chambers (June 23-24)**
- **What happened:** H.Con.Res. 86 — directing the president to remove US armed forces from hostilities against Iran without congressional authorization — passed the Senate 50-48 on June 23, following House passage 215-208 on June 3. This is the first time since the 1973 War Powers Act that both chambers passed such a resolution.
- **Republican defectors (Senate):** Collins (ME), Paul (KY), Cassidy (LA), Murkowski (AK). Fetterman (D-PA) voted against.
- **Republican defectors (House):** Massie (KY), Fitzpatrick (PA), Barrett (MI), Davidson (OH).
- **Senate walk-back (June 24):** One day later, the Senate rejected a similar measure 47-50-1 after Trump berated Republicans at a tense lunch. Paul and Cassidy switched positions.
- **Why it matters:** Though non-binding concurrent resolutions don't require the president's signature, the vote is a major bipartisan rebuke. It signals that congressional patience with the Iran conflict is thin, even among Republicans. However, enforcement mechanisms are weak — the resolution has no force of law unless enacted as a joint resolution with a veto-proof majority.
- **Source:** [CNN - Senate votes to limit Iran war powers](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/23/politics/senate-iran-war-powers-vote); [CNN - Senate backtracks](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/24/politics/senate-backtracks-iran-war-powers-vote); [Defense News/Reuters](https://www.defensenews.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/06/24/us-senate-joins-house-in-voting-to-iran-war/)

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

**Supreme Court — *Mullin v. Doe* (TPS termination)**
- **Ruling:** 6-3, June 25. The Court held that federal law (8 U.S.C. § 1252(a)(2)(B)(ii)) strips courts of jurisdiction to review the Secretary of DHS's determinations regarding TPS termination. Alito wrote for the majority; the Court also rejected equal-protection claims based on alleged racial animus.
- **Impact:** Clears the way for termination of TPS for Haiti (~350,000 people), Syria (~6,000), and likely all 13 countries where termination is pending, affecting up to 1.3 million people.
- **Next:** DHS is expected to issue termination notices for remaining TPS countries. Congressional response possible but unlikely given GOP support for the policy.
- **Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/supreme-court-allows-trump-administration-to-end-removal-protections-for-syrian-and-haitian-nati/); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/immigration-supreme-court-haiti-syria-tps-1bbbf8115f984a0d53336656924e989d)

**War Powers resolution — legal status**
- H.Con.Res. 86 (concurrent resolution) does not require the president's signature and has no force of law under Congress's current interpretation, though sponsors argue the 1973 War Powers Act makes it binding. Legal experts are divided. For enforcement, Congress would need a joint resolution passed over a veto — requiring 2/3 of both chambers, a threshold not currently met.
