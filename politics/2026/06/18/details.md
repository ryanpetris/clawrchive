## 2. White House And President

### Iran Memorandum of Understanding

**What happened:** Senior administration officials publicly read aloud the full 14-paragraph text of the U.S.-Iran MoU for the first time on Wednesday. The agreement includes:
- Immediate termination of military operations on all fronts, including in Lebanon
- U.S. Treasury waivers for Iranian oil exports, petroleum products, and associated services
- U.S. commitment to end naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz within 30 days
- Iran to restore Strait of Hormuz traffic to pre-war levels, with no charge for commercial ships for 60 days
- Framework for at least $300 billion in reconstruction and economic development for Iran (officials stress this does not commit U.S. funds)
- U.S. commitment to withdraw forces from "proximity of the Islamic Republic of Iran" within 30 days after a final deal
- Commitment to negotiate fate of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and future enrichment activities
- Eventual termination of U.N. sanctions, primary U.S. sanctions, and secondary sanctions as part of a final agreement
- No mention of Iran's ballistic missile or drone capabilities

**Why it matters:** This is the most significant U.S. foreign policy development since the Iran war began. The MoU stops short of resolving the central nuclear dispute, leaving the hardest questions for a 60-day negotiation window. The lack of specificity on nuclear enrichment, ballistic missiles, and verification has drawn sharp criticism from hawks in both parties and from Israeli officials.

**What to watch next:** The formal signing ceremony is expected Friday in Geneva, with VP JD Vance representing the U.S. Iran's official news agency IRNA published the full MoU text Wednesday evening. Pakistan, which mediated the deal, announced the MoU "enters into force with immediate effect" Thursday. Meanwhile, a U.S. source tells PBS News that Iran has continued firing drones at commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday's cease-fire announcement — the U.S. military has shot them down.

**Official position vs. media reporting:** White House officials describe the MoU as final and binding, while Trump simultaneously characterized it as a framework subject to 60 days of negotiation and threatened renewed bombing if Iran does not comply. The gap between these characterizations is significant.

### Trump Derails Clayton DNI Confirmation

**What happened:** In an early-morning Truth Social post from France Wednesday, Trump announced he was canceling Jay Clayton's scheduled Senate Intelligence Committee confirmation hearing. He said Clayton would not go forward "until Jamie McDonald is approved to be U.S. Attorney" (to replace Clayton as SDNY U.S. Attorney). He also said he would not approve FISA renewal without the SAVE America Act, and that Bill Pulte would remain as acting DNI.

**Why it matters:** Clayton was on track for near-unanimous confirmation as early as Thursday (tomorrow), which would have prevented Pulte from ever taking the DNI post. Trump's move guarantees Pulte — a Trump loyalist with no intelligence background who has used his current perch at FHFA to target perceived adversaries — will become acting DNI when Tulsi Gabbard leaves office Friday. Democrats have said they will not provide votes for FISA renewal while Pulte is in charge.

**What to watch next:** Pulte becomes acting DNI Friday. The FISA Section 702 program expired Saturday. There is no clear path to renewal given Trump's linkage to the SAVE America Act, which lacks the votes to pass the Senate.

### Defense Production Act invoked for munitions

**What happened:** Trump signed a memo invoking the Defense Production Act to address "systemic constraints in the munitions industrial base." The memo, released Tuesday, delegates Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to authorize voluntary agreements to boost production of munitions, missiles, and equipment.

**Why it matters:** The Iran war consumed nearly half of U.S. Patriot interceptor stockpiles and over 50% of THAAD systems, per a CSIS analysis. This is a direct industrial-policy response to combat depletion.

### OMB Secret Service Fund Transfer

**What happened:** OMB apportioned $351.6 million from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025 tax cuts law) to the Secret Service for "White House Security Measures." The law restricts the funds to Secret Service resources. An OMB official brought up the ballroom unprompted when asked about it, saying it's funded by private donors — but lawmakers in both parties suspect otherwise.

**Why it matters:** The Washington Post reported this week that internal estimates put the ballroom cost at $600 million, over half from taxpayers — undercutting Trump's repeated promise of private-only funding. Congress previously denied Trump's request for ballroom funding. The D.C. Circuit has allowed above-ground construction to continue pending appeal of a district judge's halt order.

## 3. Congress: Senate

### Trump-Thune rift reaches boiling point

**What happened:** Punchbowl News reports, based on conversations with nearly two dozen GOP senators Wednesday, that Trump is seeking to "obliterate Senate traditions" — particularly the filibuster and the blue-slip policy for judicial and U.S. attorney nominees. Trump blames Majority Leader Thune for not delivering on these priorities. During the closed-door GOP lunch, Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) and Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) accused Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah) of leading Trump to believe the SAVE America Act could pass, thereby prompting Trump's unrealistic demands. The lunch was described as a "pile-on" against Lee.

**Why it matters:** The Senate GOP conference is fracturing internally at a time when unified party control of government should enable legislative action. Thune is caught between Trump's demands and the reality that there aren't 50 GOP votes to gut the filibuster or pass the SAVE Act. Trump-backed primary challengers have already toppled two GOP incumbents, adding to the pressure.

**Source:**
- [Punchbowl: Behind the scenes of a Senate-Trump rift](https://punchbowl.news/article/senate/senate-trump-rift/)

### Clayton Confirmation Postponed

**What happened:** After Trump ordered Clayton not to appear, Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Tom Cotton (R-Ark.) initially said the hearing would proceed, then two hours later announced it was postponed, calling Trump's move "regrettable."

**Why it matters:** This is a remarkable public breach between a Senate committee chair and his party's president. Cotton, a Trump ally, was forced to publicly reverse himself.

### Democrats introduce CRA resolution on ACA rule

**What happened:** Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) and Sens. Tammy Baldwin (D-Wis.) and Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) introduced a Congressional Review Act resolution Wednesday to block the CMS 2027 payment parameters rule, which they say will raise out-of-pocket costs and make it harder to enroll in ACA plans.

**Why it matters:** The measure is unlikely to pass the Republican-controlled Congress, but Democrats need only 30 senators to sign a discharge petition to force a floor vote. This gives them a campaign weapon on healthcare costs — a top voter concern — heading into November's midterms.

**Why it matters:** This represents the Democratic leadership's framing of the DNI/FISA crisis — as a deliberate act of hostage-taking by the president — and sets up oversight demands if Pulte takes office.

### Office of Management and Budget (proposed rule)
- **Type:** Proposed rule (government-wide grants management)
- **What:** OMB and 41 federal agencies proposed sweeping changes to federal grants management (2 CFR). Key provisions: prohibit funding for DEI, "gender ideology," or pediatric gender procedures; require E-Verify for all recipients; allow termination of awards deemed not to advance "the national interest"; pre-application reviews to ensure alignment with presidential priorities.
- **Why it matters:** If finalized, this would be the most significant restructuring of federal grant administration in decades, giving agencies broad discretion to terminate awards based on policy alignment.
- **Effective date:** Proposed October 1, 2026. Comments due July 13.
