## Deep Analysis: US-Iran MOU Signing Today

The formal signing ceremony in Bürgenstock, Switzerland today is the culmination of the G7-adjacent negotiations. Key unresolved questions:

- **Enrichment:** Whether Iran retains any enrichment capability is the most contentious unresolved issue. The MOU's text (14 points) is silent on this. CNN reports auxiliary proposals exist but were not signed by Iran.
- **Sanctions relief timing:** The MOU says sanctions will be terminated on "an agreed upon schedule as part of the final deal" — but what constitutes a "final deal" is undefined.
- **Hormuz passage:** Only 60 days of toll-free passage guaranteed; does not preclude future fees.
- **Congressional role:** The MOU was sent to Congress after signing, not before. Wicker (R-MS) and other Republicans criticizing suggests potential legislative pushback. Corker review period may apply.
- **Senate briefing:** Thune says briefing expected "early next week."

**Iranian calculus:** Iran has not signed the auxiliary documents, giving them leverage. The "performance-based" structure means the US must show it is delivering sanctions relief before Iran must fully comply — a reversal of the traditional sequence.

**Watch for:** Israeli reaction, Gulf state reactions, oil price movement, Congressional resolutions of disapproval.

## Deep Analysis: Warsh Fed — Regime Change

Warsh's first FOMC meeting (June 16-17) was deliberately disruptive:

- **Forward guidance eliminated.** Warsh: "As a general proposition, forward guidance isn't the business we should be in."
- **SEP changes.** Warsh did not submit his own economic projections (the "missing dot"). The statement was shortened dramatically.
- **Task forces.** Five announced: communications strategy, monetary policy toolkit, balance sheet, financial stability, and AI/technology.
- **Hawkish surprise.** Markets expected Warsh to be a Trump-aligned dove. Instead, he focused on inflation which has been above 2% for five years. Rate hike odds for September jumped.
- **Trump's reaction.** Muted — Trump said "it's all right, whatever" from France, a notably softer tone than his Powell-era attacks.

**Uncertainty:** With forward guidance gone, markets are now data-dependent. Next major data point: PCE inflation report later this month. Oil prices (falling as Iran tensions de-escalate) may give Warsh cover to avoid hikes.

## White House Ballroom: Timeline of Funding Controversy

1. Congress passed $1.2B for Secret Service in the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" for hiring, training, technology post-assassination attempts.
2. Trump admin separately requested $1B for White House ballroom in DHS bill — Congress rejected it (both parties).
3. Late Friday, OMB quietly shifted $352-397M from the Secret Service accounts to "White House Security Measures."
4. Contractor estimated ballroom project now exceeding $600M — more than half from government entities, not private donors.
5. Sens. Collins (R-ME) and other Republicans expressing concern.
6. Legal challenges to the ballroom already pending.

**Separation of powers issue:** Constitution gives Congress sole power over appropriations. OMB's transfer may exceed statutory authority since the $1.2B was specifically designated for Secret Service resources. The Impoundment Control Act may be relevant.

## DOJ Denaturalization Escalation

Historical denaturalization cases:
- 1990-2017 average: ~11/year
- FY2026 target: 250 cases

The program relies on 18 U.S.C. § 1451(a), which allows revocation of citizenship obtained illegally or by fraud. The administration broadened priority categories last year to include lower-level offenses. Each case requires federal court approval. Targets become green card holders (not non-citizens) and face deportation.

**Remaining uncertainty:** How many cases have actually been filed so far? DOJ says "several dozen" in recent weeks. How many will survive judicial scrutiny?
