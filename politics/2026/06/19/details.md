## 2. White House and President

### US-Iran MOU Signed at G7; Formal Ceremony Today

**What happened:** President Trump signed a 14-point Memorandum of Understanding with Iran on Wednesday night in Versailles, France, following the G7 summit. The full text was released Thursday. A formal signing ceremony is scheduled for today in Bürgenstock, Switzerland. The MOU commits the US to terminating all sanctions against Iran (including UN, IAEA, and unilateral sanctions) on an agreed schedule in exchange for Iran permanently blocking its path to nuclear weapons. The US draft also secures toll-free passage of the Strait of Hormuz for 60 days but does not preclude future fees.

**Why it matters:** The MOU represents the most significant US-Iran diplomatic engagement since the 2015 JCPOA. It is the product of over 100 days of conflict between the US and Iran. Critics argue the "performance-based" structure provides Iran immediate sanctions relief without enforceable nuclear concessions.

**Policy/political implications:**
- Senate Armed Services Chair Roger Wicker (R-MS) publicly criticized the deal, saying he is "concerned."
- Senate Majority Leader John Thune said he anticipates an administration briefing "early next week."
- CNN reports secret auxiliary proposals exist that were not signed by Iran, including unresolved questions about whether Iran can continue enriching uranium.
- The MOU was sent to Congress after it was signed, not before — a process some senators may challenge.
- Israeli and Gulf state reactions remain unstated but will factor heavily.

**What to watch next:**
- Today's formal signing in Bürgenstock
- Congressional briefings next week
- Possible resolutions of disapproval
- IAEA inspection access and implementation timeline
- Oil price movement

### White House Ballroom Funding Controversy

**What happened:** The Office of Management and Budget redirected $352–397 million that was intended in part for Secret Service training and recruitment to accounts labeled "White House Security Measures," multiple outlets report. The funds came from the $1.2 billion Secret Service allocation in the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" passed last year. Congress had separately rejected a $1 billion administration request for the ballroom in a Homeland Security bill. The contractor reportedly estimates the ballroom project will now exceed $600 million, with more than half coming from government entities, not private donors.

**Why it matters:** The transfer raises separation-of-powers concerns, as the Constitution gives Congress exclusive authority over appropriations. Bipartisan concern is emerging — Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) said the transfer "raises the question of whether federal funds are also being used for it."

**What to watch next:**
- Congressional oversight hearings
- Legal challenges to the transfer
- Further disclosures about ballroom funding sources

## 3. Congress: Senate

### FY27 NDAA Advances to Floor

**What happened:** The Senate Armed Services Committee voted 18-9 on June 10 to advance the Fiscal Year 2027 National Defense Authorization Act to the Senate floor. The committee's report was published June 17. The bill authorizes DoD procurement, R&D, personnel end strengths, and DOE national security programs.

**Why it matters:** The NDAA is one of the few major bills that reliably passes each year. The 18-9 vote suggests some bipartisan support but also significant opposition, likely over defense policy and spending disagreements.

**Expected next step:** Floor consideration, likely in coming weeks. Amendment process could be extensive.

### Reconciliation 3.0 Stalls

**What happened:** POLITICO reports Senate Republicans are in no hurry to deliver Trump's next reconciliation bill (dubbed "Reconciliation 3.0"). **Confidence:** Medium — House may advance something, but Senate path is unclear.

**What happened:** Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) and Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) led a group of Democrats in introducing the Protect Our Polls Act, which would require congressional approval before the president can deploy federal law enforcement or troops to polling places.

**Why it matters:** The bill responds to Trump's stated willingness to send federal agents to polling locations. It is unlikely to pass the Republican-controlled Congress but serves as a messaging vehicle and potential campaign issue.

## 8. Courts and Legal or Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court: US v. Hemani (Gun Rights / Marijuana)

**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Parties:** United States v. Ali Danial Hemani
**What happened:** Unanimous decision (June 18) that the government cannot automatically disarm a person who uses marijuana a few times a week under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(3). Justice Gorsuch wrote the majority opinion. The Court held that the government failed to demonstrate the prosecution was consistent with the Second Amendment's historical tradition.
**Legal status:** Final ruling. Narrow — does not affect bans on drug addicts, intoxicated persons, or felons.
**Policy/political impact:** The Court noted 40 states have legalized marijuana to some degree and that the federal government itself has contributed to these changes (rescheduling executive order, DOJ reclassification). Roughly 300 people are charged annually under this law.
**Next:** None — case resolved.

**Source:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/supreme-court-drug-users-gun-law-us-v-hemani/)
