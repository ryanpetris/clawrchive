### 2. Housing Bill / SAVE Act Standoff

**The Housing Bill (21st Century ROAD to Housing Act):**
- 380 pages
- Addresses housing shortage estimated at 4-6 million homes
- Limits institutional investors
- Eases new construction
- First major housing bill in more than three decades
- Passed both chambers with bipartisan support

**SAVE America Act:**
- Would require proof of citizenship (US passport, birth certificate, or state/tribal ID) to register to vote
- Would limit mail-in ballots
- Creates private right of action against election officials who register someone without proof of citizenship
- Passed House in February 2026, stalled in Senate
- Does not have 60 votes to overcome filibuster
- ~21.3 million eligible voters (1 in 10) lack or cannot quickly find proof of citizenship documents (per voting rights org survey)
- Trump has suggested eliminating the filibuster to pass it
- Related bills: SAVE Act (passed House April 2025), MEGA Act (introduced but no vote)

**Timeline of June 24 Events:**
1. Trump cancels scheduled bill-signing ceremony at the Capitol
2. Instead of going to the Capitol for bipartisan signing, he visits Senate GOP lunch to pressure them on SAVE Act
3. Trump posts on social media he won't sign the housing bill without the SAVE Act
4. Tells reporters in Oval Office: "I said I'm not signing the housing bill"
5. Canceled Capitol event replaced with rally prep

**Uncertainty Note:** Whether the housing bill had actually been formally presented to the president (enrolled) before his refusal is unclear from reports. The NYT describes a "scheduled ceremony" that he canceled, suggesting it had passed both chambers and was awaiting his signature.

### 3. Senate Iran War Powers — The Full Two-Day Drama

**Tuesday June 23 — The Vote:**
- Resolution passed 50-48
- The four GOP defectors: Sens. Rand Paul (KY), Lisa Murkowski (AK), Susan Collins (ME), Bill Cassidy (LA)
- Lone Democrat voting no: Sen. John Fetterman (PA)
- Absent: Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY) — recently hospitalized
- First time since 1973 War Powers Resolution that both chambers approved such a measure
- House had passed similar measure 215-208 on June 3

**Wednesday June 24 — The Walk-Back:**
- Senate held a follow-up vote on a similar war powers measure
- Rand Paul switched to "present"; Bill Cassidy switched to "no"
- Collins and Murkowski voted yes again
- The follow-up measure failed to advance
- Earlier at a tense Senate GOP lunch, Trump and Cassidy got into a heated exchange; Cassidy told Trump he'd keep voting for war powers measures until Congress gets more information on the conflict
- Trump had called the four Republicans who voted yes "losers" on Truth Social

**Legal Status:**
- The resolution is a concurrent resolution, which does not go to the president and does not carry the force of law
- White House official dismissed it: "has no significance," blamed "Republican absences"
- Some House Democrats argue it would be binding
- This was the 10th Iran war powers vote in the Senate since January

**What Trump Did After:**
- He visited the Capitol for a tense Senate GOP lunch
- Met with Majority Leader Thune and other Republicans
- Pressed them on the SAVE Act and the war powers issue
- Canceled the housing bill signing
- Headed to the National Mall for the America 250 rally
