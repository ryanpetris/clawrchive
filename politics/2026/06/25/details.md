## 2. White House And President

### America 250 Rally & Great American State Fair

**What happened:** President Trump delivered a keynote speech Wednesday night (June 24, ~8:52 pm ET) on the National Mall, headlining a kickoff rally for the "Great American State Fair" — the centerpiece of the Freedom 250 celebration of America's 250th anniversary. The rally replaced a planned 16-day concert series after musicians dropped out citing politicization. The State Fair runs June 25 through July 10, featuring pavilions from all 50 states and six territories.

**Why it matters:** The event underscores the highly politicized nature of the semiquincentennial, with Trump's campaign-style rally replacing what was intended as a nonpartisan celebration. Organizers went ahead despite artists pulling out, transforming the event into what critics call a Trump campaign rally on public national parkland.

**Details:** Trump declared "This is the beginning of the golden age of America" and "We were a dead country. Now we're the hottest country anywhere in the world." He announced legacy projects including a new White House ballroom, a triumphal arch near Arlington, and the National Garden of American Heroes. Performers included Lee Greenwood, Christopher Macchio, and Alexis Wilkins (FBI Director Kash Patel's girlfriend) who sang the national anthem. The rally ended with Trump dancing to YMCA with the US Marine Band.

- Source: [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/trump-freedom-250-great-american-state-fair-dc-rally-12110870), [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/24/trump-freedom-250-great-american-state-fair), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/06/24/trump-rally-dc-great-american-state-fair--live/90661812007/)

### Housing Bill / SAVE Act Standoff

**What happened:** Trump abruptly canceled a scheduled Wednesday ceremony to sign the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act — the first major housing bill in more than three decades — announcing he would not sign it until Congress passes the SAVE America Act, which would impose proof-of-citizenship and photo-ID requirements for federal elections. The housing bill had passed both chambers with bipartisan support and aimed to address the 4–6 million home shortage by limiting institutional investors and easing construction.

**Why it matters:** The president killed rare bipartisan momentum on one of the most pressing economic issues facing American families. The housing bill had been painstakingly negotiated for over a year. Trump's demand that the SAVE Act pass first — a bill that lacks 60 votes to overcome a filibuster — effectively holds housing legislation hostage to voting restrictions that have no clear path through the Senate.

**What to watch:** Whether Senate Majority Leader Thune can find a path for the SAVE Act, whether Trump will relent, and whether the housing bill's supporters can override or circumvent the president's veto threat.

- Source: [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/24/us/politics/trump-housing-bill-voting-restrictions.html), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/25/nx-s1-5869577/trump-voting-save-america-act), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/06/24/trump-housing-bill-save-america-act-voting-restrictions/)

## 3. Congress: Senate

### Iran War Powers Resolution Passes... Then Gets Walked Back

**What happened:** On Tuesday June 23, the Senate voted 50-48 to adopt a concurrent resolution directing President Trump to remove US armed forces from hostilities with Iran or seek congressional authorization. It was the first time since the 1973 War Powers Resolution that both chambers approved such a measure. On Wednesday June 24, the Senate voted on a similar follow-up measure but rejected it after two GOP senators changed their votes following Trump's pressure.

**Why it matters:** While the resolution is symbolic (concurrent resolutions don't go to the president and have no force of law), the bipartisan vote — four GOP senators crossing the aisle — represented the most significant congressional rebuke of Trump's Iran policy to date. The walk-back the next day showed Trump's continued leverage over Senate Republicans, though Sen. Cassidy's public defiance suggests lingering unease within the GOP caucus.

**Key votes:**
- Tuesday (pass 50-48): GOP defectors — Paul, Murkowski, Collins, Cassidy
- Wednesday (fails to advance): Paul voted present, Cassidy voted no; Collins and Murkowski voted yes again
- Lone Democrat opposing: Sen. John Fetterman (PA)

**What to watch:** The White House $87.6B funding request will test whether Congress' symbolic opposition translates into actual resistance to appropriations.

- Source: [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/23/nx-s1-5868599/senate-iran-war-powers-resolution), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/23/politics/senate-iran-war-powers-vote), [CNN follow-up](https://us.cnn.com/2026/06/24/politics/senate-walks-back-iran-war-powers-vote), [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/23/us/politics/senate-trump-war-powers-iran.html)

## 7. Federal Agencies: News And Reported Developments

### Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)

**What is being reported:** Acting DNI Bill Pulte, installed on Friday June 19 after Tulsi Gabbard's resignation, has fired six intelligence officials — including career officers and political appointees — concentrated in the National Counterterrorism Center (NCTC). He reportedly requested a complete employee roster to determine additional firings and considered transferring counterterrorism and counterintelligence centers to other agencies, but held off for now.

**Confirmed status:** Confirmed by NYT, The Hill, CNN, and other outlets. Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) confirmed the number of firings publicly.

**Why it matters:** Pulte, who has no military or intelligence background and whose authority has been questioned as potentially unlawful, is making significant personnel changes at an agency responsible for coordinating the entire US intelligence community. The firings raise national security concerns, particularly at NCTC, which Rep. Jim Himes said has "an operations floor where people from CIA, ODNI, and FBI are watching terrorist plots unfold."

**What to watch:** Whether Democrats' legislation to block similar acting appointments advances; whether Pulte pursues deeper cuts; and whether the White House nominates a permanent DNI.

- Source: [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/24/us/politics/pulte-cuts-national-intelligence-trump.html), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5936923-pulte-firings-counterterrorism-center/), [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/national-security/2026/06/bill-pulte-unlawful-intelligence-director/687684/)

### Department of Homeland Security / Immigration

**What is being reported:** A D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled 2-1 to uphold DHS's expansion of expedited removal nationwide. Separately, the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in *Blanche v. Lau* that border officers don't need "clear and convincing evidence" to treat green card holders charged with crimes as candidates for admission — effectively making it easier to deny reentry to lawful permanent residents.

**Why it matters:** These rulings cumulatively give the administration sweeping new deportation authorities — both for undocumented immigrants nationwide (expedited removal) and for legal permanent residents with criminal charges.

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court — End of Term

**Status:** The Supreme Court will announce opinions today (Thursday, June 25). Roughly a dozen cases remain, including:
- *Trump v. Barbara* — Birthright citizenship (challenge to Trump's executive order denying citizenship to children born in the US to undocumented immigrants)
- Federal Reserve powers
- Transgender athletes in sports
- Securities law cases

After opinion announcements, the justices will meet in a private conference to discuss pending petitions.

**What to watch:** The birthright citizenship case is the highest-profile remaining decision. Trump has predicted the Court will rule against him, calling the judiciary "rigged."

- Source: [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/24/supreme-court-decisions-donald-trump-00974582), [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/23/us/politics/supreme-court-trump-major-cases.html), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/)

### Supreme Court — Recent Rulings

**Blanche v. Lau (June 23):** The Court ruled 6-3 that federal immigration law does not require border officers to have "clear and convincing evidence" that green card holders have committed a disqualifying crime before denying them reentry. Justice Thomas wrote the majority opinion; Justice Jackson dissented, warning the Court has "handed the Government a massive blank check" to put lawful permanent residents in "immigration limbo."

- Source: [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/court-sides-with-government-in-dispute-over-rights-of-green-card-holders-accused-of-committing-a/)

### DC Circuit Court of Appeals

**Expedited Removal:** The DC Circuit ruled 2-1 that DHS's nationwide expansion of expedited removal — allowing immigration officials to deport undocumented immigrants within hours or days without hearings — is likely lawful. The ruling applies to illegal immigrants without two years of residency.

- Source: [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jun/23/courts-grant-trump-powers-speed-deportations-erase-biden-era-delays/)

## 13. Risk Notes

- **Housing bill status:** It is confirmed that Trump canceled the signing and demanded the SAVE Act, but whether the bill had formally been presented to the president (enrolled) is slightly unclear from available reporting.
- **Pulte's authority:** The legal question of whether Pulte is eligible for the acting DNI role has been raised by multiple outlets but not yet adjudicated.
- **SH funding request:** The exact amount varies by source ($87.6B per AP; ~$80B per Politico). This may reflect different accounting or timing.
- **SCOTUS opinions:** The Court may or may not issue the birthright citizenship decision today. No announcements can be confirmed before they happen.
- **Primary results:** Some races may still be too close to call; the full picture is still emerging.
- **Partisan spin:** The America 250 coverage is heavily polarized; reports should be treated with awareness of source bias.
