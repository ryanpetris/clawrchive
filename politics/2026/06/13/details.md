## 2. White House And President

### Trump Raises Expectations for Iran Deal This Weekend

**What happened:** President Trump announced Thursday that the US and Iran could reach terms this weekend on an agreement to wind down the three-month-old war, saying he plans to send VP JD Vance to the signing. He described the deal as "a little conceptual" but "very strong," claiming it would block Iran from ever developing nuclear weapons. A senior US official put confidence at 80–85%.

**Why it matters:** Trump has claimed a deal is imminent multiple times without delivery. The war remains broadly unpopular, oil prices are elevated, and the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed — approximately 20% of the world's pre-war oil supply passed through it. Republican lawmakers are increasingly anxious about the war's drag on midterm prospects.

**What to watch:** Whether an actual signing occurs this weekend before Trump's 80th birthday UFC event Sunday, and the G7 summit in France next week. Thune says the Senate "ought to have a role" in reviewing any deal. Rep. McCaul (R-TX) believes Iran wants to delay through November.

**Source:** [PBS News — Trump raising expectations on Iran deal](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trump-is-raising-expectations-that-this-time-he-really-will-close-deal-with-iran-to-wind-down-war)

### Conflicting Accounts on Deal Terms

**What happened:** Multiple outlets report conflicting details. White House official says Iran agreed to dismantle nuclear program and destroy enriched uranium stockpile. Iran's Mehr news agency says deal includes release of $24B in frozen Iranian assets. White House says no cash upfront. Pakistan's PM Shehbaz Sharif confirmed "final agreed-upon text" has been reached. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Araghchi says deal will be signed "remotely" with 60 more days of talks after signing.

**Why it matters:** The gap between US and Iranian claims suggests either a framework deal with details to be negotiated (consistent with Araghchi's 60-day remark) or genuine confusion. The White House is trying to take a victory lap while Iran hedges.

**Status:** Officially confirmed — partially. Deal text agreed, but US and Iran descriptions differ significantly.

**Source:** [PBS News](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trump-is-raising-expectations-that-this-time-he-really-will-close-deal-with-iran-to-wind-down-war)

### Pacific Marine Monuments Opened to Commercial Fishing

**What happened:** Trump issued an executive order removing commercial-fishing prohibitions from Papahānaumokuākea, Mariana Trench, and Rose Atoll marine monuments — a combined area exceeding 500,000 square miles. This is the third rollback of Pacific monument protections in 14 months.

**Why it matters:** This brings the total area unprotected to approximately 900,000 sq miles — the largest rollback of marine protections in US history. It comes during Our Ocean Conference week in Mombasa. Earthjustice announced it will file a lawsuit in the District of Hawaii.

**Source:** [Eastern Herald — Trump Opens Pacific Marine Monuments to Commercial Fishing](https://easternherald.com/2026/06/13/trump-pacific-marine-monuments-commercial-fishing-papahanaumokuakea-mariana-2026/)

## 3. Congress: Senate

### FISA Section 702 Expires — Democrats Hold Firm

**What happened:** Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act expired at midnight Friday June 12 — the first lapse since its 2008 enactment. The House had rejected a short-term extension 198–218 on June 11 (199 Democrats + 19 Republicans voting no), then adjourned for a week-long recess. Senate Democrats, led by Schumer, blocked the same extension in the Senate, demanding Trump withdraw Bill Pulte as acting DNI.

**Key players:**
- **Leader Schumer (D-NY):** "We are going to use every tool we have to fight back."
- **Sen. Warner (D-VA):** "I don't deny that this is dangerous. But this didn't have to happen."
- **Sen. Murphy (D-CT):** "We cannot extend these capabilities if the president is making clear that he's going to use them not to protect the nation, but to protect himself politically."
- **Sen. Thune (R-SD):** "How did we get to the point where one party has completely abdicated any responsibility for our nation's security?"

**Why it matters:** Section 702 authorizes warrantless surveillance of foreign nationals abroad — a tool the intelligence community considers essential for counterterrorism. Its expiration creates a legal gray area. The Brennan Center notes existing FISA court certifications remain valid for up to a year, and Sen. Wyden says "ongoing investigations or imminent threats can be investigated." But new surveillance targeting is unauthorized.

**What to watch next:**
- House returns June 23; could take up another extension then.
- If Pulte takes over as acting DNI on June 19 as planned, the standoff escalates.
- Clayton confirmation hearing June 17 at 2pm (Senate Intelligence Committee) — Democrats receptive to Clayton, but Schumer demands Pulte gone first.

## 13. Risk Notes

- **Iran deal:** Trump has claimed imminent deals multiple times. The 80-85% confidence figure from a senior US official is notable but carries no guarantee. Past "about to sign" claims evaporated. The "conceptual" characterization is a yellow flag.
- **FISA:** Whether surveillance can continue under existing FISA court certifications is legally contested. DOJ and privacy groups disagree. The Brennan Center says certifications remain valid for up to one year; opponents disagree. The exact scope of what is and isn't authorized without Section 702 in effect is unsettled.
- **Pulte timeline:** DNI succession is automatic under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act unless Trump takes action. Hard deadline is June 19.
- **Clayton confirmation:** While Democrats are receptive on the record, they may slow-walk to extract concessions. Schumer has not committed to a timeline.
- **Tax bill:** July 4 deadline is Thune's stated goal, not a hard deadline. September is likely more realistic.
- **DOJ anti-weaponization fund:** DOJ may submit the sworn declaration, but if it's narrow or hedged, Brinkema could keep the case open. The separate DC case (Leon) could go differently.
- **House schedule:** Out until June 23. No emergency session expected. This limits legislative options on FISA for 10 days.
- **Pacific monuments:** Scope of Antiquities Act authority for presidents to revoke designations is unsettled by the Supreme Court. Legal risk is real but timelines are long.
- **Senate GOP divisions:** Trump's primary challenges against Cornyn and Cassidy have created real fractures. Cassidy lost his primary; Cornyn lost his. Remaining GOP senators may be less willing to cross Trump — or more willing, since they've seen primary defeat.

## 14. Bottom Line

1. **FISA 702 expired for the first time — that is the biggest institutional development.** How long it stays expired depends on whether Trump pulls Pulte. House is out, Senate can't confirm Clayton fast enough, and both sides are dug in.
2. **Iran deal looks closer than before, but skepticism is warranted.** The conflicting descriptions (White House vs. Iran) suggest a framework, not a final agreement. G7 summit dynamics add urgency.
3. **Trump's relationship with Senate Republicans is at a low point.** Reconciliation 3.0 dead on arrival, corporate rate fight splitting conference, FISA chaos blamed on Trump's Pulte appointment. Midterm anxiety among Republicans is making them less willing to absorb political risk.
4. **The anti-weaponization fund is a growing legal and political liability.** Bipartisan opposition, a preliminary injunction, and Blanche confirmation complications make this a developing story worth tracking.
5. **The NDAA passed committee on an unusually partisan vote.** Normally a bipartisan must-pass, the 18-9 split (with Kaine and Duckworth voting no over the Iran war) signals broader unease with unauthorized military action.
