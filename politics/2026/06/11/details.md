## 2. White House And President

### Trump Demands Short-Term FISA Extension, Refuses to Drop Pulte
- **What happened:** After meeting Wednesday with Speaker Johnson, Trump called on Congress to pass a short-term extension of FISA Section 702 through July 2 to "provide time for the selection and confirmation" of a permanent DNI. He reiterated that Bill Pulte will become acting DNI on June 19 and is tasked with downsizing ODNI.
- **Why it matters:** Democrats are demanding Pulte's removal before supporting any FISA extension. Trump's refusal to budge makes a lapse all but certain, which intelligence officials warn would be "calamitous" for national security.
- **Policy implications:** A FISA lapse would be the first since Section 702's creation in 2008, halting warrantless foreign intelligence collection.
- **What to watch:** The House vote Thursday morning; whether Trump names a permanent nominee (Pete Hoekstra is a potential candidate).
- **Source:** Washington Examiner; The Straits Times / Reuters; CBS News

### US Military Strikes Iran for Second Consecutive Night
- **What happened:** CENTCOM confirmed additional "self-defense strikes" against multiple targets in Iran beginning around 5:15 p.m. ET Wednesday. Trump told Fox News he spoke directly with Iranian officials who asked him to stop strikes, which he said would happen "shortly." He warned strikes would continue if no MOU is reached.
- **Why it matters:** The strikes represent a significant escalation after a fragile ceasefire and ongoing diplomatic efforts through Qatari mediators in Tehran.
- **Policy implications:** The conflict, now past 100 days, is straining US military readiness and driving up energy costs. Public polling shows Trump's approval sinking amid high gasoline prices.
- **What to watch:** Whether another night of strikes occurs; whether Qatar-mediated talks yield progress.
- **Source:** The Times of Israel; Newsweek; The Online Citizen

### Trump Endorses Third Reconciliation Bill ($350B) With Save America Act
- **What happened:** Trump on Truth Social called on Republicans to "IMMEDIATELY advance and pass" a $350 billion reconciliation bill (Recon 3.0) that includes the Save America Act. He said it is "the ONLY path to the full $1.5 TRILLION DOLLAR Military Budget."
- **Why it matters:** Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins (R-Maine) called the approach a "terrible risk" that could create funding instability. Majority Leader Thune noted the Save America Act failed to get 50 votes last week. The bill also requires eliminating the legislative filibuster to pass voting-related provisions.
- **Policy implications:** Would fund Golden Dome, F-47, B-21 production and replenish weapons stockpiles depleted in the Iran war.
- **What to watch:** Whether Senate Republican leadership moves forward despite Collins and McConnell's skepticism.
- **Source:** The Hill

### Trump Signs Secure America Act
- **What happened:** The White House live page shows Trump signed the $70 billion immigration enforcement bill passed by the House Tuesday (214-212) and Senate last week (52-47).
- **Why it matters:** The bill frontloads $38 billion to ICE, $26 billion to CBP, and $5 billion for DHS emergencies through September 2029, funding the administration's deportation agenda.
- **Source:** White House Live; Koranmanado / AP

## 3. Congress: Senate

### Senate GOP Support for Iran War Powers Resolution Growing
- **What happened:** Four Republican senators (Paul, Collins, Murkowski, Cassidy) have voted to discharge the war powers resolution from the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. Democrats believe they are close to finding a fifth GOP vote. Sen. Tim Kaine (D-Va.): "We think we can have a narrow win."
- **Why it matters:** A fifth Republican defector would give Democrats a pathway to pass the resolution in the Senate. Possible swing votes: Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) and John Curtis (R-Utah). The war passed 100 days this week, well beyond the 60-day War Powers Act clock.
- **Expected next step:** Senate parliamentarian is determining whether the House-passed concurrent resolution (H.Con.Res. 86) is "privileged." A vote could come next week.
- **Source:** The Hill; Kaine Senate press release; PolitiFact

### Senate Fails to Advance FISA Extension (47-52)
- **What happened:** Last week, the Senate defeated a procedural motion to proceed with a FISA extension by a 47-52 vote. Seven Republicans crossed the aisle to join Democrats in opposing it.
- **Why it matters:** The vote demonstrated bipartisan opposition to the current extension, with Republicans demanding warrant requirements and Democrats opposing Pulte.
- **Source:** ABC News; CryptoBriefing

### Key Senate Statements
- **Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR), Senate Intelligence Chair:** Warned the administration that the spy tool is likely to lapse.
- **Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA), Intelligence Vice Chair:** Called Pulte's appointment "a live hand grenade."
- **Sen. John Thune (R-SD), Majority Leader:** Said the Save America Act "didn't even get 50 votes last week."
- **Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME), Appropriations Chair:** Called third reconciliation bill a "terrible risk."

## 4. Congress: House Of Representatives

### House to Vote on Short-Term FISA Extension (Expected to Fail)
- **What happened:** Speaker Johnson announced the House will vote Thursday morning on a short-term extension of FISA Section 702 to July 2. The measure is being brought under suspension of the rules, requiring a two-thirds majority. Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) is a "hard no." Conservative hardliners want warrant requirements attached.
- **Why it matters:** If the measure fails, the program will likely sunset at midnight Friday. The House is scheduled to leave for a one-week recess after voting Thursday, meaning the program would remain dark until at least June 23.
- **Expected next step:** Measure almost certain to fail in the House. Senate could still attempt its own extension but needs 60 votes.
- **Source:** Washington Examiner; CBS News; NBC News

### House Passed War Powers Resolution (June 3)
- **What happened:** The House voted 215-208 to pass H.Con.Res. 86, directing Trump to end hostilities with Iran. Four Republicans (Massie, Fitzpatrick, Barrett, Davidson) broke ranks.
- **Why it matters:** While symbolic and non-binding, it is the clearest congressional rebuke yet of the administration's war policy. Because it is a concurrent resolution, it cannot be vetoed — but it also carries no force of law.
- **Source:** Natural News; TIME; NPR

### House Passed Secure America Act (June 9)
- **What happened:** House approved $70 billion immigration enforcement bill 214-212, party-line. Sent to Trump for signature.
- **Source:** Koranmanado / AP

### Impeachment Resolution Tabled
- **What happened:** Rep. Al Green (D-TX) introduced articles of impeachment against Trump for the Iran war. The House tabled the measure 344-79.
- **Source:** Natural News

## 14. Bottom Line

1. **FISA lapse is the biggest near-term institutional event.** Congress appears unable to prevent the first-ever lapse of Section 702 authority, driven entirely by the Pulte standoff. This will dominate Thursday.
2. **The Iran conflict continues to escalate with no end in sight.** Strikes are intensifying, not de-escalating. The Strait of Hormuz closure threat keeps global energy markets on edge and feeds the inflation numbers.
3. **Inflation is accelerating and war-driven.** PPI at 6.5% is the highest since November 2022, with energy costs as the primary driver. This gives the Fed a difficult choice between rate hikes and election-year politics.
4. **Congress is increasingly pushing back on the administration — but mostly symbolically.** The House war powers resolution passed, the Senate may follow, impeachment was tabled, and FISA is about to lapse. None of these are likely to change the administration's course in the near term.
5. **The GOP majority is fragile and fracturing.** House margins are razor-thin, Senate GOP support for the Iran war is eroding, and midterm election concerns are growing. Florida redistricting may help, but the political headwinds from the war and inflation are significant.
