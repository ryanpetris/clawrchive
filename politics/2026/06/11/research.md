## FISA Section 702 / Pulte Standoff — Deeper Analysis

### The Core Dispute
- Trump appointed Bill Pulte (current FHFA director) as acting DNI on an accelerated timeline (June 19 start instead of later).
- Democrats refuse to support any FISA extension — short-term or long-term — while Pulte is in the role.
- Seven Senate Republicans also voted against renewal last week, but their objection is privacy/warrant requirements, not Pulte.
- The House vote today (Thursday) is under suspension of the rules requiring 2/3 majority — essentially guaranteed to fail given Democratic opposition and conservative defections.

### Possible Outcomes
1. **FISA lapses at midnight Friday** — most likely scenario based on current vote math. House leaves for recess Thursday after voting. Program would stay dark until at least June 23.
2. **11th-hour deal** — Trump nominates a permanent DNI (Pete Hoekstra mentioned as potential pick) and Democrats relent on short extension. Unlikely given Trump's public doubling-down.
3. **Senate acts after House failure** — Senate could still attempt its own extension. But they already failed a procedural vote 47-52 last week and need 60 votes.

### What FISA Lapse Would Mean
- Section 702 allows warrantless collection of foreign communications, including when foreigners communicate with Americans.
- FISC has recertified Section 702 procedures through 2027, but underlying statutory authority would still lapse.
- Intelligence community warns of "calamitous" national security consequences.
- This would be the first lapse in the program's history since its 2008 creation.

### Key Actors
- **Trump:** Holding firm on Pulte, wants short extension to buy time for permanent nominee.
- **Speaker Johnson:** Between a rock and a hard place — needs Democratic votes he can't get and hardline Republicans demanding warrant requirements.
- **Senate Leader Thune:** Wants permanent nominee named. Told reporters administration "weighing seriously making a long-term pick."
- **Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR, Intel Chair):** Warned administration the spy tool is likely to lapse.
- **Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA, Intel Vice Chair):** Called Pulte appointment "a live hand grenade."
- **Rep. Jim Himes (D-CT, House Intel ranking):** Says Democratic support down to "single digits at best."
- **Pete Hoekstra:** Potential permanent nominee, ambassador to Canada, former House Intel chair.

## PPI / Inflation — Deeper Analysis

### Key Numbers
- Headline PPI: +1.1% month-over-month (vs 0.7% expected), +6.5% YoY (vs 6.4% expected)
- Highest 12-month rise since November 2022 (7.4%)
- Core PPI (less food, energy, trade services): +0.8% monthly (largest since March 2022), +5.1% YoY
- Final demand goods: +2.8% (nearly 80% of overall advance)
- Final demand services: +0.3%
- Energy prices: gasoline +23.4%, diesel fuel +22.5%, home heating oil +16.3%
- CPI already at 4.2% YoY (May), highest since April 2023

### Fed Implications
- Markets see ~70% probability of rate hike by end of 2026 (CEM FedWatch).
- Core PPI softer than expected (4.9% vs 5.4% forecast) provides some cover for Fed to wait.
- But headline PPI acceleration, driven by energy, is concerning.
- Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruptions are feeding energy inflation.

### Broader Context
- This is the fourth consecutive monthly acceleration in producer prices.
- Intermediate demand (stage 4) prices up 1.1% monthly, 6.5% annually.
- Transportation of freight and mail among categories seeing significant increases.
- War-related supply chain disruptions and energy costs likely to keep upward pressure.
