## 2. White House And President

### US-Iran "Stand Down" Agreement

**What happened:** After three days of retaliatory strikes — beginning with an Iranian drone attack on a Singapore-flagged cargo ship on Thursday, followed by US strikes on Friday and Saturday hitting 10 Iranian military targets, and Iranian strikes on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain on Saturday — the US confirmed it has agreed with Iran to "stand down" for now. A US official told CBS News vessels can now move freely through the Strait of Hormuz. Trump announced on Truth Social that Iran "requested a meeting" that "will take place tomorrow in Doha." However, Deputy Iranian Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi denied plans for technical talks this week.

**Why it matters:** The June 17 MoU ceasefire is fraying badly less than two weeks after it was signed. The stand-down prevents immediate escalation but neither side has confirmed the terms publicly, and Iran's denial of scheduled talks undercuts the appearance of de-escalation. The Strait of Hormuz — carrying about one-fifth of global oil and gas — had been effectively closed by Iran since late February.

**Policy or political implications:** Trump faces growing congressional pressure (the war powers resolution passed both chambers); the stand-down gives him breathing room to keep negotiating, but any new incident could trigger a rapid escalation. Trump warned Saturday that if the US must "militarily complete the job," Iran "will no longer exist."

**What to watch next:** Tuesday's reported Doha talks; whether the US-Iran "hotline" (agreed in Switzerland last week but still not operational) is established; whether Iranian attacks on commercial shipping resume; and whether the Lebanon-Israel ceasefire framework signed Friday holds.

### Housing Bill / SAVE Act Standoff

**What happened:** Trump canceled the planned June 24 signing ceremony for the bipartisan 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644), which passed the Senate 85-5 and the House with nearly 400 votes, demanding that Congress first pass the SAVE America Act — legislation requiring voter ID and proof of citizenship for registration.

**Why it matters:** The move blindsided Republican leaders, including Speaker Johnson and Majority Leader Thune, who were actively promoting the housing bill. Trump used a closed-door Senate GOP lunch to press for eliminating the filibuster to pass SAVE, but Thune said Republicans do not have the votes. The incident deepened White House-Senate GOP tensions.

**Policy implications:** Housing affordability is a top voter concern; blocking popular bipartisan legislation risks political blowback. Trump's gambit seems designed to force Senate Republicans to confront the filibuster rule, but there is no clear path to 60 votes for SAVE or to eliminating the filibuster with the current 53-47 GOP majority.

**What to watch next:** Whether Johnson can pass SAVE through reconciliation (he's floated the idea); whether Trump eventually signs the housing bill without SAVE; whether the standoff affects midterm messaging on affordability.

**Source:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-cancels-plan-sign-major-housing-bill-fights-congress-act-rcna351558), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/trump-cancels-housing-bill-signing-save-america-act.html), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-says-he-wont-sign-major-housing-bill-until-congress-passes-save-act)

## 3. Congress: Senate

### War Powers Resolution (H.Con.Res. 86) — Passed June 23

**What happened:** The Senate voted 50-48 to pass a concurrent resolution directing the President to remove US armed forces from hostilities with Iran under section 5(c) of the War Powers Resolution. Four Republicans — Cassidy (LA), Collins (ME), Murkowski (AK), and Paul (KY) — voted with Democrats. The resolution does not require the president's signature and is not legally binding.

**Why it matters:** This is the first war powers resolution against Iran to pass both chambers. While symbolic, it reflects deep bipartisan unease with the three-month conflict. Trump called the four GOP defectors "Republican Losers" and said the vote made his job "more difficult."

**Expected next step:** The resolution is a formal congressional statement; Trump has indicated he will ignore it. Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) has threatened further action, including court action to compel compliance.

**Source:** [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/23/nx-s1-5868599/senate-iran-war-powers-resolution), [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-concurrent-resolution/86/all-actions)

### Housing Bill Passage (H.R. 6644) — June 22

Senators Armstrong, Cruz, Johnson, Lee, Paul, Scott (FL), Tuberville, and Young voted against proceeding. ## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court — Final Week of Term

**What's pending (12 decisions remaining):**
- **Trump v. Barbara (Birthright Citizenship):** Whether Trump can end birthright citizenship by executive order — widely viewed as a legal long shot; Trump attended oral arguments in April, the first sitting president to do so.
- **Trump v. Slaughter (Independent Agency Removal):** Whether Trump can fire Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook and FTC members at will; could reshape independent agency structure.
- **TPS Termination Case:** Already decided — Trump administration scored a win on terminating Temporary Protected Status for certain countries.
- **Transgender Athlete Case:** Pending decision on whether schools can ban transgender athletes from women's sports.

**Why it matters:** The birthright citizenship case is one of the term's most consequential — it tests whether 150+ years of 14th Amendment interpretation can be undone by executive order. The removal-power cases could dramatically reshape the administrative state and the independence of the Federal Reserve, FTC, and other agencies.

**What to watch next:** Opinion announcements expected any day this week (the term traditionally ends by early July).

**Source:** [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/29/us/politics/supreme-court-final-decisions.html), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/06/28/supreme-courts-trump-rulings-birthright-citizenship/90730435007/), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/major-decisions-ahead/)
