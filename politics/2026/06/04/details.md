## 4. Congress: House of Representatives

### Iran War Powers Resolution Passes (215-208)
- **What happened:** The House approved a war powers resolution (concurrent resolution) to halt U.S. military action against Iran. Four Republicans crossed party lines to support it.
- **GOP defectors:** Reps. Thomas Massie (KY), Tom Barrett (MI), Warren Davidson (OH), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA).
- **Background:** This is the fourth time the House has tried. Speaker Johnson abruptly shut down floor action two weeks ago when the resolution was on the verge of approval. In April, only one Republican (Massie) voted for it.
- **Status:** The resolution is a concurrent resolution — it does not require the president's signature and does not have the force of law. A House Democratic aide argues it would be binding.
- **Next steps:** Goes to the Senate, which advanced its own war powers resolution last month but has not taken a final vote.
- **White House response:** Trump posted on Truth Social calling the four GOP defectors "GRANDSTANDERS" and the resolution "meaningless."
- **Legality:** The War Powers Act gives the White House 60 days to seek congressional approval. Administration says the ceasefire means hostilities have ceased. Pentagon, State, and USAID IGs launched a joint review — significant because it indicates inspectors general believe the 60-day clock has expired.
- **Source:** [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c0r2kxrvlgpo), [The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/donald-trump-hit-with-huge-blow-as-republicans-vote-against-his-war-in-iran/), [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/house-votes-limit-trump-iran-214547524.html)

## 8. Courts and Legal or Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court: Alabama Congressional Map (Allen v. Milligan)
- **What happened:** In a 4-page unsigned order (6-3), the Supreme Court cleared Alabama to use its 2023 congressional map for the 2026 elections, reversing a lower court that found the map intentionally discriminated against Black voters.
- **Key ruling:** The Court said the lower court "failed to follow our instruction in *Callais*" and that "the mere fact that voters of different races vote for different parties is not relevant to proving racially polarized voting patterns." The Court also said the district court "interposed itself into Alabama's ongoing efforts."
- **Dissenting (Sotomayor, joined by Kagan, Jackson):** "The majority chooses the second path and disregards both democratic values and the rule of law."
- **Impact:** Likely eliminates the second majority-Black district currently held by Rep. Shomari Figures (D-AL-02), giving Republicans a pickup opportunity. A special primary set for August 11 under the 2023 map.
- **Broader significance:** The decision dramatically expands the April *Louisiana v. Callais* ruling, making it much harder to challenge maps as racially discriminatory — even under the Fourteenth Amendment.
- **Next:** Supreme Court may announce opinions Thursday at 10 a.m. EDT. Justices meet in private conference Thursday; orders expected Monday June 8.
- **Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/supreme-court-permits-alabama-to-use-congressional-map-struck-by-lower-court-as-racially-discrim/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/supreme-court-lets-alabama-use-republican-friendly-us-house-map)
