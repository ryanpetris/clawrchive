# Research Notes - Daily Political Outlook - June 26, 2026

## Key Themes & Cross-Cutting Analysis

### Theme 1: The SAVE Act Hostage Crisis
Trump's decision to cancel the housing bill signing represents a fundamental shift in his legislative strategy. Previously, the White House had expressed support for the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act. Press Secretary Leavitt called it "one of the most significant pieces of housing affordability legislation in American history" just 24 hours before Trump called it "of minor importance."

**Strategic Analysis:**
- Trump appears to be using the housing bill as leverage to force Senate Republicans to either pass the SAVE Act or eliminate the filibuster.
- The housing bill passed 358-32 in the House and 85-5 in the Senate — overwhelming veto-proof margins.
- Under Article I, Section 7, Trump has 10 days (excluding Sundays) to sign or veto. With Congress beginning recess today (Friday June 26) but holding pro forma sessions, a pocket veto is unlikely. The bill would become law without his signature after 10 days.
- Key question: Will Trump actually veto a bill that passed with veto-proof majorities, forcing Congress to override him? Or is this a negotiating tactic that will resolve before the deadline?
- The constitutional deadline would be approximately July 8-9, 2026 (10 days excluding Sundays from when the bill was presented).

**House Paralysis:**
- Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL) is leading a blockade of House floor proceedings until the SAVE Act passes. She claims she has the votes to sustain the blockade.
- Speaker Johnson met with Trump Thursday (June 25) to resolve this.
- The House has already had to cancel "rule" votes, effectively freezing floor action.
- The blockade could last through the July 4 recess period.

### Theme 2: Iran War — Whiplash in Congress
The Senate's sequence on Iran war powers is remarkable:

**Timeline:**
1. **Tuesday June 23**: Senate passes H.Con.Res.86 (War Powers resolution) 50-48, with 4 Republicans joining Democrats. McConnell (KY) and McCormick (PA) absent.
2. **Wednesday June 24**: Trump meets with Senate GOP for lunch — described as "explosive" with a "shouting match" between Trump and Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA).
3. **Wednesday evening**: Cassidy visits White House for personal briefing from VP Vance and envoy Witkoff.
4. **Wednesday late night**: Senate votes 47-50-1 to reject a similar war powers resolution. Cassidy flips from Yea to Nay, Paul flips from Yea to Present.
5. **Thursday June 25**: Senate leaves for 2-week recess.

**Analysis of Cassidy flip:**
- Cassidy's flip after the White House briefing suggests the administration provided classified information or made a compelling argument about impact on negotiations.
- However, the sequence (berate, then brief, then flip) creates the appearance of political pressure rather than genuine reconsideration.
- This exposes Cassidy to potential primary challenge vulnerability.

**Unresolved questions:**
- The first war powers resolution (H.Con.Res.86) that passed both chambers is still technically out there — it's a concurrent resolution that doesn't require the president's signature. Its legal force is disputed. The administration has called it "unconstitutional."
- Trump's negotiating position may be strengthened by defeating the second resolution, but the first one passing still signals congressional opposition.
- The $87.6B supplemental faces an uphill battle given the war's unpopularity (78% of Americans want it to end now per CBS poll).

### Theme 3: Supreme Court End-of-Term Blitz
The Supreme Court issued 4 major rulings on June 25, continuing an end-of-term pattern of 6-3 decisions along ideological lines.

**Pattern recognition:**
- Three of four rulings were 6-3 (conservative majority). Roundup case was 7-2 with unusual cross-ideological alignment.
- All four rulings favored conservative/administration positions.
- The court appears to be deferring broadly to executive authority on immigration, limiting corporate liability on regulatory preemption, and expanding gun rights.
- Remaining cases to watch: birthright citizenship case is still pending.

**TPS ruling implications:**
- The ruling clears the way for ending TPS for 13 countries total, not just Haiti and Syria.
- The administration previously ended TPS for Venezuela (via emergency rulings in May and October 2025).
- As many as 350,000 Haitians and 6,000 Syrians could lose legal status immediately.
- Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine (R) publicly opposed the ruling, citing the 10,000+ Haitians in Springfield, Ohio who would become deportable.
- The administration has also quietly extended TPS for ~11,000 Lebanese citizens by missing a statutory deadline — a notable exception.

**Roundup decision implications:**
- Bayer has set aside $16B for settlements and proposed a $7.25B class-action settlement.
- The ruling shields Bayer from failure-to-warn claims but may not bar design defect claims.
- ~200,000 claims total filed against Bayer over Roundup.
- The political fallout with MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) movement is significant. Allies of RFK Jr. held a protest outside SCOTUS and were furious at both the ruling and the administration's support for Monsanto's position.
- Trump's executive order on regenerative agriculture (issued the same evening) appears timed to try to mollify MAHA allies, but critics called it weak.
