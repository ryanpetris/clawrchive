## 2. White House And President

### Trump Cancels Housing Bill Signing

**What happened:** President Trump abruptly canceled the planned noon signing ceremony for the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R.6644) on Wednesday, June 24, announcing on Truth Social that he would not sign "until such time as we pass the desperately needed SAVE AMERICA ACT." He later called the housing bill "of minor importance." [Source](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-signs-housing-bill-capitol/)

**Why it matters:** The housing bill is landmark bipartisan legislation — incorporating provisions from 60+ bills — that would bar large institutional investors from buying single-family homes and loosen building regulations to expand supply. It passed the House 358-32 and the Senate 85-5. Trump's refusal to sign (or veto) could allow it to become law without his signature after 10 days (excluding Sundays) if Congress remains in session.

**What to watch:** Trump hasn't said whether he will veto. An override would likely succeed given veto-proof margins. The constitutional clock is running. Congress begins recess today but plans pro forma sessions, preventing a pocket veto. The bill could become law without Trump's signature by ~July 8-9.

**Source:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-cancels-plan-sign-major-housing-bill-fights-congress-act-rcna351558), [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/housing-costs-congress-affordable-trump-85db7cc9fead2730dda9cfa7706f8189), [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/06/trump-conditions-housing-bill-signing-on-contested-proof-of-citizenship-voting-law/)

### Trump Clashes with Senate Republicans Over Iran

**What happened:** At a closed-door Senate GOP lunch Wednesday, Trump engaged in a "shouting match" with Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA) over the Iran war. Trump was furious about the Senate's 50-48 passage of a war powers resolution the day before. He called the four GOP supporters "losers" on Truth Social. [Source](https://apnews.com/article/bill-cassidy-trump-senate-republican-lunch-703c5fa45438ecae75d53062eea3aa87)

**Cassidy flip:** Cassidy was invited to the White House for a personal briefing from VP Vance and envoy Witkoff, then returned to vote against the second war powers resolution.

**Why it matters:** The confrontation exposed deep GOP divisions over the Iran war and Trump's leadership style. The whipsaw vote sequence (first passing, then rejecting an almost identical resolution) highlighted how personal presidential pressure can shift votes — but at the cost of institutional credibility.

| Vote | Date | Tally | Notable |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| H.Con.Res.86 passage | June 23 | 50-48 | 4 R's joined Dems (Collins, Murkowski, Paul, Cassidy); McConnell absent; McCormick absent |
| Second war powers resolution | June 24 (late) | 47-50-1 | Cassidy flipped to Nay; Paul voted Present; Collins and Murkowski held |

After Trump's explosive lunch meeting, Cassidy received a White House briefing and flipped. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) voted Present, saying it gave Trump "more space and leverage to negotiate."

**Why it matters:** Both resolutions are largely symbolic — concurrent resolutions don't require the president's signature and have no force of law. ## 7. Federal Agencies: News And Reported Developments

### Department of Homeland Security (TPS)

**Agency:** DHS  
**What is being reported:** The Supreme Court has cleared DHS to end Temporary Protected Status for Haitian (~350,000) and Syrian (~6,000) nationals. The administration has already moved to end TPS for all 13 countries that have come up for review.  
**Confirmation:** Confirmed — Supreme Court ruling on June 25.  
**Why it matters:** Hundreds of thousands of people who have been living and working legally in the U.S. for years — in some cases decades — could now face deportation to countries the State Department designates too dangerous for American travel.  
**What to watch:** The pace of termination implementation; legal challenges on equal protection grounds (though Alito's opinion rejected that argument); impact on states like Ohio where Gov. DeWine has opposed the policy.  
**Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/supreme-court-allows-trump-administration-to-end-removal-protections-for-syrian-and-haitian-nati/)

### Environmental Protection Agency / DOJ (Roundup)

**Agency:** EPA  
**What is being reported:** Supreme Court ruled 7-2 that federal pesticide labeling law (FIFRA) preempts state-law failure-to-warn claims against Roundup manufacturer Bayer/Monsanto.  
**Confirmation:** Confirmed.  
**Why it matters:** Blocks thousands of pending lawsuits and potentially future claims. MAHA movement considers this a betrayal by the Trump administration. EPA has not determined glyphosate causes cancer; WHO's IARC classified it as "probably carcinogenic" in 2015.  
**What to watch:** Bayer's proposed $7.25B class-action settlement; design defect claims (still potentially viable); MAHA political fallout; state-level legislative responses.  
**Source:** [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/06/25/supreme-court-cancer-case-weedkiller-glyphosate/90157628007/)

## 13. Risk Notes

- **Schumer's midterm observer program**: Described as "non-interfering" but could become a flashpoint if clashes with Trump-aligned election officials occur.
- **Pocket veto risk**: The housing bill could face a pocket veto if Congress formally adjourns. Pro forma sessions prevent this, but if leadership changes the schedule, the bill could be in jeopardy.
- **War powers enforcement**: The administration's position that the War Powers Resolution is unconstitutional could lead to a constitutional confrontation if Congress attempts to enforce H.Con.Res.86 through legal action.
- **SCOTUS birthright citizenship**: Still pending. A ruling ending birthright citizenship would be the most consequential immigration decision in decades and could dominate the news cycle.
- **$87.6B supplemental contents**: The $12.1B in classified programs and $1.2B in unspecified "Administration priorities" could face scrutiny for potential waste or lack of oversight.
- **FISA expiration**: The lapse of key surveillance authorities remains unresolved and could become a national security flashpoint.
- **Trump health transparency**: Multiple reporters have noted bruising on Trump's hands and apparent dozing during meetings. The White House has not released detailed medical information.
- **Midterm polling leads**: Democratic polling leads have narrowed from 8 points to ~5 points in recent weeks. If the trend continues, GOP could retain the House despite current headwinds.
- **MAHA political impact**: The MAHA movement is a relatively new political force. Its actual electoral weight is unknown and could be overstated by media coverage.

## 14. Bottom Line

1. **Trump's housing bill hostage move is high-risk.** The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act will almost certainly become law — with or without his signature — due to veto-proof margins and pro forma sessions preventing a pocket veto. The standoff has fractured GOP messaging on affordability, the #1 voter issue, heading into the midterms.

2. **The Supreme Court delivered Trump two major immigration wins and a corporate liability shield.** The TPS and border metering rulings enable the administration's deportation agenda. The Roundup ruling protects Bayer from thousands of lawsuits but has angered the MAHA movement.

3. **The Iran war whipsaw shows Trump can still bend his caucus to his will — but the underlying political problem remains.** 78% of Americans want the war ended. The $87.6B supplemental faces a tough path.

4. **Democrats enter the midterm stretch with structural advantages but narrowing polling leads.** The generic ballot has tightened from D+8 to D+5 in recent weeks. Trump's approval at 39-42% and growing health scrutiny are drags on the GOP, but redistricting gives Republicans a 10-seat cushion in the House.

5. **The next 2 weeks (Senate recess, possible House deadlock) are unlikely to produce major legislative action.** Watch for the housing bill deadline (~July 8-9), the birthright citizenship SCOTUS ruling, and Iran negotiation updates.
