## 3. Congress: Senate

### FISA 702 — Three Days to Expiration, No Path Forward
- **What happened:** The Senate blocked the motion to proceed on extending Section 702 of FISA (47-52) early Friday. With the June 12 deadline approaching, Senate Republicans are pressing Trump to nominate a permanent DNI to replace acting DNI Bill Pulte.
- **Monday developments:**
  - Thune (R-SD): "We can't pass this on the floor without Democrats" and "The administration, probably, at some point is going to have to come up with a nominee — a permanent nominee."
  - Grassley (R-IA): "The sooner the president gets somebody nominated, the sooner we'll be able to get 702 through."
  - Johnson (R-LA) said he's determined to pass FISA this week but offered no specific plan.
  - House Freedom Caucus Chair Andy Harris (R-MD) predicted "another short-term extension."
  - Warner (D-VA): Democrats won't support an extension while Pulte is acting DNI.
  - Wyden (D-OR): "Pulte has no business overseeing a warrantless spying program for Donald Trump."
- **Thune on ODNI downsizing:** Thune confirmed Monday that Trump administration officials are using the stalemate to push for scaling back or eliminating the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.
- **Cotton-Grassley letter:** Senators asked Secretary Rubio to prepare contingency plans, including a potential executive order to remedy a collection gap.
- **FISA Court certification:** The intelligence court approved a year-long certification in March, meaning the surveillance program could technically continue into 2027 even without congressional approval, per Sen. Durbin (D-IL).

### Todd Blanche AG Nomination Formally Submitted
- **What happened:** Trump formally nominated Todd Blanche for permanent attorney general Monday. Blanche has served as acting AG since Pam Bondi's firing in April.
- **Senate dynamics:**
  - Cassidy (R-LA) summed up the central question: "I need to know if Todd Blanche is an attorney general who used to be the president's personal attorney, or is he the president's personal attorney who just happens to be attorney general."

- **Why it matters:** Democrats are building the case that the administration is conducting an unauthorized war. The letter aims to pressure the administration into releasing legal justification and to build public support for the war powers resolution.

Key targets include DOJ and FBI leadership, the anti-weaponization fund architects, and DHS contract practices.
