## Iran-Israel Escalation — Detailed Timeline

### Pre-Sunday Context
- The April 8 ceasefire between US/Israel and Iran was always fragile. Iran repeatedly said any deal with Washington must include a halt to Israel's campaign in Lebanon against Hezbollah.
- Israel launched a ground invasion of Lebanon in March 2026. Hezbollah rejected the US-brokered Lebanon ceasefire.
- Israel has carried out 3,491 air strikes, 407 controlled demolitions, and 6 "razing" operations in Lebanon since April 17 (per PM Nawaf Salam).

### Sunday-Monday Exchange
1. Hezbollah fired missiles toward Israel, accusing Israel of repeatedly violating the Lebanon ceasefire.
2. Israel struck Beirut's southern suburbs (Dahiyeh) Sunday — killing at least 2 people.
3. Iran retaliated Sunday evening, launching ~30 ballistic missiles toward Israel. This was Iran's first large direct strike on Israel since the April ceasefire.
4. Trump called Netanyahu, urged restraint. He posted on Truth Social for both sides to stop.
5. Israel struck back anyway early Monday — hitting military sites in Tehran, Isfahan, Tabriz, and a petrochemical plant at Mahshahr.
6. Trump posted again: "Both sides, Israel and Iran, are looking to do an immediate CEASEFIRE!"
7. Iran announced halt to operations Monday afternoon, describing it as a "completed operation" not a ceasefire. Iran claimed its "painful response" had been delivered.
8. Netanyahu announced Israel was holding fire "at the moment" but the fight against Hezbollah in Lebanon was "not finished."

### Analysis Points
- RFE/RL analysis describes Iran's posture as a "credibility operation" — Iran had publicly committed to responding if Israel struck Beirut, so it had little choice but to follow through.
- Strait of Banks note: Iran's deterrent posture may be nominally intact, but the situation is highly volatile with both sides describing the pause as conditional.
- Israel's defiance of Trump's request not to retaliate demonstrates Netanyahu's willingness to act independently. The Straits Times interprets this as Israel trying to secure a seat at peace talks from which Trump has excluded it.
- Trump's FT interview quote ("I call the shots. I call all the shots. He doesn't call the shots.") contrasts with Israel striking hours later.

## FISA 702 — Detailed Vote Breakdown and Path Analysis

### The Vote (Friday June 5)
- Motion to proceed failed 47-52 (needed 60 effectively due to filibuster rules)
- 7 Republicans voted no: Hawley (MO), Lee (UT), Paul (KY), Schmitt (MO), Scott (FL), Kennedy (LA), Tuberville (AL)
- 1 Democrat voted yes: Fetterman (PA)
- All other Democrats voted no

### Why Democrats Oppose
- Primary reason: Bill Pulte as acting DNI. Intelligence Committee Vice Chair Warner says Democrats "can't abide" granting enhanced surveillance powers when Pulte could use intelligence against political enemies.
- Some Democrats also want warrant requirements for querying Americans' data.

### Why 7 GOP Privacy Hawks Oppose
- The three-year reauthorization bill does not include a warrant requirement for searching Americans' communications data.
- Hawley, Lee, Paul, Schmitt, Scott, Kennedy, Tuberville have all long opposed warrantless surveillance.

### Path Analysis (as of Tuesday June 9)
- **Option A: Trump nominates permanent DNI → Cotton-Warner bipartisan deal revived.** This is what Thune, Grassley, and others are publicly pressing for. Would likely unlock enough Democratic votes. But the House has a separate CBDC moratorium provision that complicates passage.
- **Option B: Short-term extension.** House Freedom Caucus opposes a "clean" short extension absent reforms. Democrats also oppose extension as long as Pulte remains. Thune said another short extension is "not resolving the core issue."
- **Option C: FISA Court certification carries the program.** Durbin noted the FISA Court approved a year-long certification in March, potentially allowing the program to continue into 2027 even without congressional reauthorization. This is legally contested terrain.
- **Option D: Lapse.** Most likely if no action by Friday. Cotton-Grassley letter to Rubio asks for contingency planning, including a potential executive order.

### ODNI Downsizing Angle
- Punchbowl reported that Trump administration officials are using the stalemate to push for scaling back or eliminating ODNI.
- Thune confirmed this is "part of the conversation" but said resolving the immediate situation would still require a permanent nominee.
- Most senators don't see ODNI's future as connected to the FISA deadline.
