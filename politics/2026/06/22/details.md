## 2. White House and President

### U.S.-Iran Talks Conclude First Round in Switzerland

**What happened:** Vice President JD Vance wrapped high-level talks with Iranian parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi at Bürgenstock, Switzerland. Mediators Pakistan and Qatar announced a joint statement declaring "encouraging progress," agreeing to a High Level Committee for political oversight, a "roadmap towards reaching a final deal within 60 days," and a "de-confliction cell" for Lebanon.

**Why it matters:** This is the first face-to-face engagement since the U.S.-Iran MOU was signed last week, extending a ceasefire from April. The talks tested whether the framework can hold amid ongoing Hezbollah-Israel fighting and Iran's on-again, off-again closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

**What Vance said:** "The final deal is the house. We set the foundation. We haven't built the house, but we've laid a successful foundation."

**Policy implications:** Vance floated unfreezing Iranian assets to purchase U.S. soy, corn, and wheat — an idea developed by Jared Kushner with Qatari officials. This could become a mechanism for sanctions relief tied to U.S. agricultural exports.

**Trump's role:** Trump nearly derailed the talks by threatening Iran on Fox News ("they won't even make it back") and on Truth Social. Talks briefly paused after Iranian officials objected, but back-channel efforts resumed them. The White House later confirmed everything Vance said has Trump's backing.

**What to watch next:** Technical talks continue this week in Switzerland. U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are handling details. The Lebanon ceasefire — the longest lull since March 2 — is holding but fragile. Iran says progress on Lebanon is the "real test."

### Senate Passes AI Accountability Act (68-29)

**What happened:** The Senate passed the AI Accountability Act last Thursday in a 68-29 vote. Fourteen Republicans joined all Democrats and independents. The bill requires federal agencies and vendors to document high-risk AI systems, report serious incidents within 72 hours, and face civil penalties for discriminatory outcomes in housing, credit, and employment.

**Key negotiators:** Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-WA, Commerce Chair) spent months shuttling between civil rights groups and the Chamber of Commerce.

**Why it matters:** This is the most significant federal AI legislation since 2023's voluntary commitments. Private lawsuits were limited to cases where federal contractors failed to maintain documentation or ignored stop-use orders.

**What to watch next:** House Speaker Mike Johnson will refer the bill to Energy & Commerce and Judiciary simultaneously. Freedom Caucus wants defense/intel exemptions; Progressive Caucus opposes sweeping exemptions.

**Source:** [Credence Wire](https://credencewire.com/article/senate-passes-ai-accountability-act-june-2026)

**Agency:** Department of Justice
**What's happening:** The DOJ is set to provide ~2 hours of recordings of former President Joe Biden speaking to his ghostwriter Mark Zwonitzer (2016-2017) to the House Judiciary Committee within three weeks, unless a court blocks it. Separately, DC District Judge Dabney Friedrich has already endorsed public release via the Heritage Foundation's FOIA lawsuit, pausing for three weeks for appeal.

**What to watch:** Biden is appealing; DC Circuit Court will weigh in during the three-week pause. Heritage Foundation lawyers argue the tapes are relevant to the 2026 election and questions about Biden's cognitive decline.
