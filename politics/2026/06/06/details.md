## 2. White House And President

### Schedule Policy/Career Executive Order (June 3)

- **What happened:** President Trump signed an executive order implementing "Schedule Policy/Career" in the excepted service, reclassifying approximately 8,000 senior career federal positions as at-will employment. The order revives and renames the 2020 Schedule F initiative that went unimplemented and was rescinded under Biden.
- **Why it matters:** The order strips civil service job protections from senior policy-influencing career positions, making those employees terminable for any reason. While scaled back from initial OPM estimates of 50,000 positions, the administration has not ruled out expanding the pool. Union and good-government groups have criticized it as politicizing the career civil service.
- **Official administration position:** OPM Director Scott Kupor said there will be no political litmus tests and the competitive service hiring process will be retained.
- **What to watch:** Whether agencies move quickly to reclassify positions, potential court challenges, and whether the administration expands the program.
- **Source:** [White House EO text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/implementing-schedule-policy-career-in-the-excepted-service/), [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/06/04/trump-executive-order-schedule-policy-career-classification-fire-federal-workers-at-will/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/03/nx-s1-5742806/trump-federal-employees-civil-service-job-protections-schedule-f), [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/workforce/2026/06/trump-moves-about-8000-federal-positions-to-schedule-policy-career/)

## 3. Congress: Senate

### FISA Section 702 Reauthorization Blocked

- **What happened:** Early Friday morning, the Senate voted 47-52 against a procedural motion to begin debate on a 3-year reauthorization of FISA Section 702. Seven Republicans joined nearly all Democrats in opposition. Only Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) crossed party lines to support the measure. The program expires June 12.
- **Why it matters:** Section 702 authorizes warrantless collection of foreign intelligence communications, which can incidentally sweep up Americans' data. The bill had bipartisan negotiated reforms. Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Tom Cotton (R-AR) and Ranking Member Mark Warner (D-VA) had reached a compromise, but Warner and other Democrats withdrew support after Trump tapped Bill Pulte (currently FHFA director) to serve as acting DNI. Sen. Warner said Democrats would not support FISA reauthorization with Pulte in charge.
- **Key players:** Senators Tom Cotton, Mark Warner, John Thune, Chuck Schumer, John Fetterman, Bill Pulte (nominee for DNI)
- **Expected next step:** The Senate is expected to revisit the legislation when it returns next week, with the June 12 deadline looming. Thune warned the program "goes dark" without action.
- **Source:** [Reuters/Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/us-senate-blocks-debate-fisa-115321608.html), [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/senate-push-reauthorize-nations-spy-powers-weathers-another-controversial-trump-decision), [AP/Times Union](https://www.timesunion.com/news/article/senate-blocks-extending-key-surveillance-program-22292373.php)

## 4. Congress: House Of Representatives

### Iran War Powers Resolution Passes (June 3)

- **What happened:** The House voted 215-208 to pass a war powers resolution directing President Trump to withdraw U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran. Four Republicans joined Democrats in support. The measure invokes the 1973 War Powers Act, which requires the president to end military action within 60 days without congressional authorization (with a possible 30-day extension).
- **Why it matters:** The vote marks a rare bipartisan congressional rebuke of an ongoing military conflict. However, it is largely symbolic — the Senate has not passed a companion measure, and even if it did, Trump would likely veto it. The conflict began Feb. 28 and has now surpassed 90 days.
- **Key players:** Four Republicans who crossed party lines (names not fully specified in available sources); Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) defended Trump's actions ahead of the vote.
- **Source:** [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/03/nx-s1-5845102/house-iran-war-powers-vote), [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/06/04/trump-iran-war-powers-resolution-house-republicans/), [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/03/us/politics/house-vote-trump-iran-war-powers.html)

### Ukraine Support Act Passes (June 4)

- **What happened:** The House passed the Ukraine Support Act 226-195, with 18 Republicans joining all but one Democrat (Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-MN) in support. The bill was brought to the floor via a discharge petition led by Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY), the ranking member on House Foreign Affairs, after months of GOP leadership refusing to schedule a vote. Rep. Kevin Kiley (CA-I) provided the decisive 218th signature on the discharge petition.
- **Why it matters:** The vote demonstrates significant Republican willingness to break with Trump on Ukraine policy. The legislation authorizes over $1 billion in direct assistance, up to $8 billion in military financing loans, reconstruction support, Baltic security aid, and sweeping new Russia sanctions targeting financial, energy, mining, and government sectors. However, the bill is unlikely to advance in the Senate, where Majority Leader John Thune has aligned closely with Trump.
- **Republicans who voted yes (18):** Don Bacon (NE), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA), Andrew Garbarino (NY), Carlos Gimenez (FL), Rob Bresnahan (PA), Nick LaLota (NY), Mike Lawler (NY), Michael McCaul (TX), Mike Carey (OH), Jeff Hurd (CO), David Joyce (OH), Jen Kiggans (VA), Max Miller (OH), Greg Murphy (NC), Dan Newhouse (WA), Glenn Thompson (PA), Mike Turner (OH), Joe Wilson (SC).
- **Expected next step:** The bill goes to the Senate, where its prospects are dim. Senate Republican leadership has shown no appetite for confronting Trump on Ukraine.
- **Source:** [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/international/5911086-house-passes-ukraine-aid/), [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/house-vote-ukraine-support-russia-sanctions-trump/33773123.html), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/house-passes-ukraine-aid-bill-full-list-of-republicans-who-backed-it-12034949)

### HASC Passes $1.15 Trillion NDAA (June 4)

- **What happened:** The House Armed Services Committee voted 44-12 to pass the FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act after a 14-hour markup session debating approximately 900 amendments. The bill authorizes $1.15 trillion in discretionary defense spending.
- **Why it matters:** All Republicans and nearly half of Democrats voted in favor, though Democratic support was unusually low for the historically bipartisan committee. The bill mirrors Trump's FY2027 defense request and is separate from an additional $350 billion the White House wants through budget reconciliation (bringing the total to $1.5 trillion). Chairman Mike Rogers (R-AL) called it the first budget in 40 years "that accounts for the true needs."
- **Expected next step:** The bill heads to the full House floor. The Senate Armed Services Committee has not yet announced a markup schedule.
- **Source:** [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/5911353-ndaa-amendments-pentagon-house-armed-services/), [InsideDefense](https://insidedefense.com/insider/house-armed-services-committee-passes-fy-27-defense-bill), [Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/06/05/1-15-trillion-in-defense-spending-approved-by-house-armed-services-committee/)

- **Agency:** U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (DHS)
- **What happened:** Judge McConnell vacated four policies that: (1) paused all asylum requests regardless of origin, (2) halted benefits adjudication for citizens of 39 "high risk" countries, (3) ordered reconsideration of past grants, and (4) required treating country of origin as a "significant negative factor."

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Judge Strikes Down USCIS Immigration Benefits Freeze
- **Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island
- **Judge:** Chief Judge John J. McConnell Jr. (Obama appointee)
- **Parties:** Dorcas International Institute of Rhode Island, Refugee Dream Center (plaintiffs) vs. USCIS, DHS (defendants)
- **What happened:** 135-page ruling vacating four policies. McConnell found the policies arbitrary and capricious, contrary to the Administrative Procedure Act, the Immigration and Nationality Act, and the Constitution's due process protections.
- **Key quote:** "The Government effectively invites the Court to shut its eyes and ignore the strong evidence of anti-immigrant animus before it. Doing so would require profound naiveté on the Court's part."
- **Status:** Immediate vacatur. Government may appeal.
- **Impact:** Hundreds of thousands of pending applications from citizens of 39 countries — including Iran, Nigeria, Venezuela — can now be processed. Affects asylum, green cards, work permits, and citizenship applications at USCIS.
- **Next:** Government has not yet announced an appeal.
- **Source:** [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/judge-trump-admin-illegally-paused-immigration-benefits-due/story?id=133628719), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/judge-strikes-down-trump-policy-that-halted-asylum-decisions-for-39-countries)
