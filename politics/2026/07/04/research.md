### Trump's America 250 Strategy
Trump has fully co-opted the semiquincentennial commemoration. The nonpartisan America250 commission (established by Congress in 2016) has been sidelined in favor of Freedom 250, a Trump-backed nonprofit. This mirrors his 2020 approach to Independence Day but on a much larger scale. Key elements:
- Mount Rushmore speech (July 3) — heavy on anti-communism framing
- "Salute to America" fireworks on National Mall (July 4) — record-breaking show
- Great American State Fair on National Mall — modest crowds reported
- Future events: Freedom 250 Grand Prix (August), Patriot Games youth athletic contest ($250k prize)

### SCOTUS Term Fallout (Term Just Ended)
The June 2026 SCOTUS term ended with several landmark rulings:
- **Birthright citizenship (6-3):** Struck down Trump's executive order; upheld 14th Amendment guarantee. Kavanaugh reportedly argued it could have been resolved on statutory grounds.
- **Independent agencies / removal power:** Ruled Trump may fire commissioners of most independent agencies — but carved out an exception for the Federal Reserve Board of Governors.
- **Campaign spending:** Potentially major impact on 2026 midterms per CFR analysis.
- **Mail-in voting:** Upheld state authority.
- **Gun purchasing age limits:** Declined to hear challenges.
- **TPS terminations:** Upheld administration's authority to terminate Temporary Protected Status.

### Trump's Election Law Losing Streak (Per The Atlantic)
The administration has lost at least a dozen election-related lawsuits. Specific losses in the past two weeks:
- Trump's March executive order on voting (blocked by district judges)
- DOJ effort to compel states to hand over voter rolls (blocked)
- DHS modified SAVE database expansion (blocked — judge said expanded system likely violated privacy and due process)
- USPS rule to restrict mail ballots (subject to federal injunction, then governor pushback)
- SAVE America Act — doomed in Senate, Trump at war with own party over it

### USPS Rule — Legal Status
- Trump signed executive order in March directing USCIS and SSA to create a "citizenship list" and USPS to limit mailed ballots to those on the lists
- USPS filed proposed rule in late May
- A federal judge has since blocked the executive order and barred agencies from implementing it, ruling it unconstitutional (only states and Congress set election rules)
- Democratic governors now pushing USPS to withdraw the proposed rule entirely
- Separate lawsuit by Democratic and civil rights groups — judge declined to block the EO initially because admin hadn't acted; groups have appealed
