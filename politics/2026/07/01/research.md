## Supreme Court Term-End Analysis

### Three Major Losses for Trump This Term
1. **Tariffs (February):** Court struck down most of Trump's global tariff authority.
2. **Fed Governor Firing (Monday June 29):** Court blocked Trump's bid to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook.
3. **Birthright Citizenship (Tuesday June 30):** Court struck down Executive Order 14,160.

### Shadow Docket Record
- Since Trump's second term began, the court has ruled on 23 shadow docket cases involving his administration.
- Issues have included: immigration raids, domestic troop deployment, abortion protections, firings of agency officials, transgender rights, cuts to foreign aid, and more.
- The conservative majority has backed him on many immigration-related policies but drawn lines on separation-of-powers issues.

### Campaign Finance Ruling — NRSC v. FEC
- Case originated in 2022 when then-Sen. JD Vance, then-Rep. Steve Chabot, NRSC, and NRCC sued in Cincinnati.
- The Trump administration's FEC declined to defend the law, instead joining challengers.
- The court appointed Roman Martinez to defend the limits; Democratic campaign committees also intervened.
- Ruling overrules FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee (2001).
- Practical effect: RNC has $125M cash-on-hand vs. DNC's $14.8M — disparity now far more impactful.

### Transgender Sports Ruling — Nuances
- **West Virginia v. B.P.J. & Little v. Hecox:** Combined opinion by Justice Kavanaugh.
- 6-3 on Title IX (ideological lines), but unanimous that state bans don't violate Title IX.
- 5-4 split on the constitutional question (Roberts, Barrett, Kagan, Sotomayor, Jackson vs. Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh).
- Left unresolved: lawsuits challenging laws in Connecticut, California, and other states that permit transgender athletes to compete.
- 27 states have enacted similar bans since 2020.

### Birthright Citizenship Ruling — Key Split
- **Trump v. Barbara:** Opinion by Chief Justice Roberts.
- 5 justices held the 14th Amendment itself bars the order: Roberts, Barrett, Kagan, Sotomayor, Jackson.
- Kavanaugh concurred in judgment only: the order violates federal statute (8 U.S.C. §1401(a)), not the Constitution — leaving the door open for Congress to amend the statute.
- Thomas, Alito, Gorsuch dissented, arguing the 14th Amendment allows the order.
- Kavanaugh's concurrence is significant: it provides a roadmap for legislative action without a constitutional amendment.
- However, it is extremely unlikely any bill curtailing birthright citizenship could clear a 60-vote Senate threshold.

## US-Iran Talks — Detailed Status

### Timeline
- **June 17:** US and Iran signed MoU extending ceasefire by 60 days.
- **Late June:** Renewed Strait of Hormuz tensions — Iran attacked vessels including a Qatari crude tanker, US launched retaliatory airstrikes, Iran attacked Bahrain and Kuwait.
- **June 30:** Witkoff and Kushner arrive in Doha; meet with Qatari PM.
- **July 1 (today):** Iranian negotiators meeting with Qatari officials on frozen assets and MoU implementation.

### Key Unresolved Issues
1. **$6B frozen Iranian assets:** Iran expects release as first step. Clause 13 ties final negotiations to progress on commitments.
2. **Strait of Hormuz:** Iran's "diplomatic gambit" to control the strait. US wants clarification of Iran's role per MoU.
3. **Nuclear program:** MoU calls for Iran to dilute enriched uranium stockpile.
4. **Lebanon:** Implementation of ceasefire provisions linked to broader deal.
5. **Direct vs. indirect talks:** US says it's ready for direct talks; Iran denies any direct meetings are planned.

### Iran's Position
- Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf: No final agreement talks until US implements key parts of interim deal.
- President Pezeshkian: Expects $6B release as first step.
- Tehran denies holding direct talks; says discussions are with Qatari mediators only.

### US Position
- VP Vance told Fox News the US is in a "great position" regardless of outcome.
- Trump reportedly told advisers he prefers diplomacy and is willing to extend the 60-day truce for nuclear discussions (per WSJ).
- Envoys not attending direct talks.
