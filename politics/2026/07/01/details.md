## 4. Congress: House of Representatives

### Floor Collapse Over SAVE America Act
- **What happened:** The House floor shut down after 14 Republicans joined Democrats, defeating a procedural rule 224-198. The rule would have set debate on the NDAA with the SAVE America Act attached via engrossment.
- **Key players:** Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL) led the revolt. Other holdouts included Reps. Max Miller (R-OH), Eric Burlison (R-MO), Tim Burchett (R-TN), Eli Crane (R-AZ), Victoria Spartz (R-IN), Thomas Massie (R-KY), and Lauren Boebert (R-CO). Majority Leader Scalise voted no to preserve reconsideration.
- **Why it matters:** The NDAA (troop pay raises, Pentagon programs), State Department appropriations, housing bill, and other legislation all stalled. House won't return until ~July 13.
- **What to watch:** Whether Johnson can negotiate an amendment strategy with Luna or pivot to a grants-based approach.
- **Source:** [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/save-act-mike-johnson-housing-bill-f9af93810930ad282ebb96934cbe1955), [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/07/01/gop-republican-house-defense-save-america-act/), [Freedom for All Americans](https://freedomforallamericans.org/speaker-johnson-save-act/)

### Tlaib Lebanon War Powers Resolution Defeated
- **What happened:** H.Con.Res. 108 failed 189-235. The revised version (exempting embassy protection and Lebanese Armed Forces cooperation) won Democratic leadership support but 22 Democrats still voted against it.
- **Why it matters:** The vote shows Democratic divisions on Israel-Lebanon policy, though far fewer Dems defected than the 117 who opposed the original version.
- **What to watch:** Potential for further war powers votes if US involvement in the region escalates.
- **Source:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/house-vote-rashida-tlaib-updated-war-powers-resolution-lebanon/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/30/lebanon-war-powers-vote-house-democrats-tlaib)

## 8. Courts and Legal/Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court: Birthright Citizenship (Trump v. Barbara)
- **Parties:** Trump administration vs. multiple challengers
- **What happened:** SCOTUS struck down Trump's executive order ending birthright citizenship, 6-3. Roberts wrote for the majority that the 14th Amendment guarantees citizenship to children born in the US regardless of parental status. Kavanaugh concurred only on statutory grounds.
- **Policy impact:** Major defeat for Trump's immigration agenda. Three Trump appointees (Gorsuch, Thomas, Alito) dissented. Kavanaugh's concurrence leaves open legislative pathways to restrict birthright citizenship via statute.
- **What to watch:** Whether House Republicans advance H.R. 569 (Birthright Citizenship Act) or other bills, and whether any such legislation could clear the Senate.
- **Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/supreme-court-strikes-down-trumps-order-ending-birthright-citizenship/), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-nixes-trump-attempt-limit-birthright-citizenship-rcna266935)

### Supreme Court: Transgender Athlete Bans (West Virginia v. B.P.J. / Little v. Hecox)
- **Parties:** West Virginia and Idaho vs. transgender athletes
- **What happened:** Court upheld state laws barring transgender girls and women from school sports, 6-3 on Title IX grounds. Kavanaugh wrote that states may maintain women's sports for biological females.
- **Policy impact:** Validates laws in 27 states. Left unresolved: lawsuits in states like Connecticut and California that permit transgender athletes to compete.
- **Source:** [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/supreme-court-transgender-athletes-school-teams-e01548be1fc0f574d9c274e077414075), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/court-rules-that-states-can-exclude-transgender-athletes-from-girls-and-womens-sports-teams/)

### Supreme Court: Campaign Finance (NRSC v. FEC)
- **Parties:** NRSC, NRCC, JD Vance, Steve Chabot vs. FEC
- **What happened:** Court struck down federal limits on coordinated party spending, 6-3, overruling a 2001 precedent. Kavanaugh wrote that limits violate the First Amendment.
- **Policy impact:** Removes caps on party-candidate coordinated spending (previously ranged from $63K to ~$4M depending on race). Republicans hold massive cash advantage — RNC $125M vs. DNC $14.8M.
- **What to watch:** How Democrats respond; potential for new legislation or disclosure requirements.
- **Source:** [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/supreme-court-campaign-finance-party-spending-ohio-91e49ee112197ae1210a9abfa46986ed), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/justices-strike-down-campaign-finance-law/)
