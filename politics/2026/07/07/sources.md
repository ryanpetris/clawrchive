## Federal Judge Rules Trump's Jan. 6 Pardon Does Not Cover DC Pipe Bomb Suspect

**Source:** CNN Politics
**URL:** https://edition.cnn.com/2026/07/06/politics/judge-jan-6-pardon-not-apply-dc-pipe-bomb-suspect
**Published:** July 6, 2026, 9:59 PM ET
**Author:** Piper HudspethBlackburn

### Full Text

A federal judge ruled Monday that President Donald Trump's expansive pardon of January 6 rioters does not apply to a Virginia man accused of planting two pipe bombs in Washington, DC, the night before the 2021 US Capitol insurrection.

Prosecutors say Brian Cole Jr. placed bombs near the Republican and Democratic national committees' headquarters on the night of January 5, 2021. His attorneys argued in March that Cole should be covered by Trump's pardon because the alleged conduct is "so inextricably and demonstrably tethered to" the events at the Capitol on January 6.

In a three-page order, US District Judge Amir H. Ali, an appointee of President Joe Biden, rejected Cole's argument. "Even assuming that the conduct Cole is charged with is 'related to events that occurred at or near the United States Capitol on January 6, 2021,' the pardon is expressly limited to people who had been 'convicted of offenses' related to those events," Ali wrote. "Cole had not been convicted of the conduct at issue when the President issued the pardon; indeed, he was not charged until many months after the President's proclamation."

Cole is due back in court Wednesday for a status hearing.

When Cole's lawyers asked the court in March to dismiss the case against him, a White House official disputed the defense team's argument: "The pipe bombs were placed on Jan 5. The pardon pertained to events at or near capitol on January 6 and clearly does not cover this scenario."

The FBI arrested Cole in December at his home. He pleaded not guilty in January to federal charges of transporting and attempting to use explosives.

## NATO Summit Opens in Ankara — Trump Pushes "Buy American" Defense

**Source:** POLITICO Europe
**URL:** https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/global-security/trump-moves-natos-goalposts-again/
**Published:** July 7, 2026
**Author:** Jack Detsch, Megan Messerly, Victor Jack, Laura Kayali, Zi-Ann Lum, Paul McLeary

### Key Excerpts

The NATO summit opens in Ankara, Turkey, today with leaders from 32 nations. Trump is pushing allies to buy American arms to help pay for increased defense spending. NATO nations increased spending a record 20% ($1 trillion) last year.

European allies plan to ink several missile deals with American defense contractors to kick off the summit. European officials fear Trump might go further and impose a "Buy-American" rule, putting them in a no-win situation between supporting their own defense industries and meeting Trump's demands.

The summit will be the last major diplomatic gathering for British PM Keir Starmer. French President Emmanuel Macron, term-limited, is also looking to leave his mark.

Trump will also push allies to contribute naval assets to help secure the Strait of Hormuz. European officials want to make the case against withdrawing U.S. troops ahead of a six-month Pentagon review announced by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Greenland remains a point of contention – U.S. officials said Trump's first choice is to "acquire" the Danish territory, though talks exploring other mechanisms are ongoing.

Canadian PM Mark Carney announced Germany's ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems as the supplier for Canada's next-generation submarines, a major shift from reliance on U.S. naval equipment.

## How This Supreme Court Term Empowered the President — and Reined Him In

**Source:** Christian Science Monitor
**URL:** https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Justice/2026/0707/supreme-court-presidential-power-limits
**Published:** July 7, 2026, 5:00 AM ET

### Key Excerpts

The Supreme Court's concluded term solidified a decades-long movement concentrating executive power under the president alone. Key rulings:

- **Trump v. Slaughter** (6-3): President can fire heads of independent regulatory agencies at will, with an exception for the Federal Reserve.
- **Trump v. Cook**: Lisa Cook can continue as Fed governor while challenging her firing, preserving Fed independence.
- **Emergency tariff ruling** (February): Court struck down Trump's emergency tariff regime.
- **Birthright citizenship**: Court ruled executive order reinterpreting birthright citizenship was unconstitutional.

The court issued 63 shadow docket decisions in the 2024-2025 term, more than any period in two decades, and more than the 56 merits decisions.
