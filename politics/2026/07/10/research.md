## Election Administration Overhaul Cluster

The last 48 hours represent the most dramatic federal intervention in election administration since the 2000 recount. Key context:

**EAC firings (July 9):** Trump removed Commissioners Thomas Hicks (D), Benjamin Hovland (D, chair), and Christy McCormick (R). The fourth commissioner, Donald Palmer (R), departed in April. This leaves zero commissioners. The EAC can no longer certify voting systems, accredit testing labs, or update the federal voter registration form — all critical functions 4 months before the midterms.

**DOJ letters to all 50 states (week of July 6-9):** Sent by a senior DOJ official. The letters warn election officials they face criminal prosecution if noncitizens vote. States given a 5-day deadline to respond identifying steps they've taken to prevent noncitizen voting. Illinois, Nevada, Idaho among states publicly confirmed as recipients.

**DHS grant conditions (July 2026):** FEMA's Homeland Security Grant Program (over $1B) will require states to:
- Transition from QR code/barcode voting systems to hand-marked paper ballots
- Conduct manual audits of at least 5% of ballots after each federal election
- Match voter participation numbers with ballot counts within 120 days of grant award
- Use SAVE database to verify citizenship of every registered voter
This is separate from the DOJ lawsuit against Pennsylvania (Judge Bissoon ruling that DOJ can't demand voter records with SSNs).

**Context:** These moves come after Republicans failed to pass the SAVE America Act through Congress. The strategy appears to achieve SAVE Act goals through executive action — using EAC regulation, DHS funding conditions, and DOJ prosecution threats rather than legislation.

**Uncertainty:** Whether any fired EAC commissioners will sue to challenge their removal. If they do, it would be the first direct test of whether the Supreme Court's new removal-power doctrine (Trump v. Slaughter) extends to bipartisan election agencies.

## Trade & Economy

However, Bloomberg reports the US will NOT immediately impose tariffs — instead directing negotiations with trading partners. This is a softer approach than the pharmaceutical Section 232 proclamation (April) which imposed 100% tariffs.
