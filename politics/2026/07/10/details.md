## 2. White House And President

### Trump fires all Election Assistance Commission members

**What happened:** On July 9, President Trump terminated the last three members of the four-member bipartisan Election Assistance Commission. Democratic Commissioners Thomas Hicks and Benjamin Hovland were fired via email from the White House Presidential Personnel Office. Republican Commissioner Christy McCormick was allowed to resign. The fourth commissioner, Republican Donald Palmer, left in April.

**Why it matters:** The EAC is the only federal agency solely devoted to election administration. It certifies voting systems, accredits testing laboratories, maintains the national mail voter registration form, and serves as a clearinghouse for election information. With no commissioners, the agency cannot take official action — meaning voting system certification, form updates, and all other EAC functions are frozen four months before the midterm elections. The firings follow the Supreme Court's late-June ruling in Trump v. Slaughter that granted the president expanded power to fire leaders of independent agencies.

**Policy/political implications:** The White House stated the president "reserves the right to remove individuals that may not be totally aligned with the important task of securing America's elections." Critics — including Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA) and the Brennan Center — say the removals represent political interference in election administration. Notably, the firings remove both Democratic AND Republican commissioners, suggesting the administration views the EAC's entire independent structure as an obstacle. The Senate-confirmed commissioners were all serving fixed terms.

**What to watch next:** Whether any fired commissioners challenge their removal in court. This would be the first direct test of whether the Supreme Court's new removal-power doctrine extends to bipartisan election agencies. Trump can appoint replacements, but they require Senate confirmation.

### Aircraft tariff proclamation (Section 232)

**What happened:** Trump signed a proclamation adjusting imports of commercial aircraft, jet engines, and aircraft/engine parts under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act.

**Why it matters:** Unlike the April pharmaceutical Section 232 proclamation (which imposed 100% tariffs), this one does NOT immediately impose tariffs. Instead, Trump directs the administration to negotiate with trading partners to adjust imports. Bloomberg reports the US "declined to immediately put tariffs" on these products.

**Policy implications:** This is a more measured approach that keeps tariff threats as leverage while pursuing negotiated outcomes. The aircraft industry has complex global supply chains (Boeing sources from multiple countries), making immediate tariffs disruptive. The proclamation follows a Commerce Department Section 232 investigation.

**What to watch:** Negotiation outcomes with key trading partners; whether tariffs are eventually imposed if talks stall.

**Sources:** [White House Proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/07/adjusting-imports-of-commercial-aircraft-jet-engines-and-aircraft-and-engine-parts-into-the-united-states/), [White House Fact Sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/07/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-adjusts-imports-of-commercial-aircraft-jet-engines-and-aircraft-and-engine-parts-into-the-united-states/), [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/us-declines-to-immediately-put-tariffs-on-aircraft-jet-parts)

### NATO Summit and Turkey Policy

**What happened:** Trump concluded the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, with several major announcements:
- He will lift CAATSA sanctions imposed on Turkey after its 2019 acquisition of the Russian S-400 missile system
- He will consider bringing Turkey back into the F-35 joint strike fighter program
- He met with Ukrainian President Zelenskyy and agreed to grant Ukraine a license to manufacture Patriot air defense systems domestically
- NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte publicly praised Trump throughout the summit

**Why it matters:** Lifting Turkey sanctions and returning Turkey to the F-35 program represent a major shift in US-Turkey relations, but face significant congressional opposition. The Hill reports a "fight" brewing in Congress over these moves. Separately, the F110 engine sale to Turkey — for its F-16 fleet — is proceeding after Congress did not block it.

**Policy/political implications:** Trump's approach at the summit generated significant commentary — NYT described him insulting allies while the alliance quietly moved toward European defense responsibility. NBC News characterized it as "NATO survives another summit in the Trump era." The Secret Service reportedly advised Trump not to fly on the newer Qatari-gifted Air Force One for his departure from Turkey, citing security concerns; Trump switched planes mid-journey and denied security issues.

**What to watch:** Congressional reaction to Turkey sanctions relief and F-35 plans; implementation timeline for Ukraine Patriot license.
