### Supreme Court Term Recap (2025-2026)
The Supreme Court's just-concluded term was one of the most consequential in decades. Key themes:
- **Presidential power:** The *Slaughter* and *Cook* decisions dramatically expand presidential removal power over the federal bureaucracy, overturning 1935 precedent (*Humphrey's Executor*).
- **Immigration:** Several decisions shifted immigration enforcement authority.
- **Tariffs:** The Court upheld certain tariff authorities.
- **Voting rights:** Decisions on election law and voter ID requirements.
- **Women's sports:** A Title IX-related decision.
- **Criminal law:** 16-17 "pure" criminal law cases decided, representing 28% of the 57 argued cases.

The Federal Reserve Board carve-out in *Slaughter/Cook* is notable — the Court appears to have drawn a line at independent monetary policy functions, but the scope of that exception is untested.

### Democratic Divide on Israel — Deeper Context
The 103-98-10 split among House Democrats on the Massie amendment is historic. Some key context:
- Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) is a libertarian-leaning Republican who frequently breaks with his party on foreign policy and civil liberties. His alliance with progressive Democrats on this issue reflects a cross-ideological coalition on anti-war and anti-foreign-aid positions.
- Minority Whip Katherine Clark (D-MA) breaking with Leader Jeffries (D-NY) is significant. In normal circumstances, the whip enforces party discipline. Her vote signals that the party leadership itself is divided on Israel.
- The 10 Democrats who voted "present" are notable — they declined to take a position, which in itself is a political statement.
- The vote was on an amendment to the State Department appropriations bill, which the House passed later Wednesday. The Massie amendment would have eliminated $3.3 billion in planned aid.

### Blanche Confirmation Math
The Senate Judiciary Committee has 12 Republicans and 10 Democrats (a 12-10 majority for the 119th Congress). With all Democrats expected to oppose Blanche:
- 12 Republicans are on the committee.
- If 11 vote yes and 1 votes no, the vote is 11-11, which means the nomination fails on a tie (tie votes do not advance nominations).
- If 12 Republicans vote yes, the nomination advances 12-10.
- So a single Republican "no" vote is indeed fatal.
