## 3. Congress: Senate

### Todd Blanche AG Confirmation Hearing

- **What happened:** Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche appeared before the Senate Judiciary Committee on Wednesday for his confirmation hearing to become permanent Attorney General. He faced intense questioning from both Democrats and some Republicans.
- **Key issues:**
  - **"Anti-weaponization" fund:** Blanche told the committee the $1.8 billion fund he helped launch is now "dead" amid bipartisan backlash. However, he conceded that the underlying settlement agreement remains an "enforceable contract," which critics say could allow Trump to sue for breach of contract and keep alive the fund's purpose.
  - **Epstein files:** Blanche was pressed on the release of Epstein-related documents and acknowledged mistakes in about 1% of documents that were released, saying the DOJ pulled down documents "within minutes" of being informed.
  - **Jan. 6 cases:** Democrats questioned Blanche about his independence from Trump and handling of Jan. 6-related prosecutions.
- **Why it matters:** Every Democrat on the Judiciary Committee is expected to vote against Blanche. That means a single Republican "no" vote would block his nomination from reaching the full Senate. Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) — who lost his primary after Trump endorsed his opponent — remains the key undecided vote. Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) walked away saying Blanche did "a good job" but wouldn't commit.
- **Next steps:** The committee will hear from outside witnesses on Thursday. Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) said Blanche would need roughly two weeks before a committee vote, meaning the earliest vote is July 30. Senate GOP leaders want to confirm Blanche on the floor the first week of August before the August recess.

## 8. Courts and Legal Developments

### Supreme Court — *Trump v. Slaughter* and *Trump v. Cook*
- **What happened:** The Supreme Court issued landmark decisions ending for-cause removal protections for most federal agency employees. The rulings overturn nine decades of precedent established during the New Deal era.
- **Scope:** The Court ruled that requiring cause to fire most federal employees is unconstitutional — they now serve at the president's pleasure. A narrow exception was carved out for members of the Federal Reserve Board.
- **Why it matters:** These decisions fundamentally reshape the structure of the federal administrative state, giving the president direct removal power over a vast range of agency officials who previously had civil service protections. The ruling could lead to sweeping personnel changes across the federal government.
- **Next:** Lower courts will grapple with the scope of the ruling. The Federal Reserve exception is narrow and may be tested.
- **Source:** [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/15/supreme-court-ruling-puts-torpedo-administrative-state), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/constitutional-administrative-law/1817744/the-end-of-for-cause-removal-supreme-court-reshapes-removal-protections-for-independent-agency-leadership), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/07/narrow-holdings-with-broad-consequences)

### Supreme Court — *Cisco Systems, Inc. v. Doe*
- **What happened:** The Supreme Court limited the scope of the Alien Tort Statute, ruling that federal courts cannot create new causes of action under the statute and limiting Torture Victim Protection Act claims.
- **Source:** [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/court-procedure/1817202/the-end-of-the-ats-road-supreme-court-limits-international-human-rights-lawsuits-in-us-courts)

### Supreme Court — *Wolford v. Lopez*
- **What happened:** The Court ruled on a Second Amendment case involving firearms on private property held open to the public, with the decision framed as a narrow answer to the specific question before the Court.
- **Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/07/narrow-holdings-with-broad-consequences)
