**Daily Political Outlook — July 16, 2026 — Trump Primetime Speech, Blanche Confirmation in Jeopardy**

- Trump delivers a primetime address tonight (6 PM PT / 9 PM ET) that he's calling "really, really big news" about election security. The Washington Post reports he plans to claim China accessed U.S. voter data. Democrats fear he's laying groundwork for a national emergency declaration or federal takeover of election administration ahead of the November midterms.

- Todd Blanche's confirmation as Attorney General is in serious doubt after a rocky Senate Judiciary Committee hearing. Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) remains uncommitted, and with all Democrats expected to oppose, a single Republican "no" would block the nomination. The committee vote is expected no earlier than July 30.

- 103 House Democrats voted to end $3.3 billion in military aid to Israel in a symbolic but striking rebuke. Minority Whip Katherine Clark broke with leadership to support it. The amendment failed 104-314, but the vote exposed deep Democratic divisions on Israel policy.

- The Supreme Court wrapped a landmark term with decisions in Trump v. Slaughter and Trump v. Cook, ending for-cause removal protections for most federal agency employees. The rulings reshape presidential power over the administrative state, overturning nine decades of precedent, though carving out an exception for the Federal Reserve Board.

- The U.S.-Iran ceasefire has fully collapsed. The U.S. launched new strikes on Iran Wednesday, reinstated a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, and struck a tanker trying to skirt the blockade. Trump is considering ground operations to seize Iranian islands and bombing the Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site.

- Trump is meeting Darline Graham, sister of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC), at the White House today. Graham's death remains a subject of conspiracy theories, which Trump has fueled.

- Jay Clayton, Trump's nominee for Director of National Intelligence, faced a contentious confirmation hearing where he refused to say whether Joe Biden won the 2020 election. Sen. Ossoff called his answers "disqualifying." The committee is expected to vote next week.

- CDC nominee Dr. Erica Schwartz vowed not to "betray the science" in her confirmation hearing, though several senators expressed skepticism about her independence from the White House and RFK Jr.'s influence.

- The House passed a bill to make daylight saving time permanent (308-117). Senate passage remains uncertain.
