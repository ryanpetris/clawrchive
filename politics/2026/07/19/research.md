## Iran War — Deeper Context

### Timeline of the War
- **Feb 28, 2026:** US and Israel launched initial airstrikes on Iran. The stated objective was preventing Iran from developing a nuclear weapon and securing the Strait of Hormuz.
- **April 8, 2026:** Trump announced an initial ceasefire with Iran.
- **June-July 2026:** The ceasefire unraveled. The US reimposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports. Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed.
- **Week of July 13:** The US resumed major airstrikes. Iran retaliated by hitting power and desalination plants in Kuwait.
- **July 18:** Two US service members killed in Jordan. Iran also hit Saudi Arabia with missile alerts.
- **July 19:** Seventh night of US strikes. Iran threatened UAE airports. Israel warned of Iranian missiles headed toward Aqaba, Jordan.

### Casualties
- US: 16 killed, 430+ wounded since the war began. 2 killed in this latest attack, 1 missing.
- Iran: At least 50 killed, 500+ wounded in the latest round of US strikes (per Iranian authorities — cannot independently verify).

### Iranian Demands / Position
- Supreme Leader Khamenei warned of "unforgettable lessons."
- Iran's joint military command threatened "devastating response" to US attacks.
- Iran suspended commitments to the interim peace deal.
- Iran has targeted Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and Jordan with retaliatory strikes.
- The UAE has not yet been targeted but was threatened via Fars news agency.

### Key Unresolved Questions
- Does the US have a defined endgame? Trump's initial April speech said "the hard part is done" but the war has since escalated significantly.
- Will Israel re-enter direct combat? Israel launched the war alongside the US but has refrained from reentering the renewed fighting. An Iranian strike on Israeli territory would likely change this.
- How long can the US sustain the current operational tempo?
- What is the diplomatic off-ramp after the interim deal collapsed?

## SAVE Act — Senate Vote Count Analysis

Public positions based on available reporting:
- **Supporting (estimates):** Roughly 45-47 GOP senators may support, but not confirmed.
- **Opposed — GOP:** Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK), Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) likely also opposed given her record. Possibly others.
- **Opposed — Democratic:** All 47 Democrats/Independents.

Math: 53 GOP seats. If 4 GOP senators oppose (Murkowski confirmed + Tillis + Collins + at least one other), that's 49 votes. Even if every other GOP senator supports, they need 60 to overcome a filibuster. The bill cannot pass without at least 7 Democratic votes, which appears impossible given Democratic opposition.

This confirms the SAVE Act is legislatively dead in the current Congress.

## Reconciliation 3.0 — History and Context

### Previous Reconciliation Bills
1. **One Big Beautiful Bill Act** (July 2025): $150B for defense. Used reconciliation to bypass filibuster. Signed into law.
2. **Immigration/Enforcement Package:** "Skinny" bill focused on Border Patrol funding. Passed.
3. **Reconciliation 3.0** (current): $95B framework — $60B defense, $13B intelligence, $12B farm aid, $10B state voter ID grants.

### Why the 83% Reduction
The Pentagon requested $350B through reconciliation for the broader "defense supercycle." The $350B was always two documents: a $1.15T discretionary budget request through normal appropriations, and $350B through reconciliation. Political reality: fiscal hawks won't add $350B to deficit without offsets; moderates won't vote for cuts. Result: $95B — "a number small enough to pass."

### Impact on Defense Programs
The $350B was concentrated in specific programs. Some high-profile defense programs draw 98% of their funding from the reconciliation bill. The 83% cut has significant implications for the defense-industrial base narrative that has driven some market expectations.

Sources: Alpha Briefing, Federal News Network

## ACIA Corporate Tax Bill — Compromise Options

Three frameworks being discussed:
1. **Sunset Option:** Rate cut temporary through 2031, saves ~$400B over 10 years
2. **Offset Package:** Carried interest elimination + tighten conservation easement deduction = $200-280B in offsets
3. **Phased Approach:** Immediate cut to 19.5%, step down to 18% contingent on deficit benchmarks

Private equity and real estate have made carried interest a red line. AIC (American Investment Council) has retained 3 additional lobbying firms since July 1.

TSMC's $15B Arizona Fab 3 and Nucor's Louisiana plate mill are conditional on passage.
