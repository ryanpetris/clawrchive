# Research Notes — July 17, 2026

## Additional Context And Analysis

### The Iran War — Deeper Background

- The conflict is now 138 days old. Trump declared the MOU with Iran "over" after previously claiming a ceasefire.
- The Pentagon has warned it's running out of money to conduct operations.
- The White House originally sought $350 billion in the reconciliation package, then $67 billion as a supplemental. What emerged is $73B for defense/intel in the $95B framework.
- The Pentagon comptroller nominee (Jules Hurst) couldn't tell senators the current cost of the war ($29 billion previous estimate) during his confirmation hearing.
- The NDAA includes provisions to deepen U.S.-Israeli intelligence integration that many Democrats find objectionable — including "data fusion" and mandatory intelligence sharing.

### The SAVE America Act — Legislative Mechanics

- The House passed the SAVE Act as standalone legislation earlier, but it lacks 60 Senate votes.
- Budget reconciliation is the only path to bypass the filibuster, but the Byrd Rule limits reconciliation to spending and tax provisions.
- The $10 billion in the House Administration Committee would fund state implementation of voter ID requirements, but the actual policy changes (requiring citizenship proof, photo ID) may not be achievable through reconciliation.
- Senate Majority Leader Thune has been consistently skeptical. Tillis is openly hostile to the strategy.
- 2026 is a midterm election year. Many state election processes are already underway, making it practically difficult to implement new requirements before November.

### Todd Blanche Confirmation — The Numbers

- Republicans hold 53 seats. Blanche can afford to lose 3 GOP votes (assuming all Dems vote no).
- Tillis (R-NC) and Cornyn (R-TX) are the two most significant question marks. Murkowski (R-AK) and Collins (R-ME) are also potential no votes.
- If Blanche loses, Trump would need to nominate a new AG, restarting the confirmation process — a significant delay.

### Midterm Fundraising — The Numbers

- NYT reported Democratic Senate candidates outraising Republicans in every key Senate race except Iowa.
- Punchbowl: Democratic candidates "raked in gargantuan amounts" of cash.
- Politico: Seven of the top 10 House battleground fundraisers were Democratic challengers.
- Roll Call: Democratic candidates "sustaining fundraising pace as GOP strains to keep up."
- The gap is especially notable because the political environment typically favors the out-party in midterms, and Republicans are defending very slim majorities.

### The Veterans Bill Failure

- The pull was particularly embarrassing because it happened minutes before the scheduled vote.
- This is the second major legislative embarrassment for Johnson in recent weeks (after the NDAA/SAVE Act fight).
