## 2. White House and President

### Housing Bill Becomes Law Without Signature

**What happened:** President Trump refused to sign the bipartisan housing affordability bill (the largest such bill in decades), demanding Congress first pass the SAVE Act — a strict voter ID bill that has not advanced in the Senate. Under constitutional rules, the bill became law automatically at midnight without his signature.

**Why it matters:** The housing bill enjoys broad bipartisan support and addresses a major affordability crisis. Trump's refusal to sign it — even while allowing it to become law — is a symbolic protest aimed at Senate Republicans who have been unable to advance voter ID legislation. It puts vulnerable House Republicans in a difficult position: they must defend a popular housing policy while their party's leader publicly rebukes them for not passing his priority voting bill.

**Political implications:**
- Vulnerable Republicans in swing districts face pressure from both Trump (who wants the SAVE Act) and constituents (who want housing relief)
- Democratic leaders are framing the episode as Trump putting election security demands over practical housing policy
- Trump's move signals he is willing to punish his own party to push the SAVE Act

**What to watch next:**
- Whether Senate leadership schedules SAVE Act votes
- Democratic messaging strategy around the housing bill's passage
- Polling on public reaction to Trump's refusal

### Iran Ceasefire Declared "Over"; Talks Continue

**What happened:** President Trump posted on social media Friday that the U.S.-Iran ceasefire is "over" — "in no uncertain terms," per his statement — but that he had agreed to continue negotiations with Tehran. Qatar, which helped broker the original truce last month, is mediating to prevent a return to hostilities. Meanwhile, U.S. Central Command conducted additional strikes against Iran to further degrade its ability to threaten freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** The ceasefire's collapse risks a return to active conflict in the U.S.-Israel campaign against Iran that began in February. Trump's simultaneous declaration that the ceasefire is "over" while agreeing to talk creates significant ambiguity about the status of hostilities.

**Additional context:** Israeli intelligence reportedly shared a plot by Iran to assassinate President Trump, which the president acknowledged on Wednesday as he departed the NATO summit in Turkey. This plot may have influenced the ceasefire decision.

### EPA — 2026 Unified Agenda Including PFAS Rulemakings

**What:** The Office of Management and Budget published the Trump administration's 2026 Unified Agenda on July 3. The EPA's agenda includes multiple rulemakings regarding per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS).

**Why it matters:** PFAS regulation has been a bipartisan priority. The unified agenda signals the administration's regulatory priorities for the coming year.

**Source:** [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/epas-2026-unified-agenda-includes-pfas-rulemakings)

### EPA — Proposed Revision to Diesel Emissions Regulations

**What:** The EPA proposed changes to federal diesel emissions regulations that would eliminate vehicle deratements caused by Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF) system failures.

**Why it matters:** This would reduce costs for trucking fleets and eliminate an issue manufacturers say has complicated purchasing decisions. The EPA proposal does not change the tougher NOx standard itself.

**Source:** [Brownfield Ag News](https://brownfieldagnews.com/news/epa-proposes-end-to-def-related-engine-deratements) / [Heavy Duty Trucking](https://truckinginfo.com/news/epa-proposal-could-ease-2027-truck-costs-and-buying-uncertainty)

### Election Assistance Commission — Trump Fires All Commissioners

**What happened:** President Trump fired all four members of the Election Assistance Commission. The move leaves the EAC with no commissioners and unable to take official actions.

**Why it matters:** This follows recent Supreme Court rulings that expanded executive authority over election administration. The EAC is the federal agency responsible for distributing election security funding and certifying voting systems. Without commissioners, it cannot issue grants, certify equipment, or take enforcement actions — just as the 2026 midterm election cycle intensifies.

**Status:** Confirmed — reported by Salon, Truthout.
