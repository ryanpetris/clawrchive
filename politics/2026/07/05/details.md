- **What happened:** The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) — created by executive order on Trump's first day back in the White House — reached its scheduled July 4, 2026 sunset date. - **What to watch next:** Whether individual DOGE cuts (contract cancellations, lease terminations) remain in effect or face legal challenges; whether any rump structure remains within the U.S. DOGE Service (formerly U.S. Digital Service).

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Supreme Court: Trump v. Slaughter and Trump v. Cook

- **Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
- **Parties involved:** President Trump v. FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter; President Trump v. Fed Governor Lisa Cook
- **What happened:** On June 29, the Court issued twin rulings simultaneously — a rare procedural move. In *Trump v. Slaughter* (6-3), the Court overturned the 91-year-old *Humphrey's Executor* precedent, ruling that the president has constitutional authority to fire heads of independent agencies (FTC, SEC, NLRB, etc.) at will. In *Trump v. Cook*, the Court held that the Federal Reserve's unique structure and historical role as a quasi-private monetary authority makes it an exception — Trump cannot fire Fed governors without cause.
- **Legal status:** Final rulings; no further appeals possible
- **Policy/political impact:** The Slaughter decision represents a fundamental restructuring of the separation of powers, giving the president direct control over agencies Congress had long insulated from political removal. The Cook carve-out preserves the Fed's monetary policy independence.
- **Next expected developments:** Potential further litigation over the scope of the Cook exception; possible removal actions against other agency heads; congressional response (bills to restructure agencies).
- **Analysis:** Chief Justice Roberts' reasoning in both cases relied heavily on historical practice rather than textual analysis — citing Jefferson, Madison, and the First Congress's understanding of the removal power. The coordinated release of both decisions simultaneously (to avoid market disruption from announcing Slaughter first without the Cook exception) was described by the Washington Examiner as "rare and possibly unprecedented."
- **Source:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-rules-trump-cannot-fire-fed-member-lisa-cook-grants-powe-rcna234931), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/06/court-prevents-trump-from-firing-fed-governor/), [Washington Examiner](https://washingtonexaminer.com/opinion/editorials/4631511/roberts-threads-needle-executive-power)

3. **Iran diplomacy is paused for Khamenei's funeral,** which concludes Thursday. The Hormuz toll dispute could become the next flashpoint after the burial. Erdogan's accusation that Israel is undermining the peace deal adds a complicating factor.

5. **Today is a light news Sunday following a major holiday weekend.** The substantive Washington news cycle resumes Monday, with the Iran funeral, OMB deadline, and post-Slaughter agency actions likely to dominate the week ahead.
