## 2. White House And President

### U.S. Launches Major New Strikes on Iran

- **What happened:** The United States confirmed a series of major new attacks against Iran, striking more than 80 sites including air defense systems, command and control networks, coastal radar sites, anti-ship missile capabilities, and over 60 Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps small boats. CENTCOM said the strikes were "an immediate response" to Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. Simultaneously, OFAC revoked the authorization for Iran to export oil under the provisional peace agreement, giving Iran until July 17 to complete existing sales.
- **Why it matters:** This represents the most significant military escalation since the U.S.-Iran ceasefire, directly threatening the 60-day negotiation window for a permanent deal. The interim peace agreement — which included oil sales, sanctions relief, and a $300 billion reconstruction fund — is now in serious jeopardy.
- **Policy or political implications:** Oil prices rose more than 5% immediately. The strikes are a major test of whether the provisional peace agreement can survive. Trump is under domestic political pressure as polling shows sustained opposition to the war. The administration insists negotiations will continue.
- **What to watch next:** Iran's response; whether the 60-day negotiation window (through ~August 21) collapses; further Strait of Hormuz attacks; oil market reactions.
- **Source:** [EL PAÍS](https://english.elpais.com/international/2026-07-08/us-launches-new-strikes-against-iran-in-retaliation-for-attacks-on-tankers-in-the-strait-of-hormuz.html), [The Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/07/08/world/politics/us-strikes-iran-oil-shipping-attacks/)

### NATO Summit — Trump Demands Greenland, Attacks Allies, Offers F-35s to Turkey

- **What happened:** At the NATO summit in Ankara, Trump renewed his demand that Greenland "should be controlled by the United States, not by Denmark." He criticized European allies for not supporting the U.S. in the Iran war, singled out UK PM Starmer, Italy, Germany, and France, and threatened to reduce U.S. military presence in Europe. He signaled he would lift sanctions on Turkey and consider allowing F-35 fighter jet sales to Turkey. NATO Secretary General Rutte showcased billions in new contracts including Saab GlobalEye surveillance planes, Airbus transport aircraft, and Triton surveillance drones — an effort to appease Trump over defense spending.
- **Why it matters:** The NATO alliance is facing its most serious internal crisis in decades. Trump's threats to withdraw from NATO, his demand for Greenland (a NATO member's territory), and his overtures to Erdogan signal a fundamental reshaping of the transatlantic alliance. The U.S. is conducting a six-month review of its European force posture.
- **Policy or political implications:** European allies are racing to fill defense gaps as the U.S. pulls back. The U.S.-Turkey relationship is warming while traditional European alliances fray. The Iran war continues to be a central point of friction.
- **What to watch next:** Day 2 of NATO summit (July 8); final communiqué language on defense spending; any concrete U.S. troop reduction announcements; Turkey F-35 timeline.
- **Sources:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nato-summit-begins-in-turkey-as-trump-pushes-for-more-defense-spending/), [PBS News](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trump-again-demands-greenland-as-nato-unveils-military-projects-worth-billions-to-prove-its-firepower), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/07/07/trump-greenland-should-be-controlled-by-united-states-alliance-nato-summit/), [EL PAÍS](https://english.elpais.com/international/2026-07-07/at-nato-summit-trump-renews-his-threats-over-greenland-and-lashes-out-at-european-allies.html)

## 3. Congress: Senate

### DOJ Letters to All 50 States on Noncitizen Voting

- **What happened:** Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon (Civil Rights Division) sent letters to election officials in all 50 states and D.C., threatening criminal prosecution if they knowingly allow noncitizens to vote or remain on voter rolls. States have five days to respond explaining how they will comply.
- **Why it matters:** This is a major escalation of the Trump administration's push for federal control over elections. The DOJ has lost all 11 lawsuits seeking voter roll data, including a recent Sixth Circuit defeat. Experts say noncitizen voting is exceedingly rare (0.0001% in 2016 per a Brennan Center study).
- **Key players:** AAG Harmeet Dhillon (DOJ Civil Rights); state election officials (AZ's Adrian Fontes, NV's Francisco Aguilar, CO's Jena Griswold)
- **Expected next step:** States' five-day response deadline; likely legal challenges; continued DOJ litigation for voter roll access.
- **Sources:** [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/ahead-midterms-doj-warns-state-officials-potential-criminal/story?id=134566080), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/doj-threatens-criminal-action-against-states-that-allow-non-citizens-to-vote/)

### SAVE America Act Debate

- **What happened:** Senate Republicans voted 51-48 to take up the House-passed SAVE America Act, which would require proof of citizenship to register to vote and photo ID at polls. Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) voted against opening debate; Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) did not vote. Trump called the bill his "No. 1 priority in Congress." Debate is expected to continue at least into next week.
- **Why it matters:** Democrats uniformly oppose the bill, arguing it would disenfranchise millions of voters who lack birth certificates or passports. With a 53-47 Republican majority, the bill would need 60 votes to overcome a filibuster — a threshold it is very unlikely to reach.
- **Key players:** Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD); Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK); President Trump; all Senate Democrats
- **Expected next step:** Continued floor debate; Democratic filibuster likely; Trump has suggested eliminating the filibuster.
- **Source:** [ORT / CNBC TV18](https://ort.org/c4799230/546879beBSJBfRoeAhwLLVk)

### McConnell Remains Hospitalized

- **What happened:** Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY), 84, remains in a Washington, D.C., hospital since June 14. His office has not disclosed his condition. Thune, Barrasso, and strategist Scott Jennings have spoken with him by phone. He is said to be "fully engaged" and "eager to get back."
- **Why it matters:** McConnell's absence complicates Senate math. His missed vote on June 23 allowed a resolution to pass directing Trump to withdraw troops from Iran (with 4 GOP defectors). His absence also casts doubt on the Pentagon budget reconciliation package and defense supplemental.
- **What to watch next:** Whether McConnell returns when the Senate resumes next week; whether his condition requires a special election to fill his seat for the remainder of 2026.
- **Sources:** [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4639088/top-republicans-spoke-phone-call-mitch-mcconnell-while-in-hospital-john-barrasso/), [DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/07/08/mcconnell-speaks-to-republican-leaders-as-speculation-swirls-about-his-health-remains-hospitalized/)

### Drug Pricing Bill Splits Healthcare Industry

- **What happened:** The Prescription Drug Affordability and Access Act (introduced June 30 by Sen. Ron Wyden, D-OR) would expand Medicare drug price negotiation from 20 to 75 drugs annually, shorten exclusivity periods, and tie Medicaid rates to Medicare benchmarks. CBO scores it at $340B in savings over a decade. PhRMA has launched a massive lobbying campaign with 23 registered firms. The XBI biotech ETF has dropped 8.3% since introduction.
- **Why it matters:** This is the most aggressive federal drug pricing legislation since Medicare Part D. It pits the pharmaceutical industry ($200M committed lobbying) against insurers (AHIP has endorsed) and creates a difficult political calculus for the White House, which needs drug industry investment for its reshoring agenda.
- **Key players:** Sen. Ron Wyden (D-OR); Sens. Raphael Warnock (D-GA) and Bill Cassidy (R-LA) (co-sponsors); HHS Secretary RFK Jr.; PhRMA
- **Expected next step:** Senate Finance Committee markup begins July 22.
- **Source:** [USA Business Times](https://usabusinesstimes.com/senates-new-drug-pricing-bill-is-splitting-the-healthcare-industry/)

### Farm Bill 2.0 Discussion Draft Released

- **What happened:** Senate Agriculture Committee Chairman John Boozman (R-AR) released a discussion draft for Farm Bill 2.0 incorporating more than 100 bipartisan measures.
- **Why it matters:** A new farm bill has been a priority for agricultural states. The bipartisan framing suggests a potential path through a divided Congress.
- **Source:** [Senate Agriculture Committee](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/newsroom/rep/press/release/farm-bill-20-built-for-farmers-with-more-than-100-bipartisan-measures)

### Senate Passed Short-Term Funding Bill (Prior Day)

- **What happened:** The Senate passed a short-term funding bill to reopen government, including back pay for federal workers, current-level funding, and targeted emergency funds for border management and disaster response. The extension runs only into early spring, ensuring another fiscal showdown.

- **What happened:** Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) told reporters Congress "can eliminate an entire district court" and has "power of funding over the courts." - **Key players:** Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA); Rep. Jim Jordan (R-OH); Rep. Darrell Issa (R-CA); Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO); Rep. Dave Joyce (R-OH)
