## 2. White House And President

### Trump Primetime Address on Election Security (July 16)

- **What happened:** President Trump delivered a ~30-minute primetime address from the White House alleging China obtained 220 million U.S. voter registration files between 2020-2023. He declassified and released hundreds of pages of intelligence documents through a new White House website. Trump claimed the data includes names, addresses, phone numbers, and party affiliations, calling it "an unprecedented election security nightmare."
- **Why it matters:** The speech reprised Trump's central 2020 election grievance without presenting evidence that votes were changed or machines hacked. He used the address to pressure Congress to pass the SAVE America Act, which would require government-issued photo ID to vote and documentary proof of citizenship to register. The speech is widely viewed as laying groundwork to challenge the 2026 midterm results if Republicans lose.
- **Fact check:** Intelligence community assessments have consistently found China did not interfere with election infrastructure or vote-counting. Voter registration data is publicly available in many states. The 2021 National Intelligence Council report — with high confidence — assessed that China "did not deploy interference efforts" in the 2020 election.
- **What to watch next:** Whether the SAVE Act gains any new momentum in the Senate following the speech, and how state election officials respond to White House notifications about "compromised" voter data.

## 3. Congress: Senate

- **What happened:** The race to replace the late Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) — who died July 11 of an aortic dissection at age 71 — is rapidly taking shape. Gov. ### NDAA Stalled — Democrats Block Motion to Proceed

- **What happened:** Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) blocked the motion to proceed on the FY2026 National Defense Authorization Act, a procedural move that Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) called "unprecedented." Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) called the bill a "war-funding authorization bill" for the Iran conflict. Some Democrats, including Sen. Jack Reed (D-RI), the ranking member of Armed Services, were reportedly wavering on whether to support proceeding.
- **Why it matters:** The NDAA has passed Congress for 64 consecutive years. Blocking it is a significant escalation in partisan warfare over defense policy, specifically tied to Democratic opposition to the Iran military campaign. The bill contains major defense spending increases, and Democrats argue it was drafted without bipartisan input.
- **What to watch next:** Whether a compromise path forward emerges, or whether the NDAA becomes a vehicle for a larger budget or Iran policy fight. Thune may attempt to bring the bill back to the floor under different procedural terms.
- **Source:** [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/4532165-schumer-blocks-ndaa-senate/)

### SAVE America Act

- **What happened:** The SAVE America Act — which would require government-issued photo ID to vote and documentary proof of citizenship to register, and ban most mail voting — remains stalled in the Senate. Trump devoted a significant portion of his July 16 address to urging its passage. - **Why it matters:** The bill is the centerpiece of Trump's election agenda. Senate Republicans are divided on the filibuster question, with some (including Norman, Lee, and Scott) advocating to eliminate it, while others (including Thune and McConnell) have historically defended it.
- **What to watch next:** Whether Trump's primetime address shifts any Senate votes on the SAVE Act, and whether the SC Senate race becomes a proxy battle over the filibuster.

## 6. Federal Agencies: Official Announcements

### Securities and Exchange Commission

- **Regulation E-Delivery Proposal (July 16):** SEC proposed a new rule allowing electronic delivery of prospectuses, shareholder reports, proxy statements, trade confirmations, and other disclosures as the default method, replacing the current paper-by-default regime. Recipients would receive two paper notices before transition and could opt out. The rule would save issuers, brokers, and advisors significant printing/postage costs. 60-day public comment period following Federal Register publication.
- **Why it matters:** This is a major deregulatory shift under SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins, modernizing disclosure for the digital age.
- **Source:** [SEC Press Release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-67-sec-proposes-new-e-delivery-approach-make-information-more-readily-accessible-useful-investors)

### Federal Communications Commission

- **Broadcast Ownership Cap Repeal Vote Announced (July 15):** FCC Chair Brendan Carr announced the Commission will vote next month to repeal the national broadcast ownership rule, which currently prohibits a single company from reaching more than 39% of U.S. TV households. The move benefits Trump-aligned media companies like Sinclair and Nexstar. Expected to face court challenges.
- **Covered Equipment Import/Marketing Ban (Effective July 16):** FCC's Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau and Office of Engineering and Technology prohibited continued importation and marketing of previously authorized communications equipment added to the "Covered List" in 2024 or earlier, citing national security risks. Equipment from Huawei, ZTE, and other designated entities is affected. Existing consumer equipment remains legal to use.
- **Submarine Cable Licensing Requirements (June 30):** FCC adopted new licensing requirements for Submarine Line Terminal Equipment (SLTE) owners/operators, with national security certification standards. Presumptively exempts applications meeting 10 security standards from Team Telecom referral.
- **Spectrum Sharing Modernization (Effective July 13):** Final rule replacing decades-old EPFD limits for satellite broadband with performance-based GSO protection criteria, benefiting NGSO systems (Starlink, etc.).
- **Sources:** [CNN - Broadcast Cap](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/15/media/fcc-carr-trump-broadcasters-sinclair-nexstar), [Federal Register - Covered Equipment](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-06/pdf/2026-13518.pdf), [GT Law - Submarine Cables](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/7/fcc-establishes-new-submarine-cable-licensing-requirements-seeks-comment-on-additional-measures), [Federal Register - Spectrum](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-13/html/2026-09565.htm)

### Environmental Protection Agency

- **New Approach Methods (NAMs) for Chemical Testing (June 2):** EPA announced two major actions to replace animal testing with advanced alternatives (NAMs) for chemical assessments under TSCA and FIFRA, advancing the goal of eliminating all mammalian testing by 2035.
- **TSCA Review of Five Chemicals:** EPA advanced review of 1,1,2-trichloroethane, TBBPA, 1,2-dichloropropane, ethylene dibromide, and trans-1,2-dichloroethylene; comments due July 23.
- **UCMR 6 Proposed (July 1):** EPA proposed the sixth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule for drinking water, targeting PFAS and other emerging contaminants. Comments due August 31.
- **Asbestos Legacy Use/Disposal Info Collection:** EPA seeking data to develop TSCA regulations for legacy uses and disposals of asbestos. Responses due August 24.
- **HFC Phasedown Reconsideration (Effective July 27):** EPA final rule extending compliance deadlines for certain refrigeration and air conditioning sectors under the AIM Act, including delaying semiconductor manufacturing equipment deadlines to 2030.
- **NEPA Procedures Proposed Amendments (Comments due July 27):** EPA proposed amendments to its NEPA implementing procedures.
- **Plywood MACT Final Rule (Effective July 6):** Final emissions standards for hazardous air pollutants from plywood/ composite wood products.
- **Sources:** [Bergeson & Campbell - EPA Developments](https://www.lawbc.com/recent-federal-developments-for-july-2026/), [Federal Register - HFC Rule](https://thefederalregister.org/documents/2026-10387/phasedown-of-hydrofluorocarbons-reconsideration-of-certain-regulatory-requirements-promulgated-under-the-technology-tran)
