### U.S.-Iran Conflict: Timeline Context

The current escalation represents a dramatic reversal from the late-June 2026 interim agreement (MOU) that was supposed to open the Strait of Hormuz and provide a 60-day negotiation window. Key context:

- **Feb 28, 2026:** Trump announced "major combat operations" against Iran with joint U.S.-Israeli strikes
- **Late June 2026:** U.S.-Iran signed interim agreement; strait reopened; 60-day pause for negotiations
- **July 7, 2026:** U.S. resumed "defensive strikes" per Trump's War Powers notification
- **July 11-13, 2026:** Three consecutive nights of U.S. strikes against Iranian military targets
- **July 13, 2026:** Trump announced blockade reimposition and proposed 20% toll
- **July 14, 2026:** Blockade effective 4 PM ET; Iran hits Emirati tankers and Bahrain

The toll proposal is notable — Trump explicitly framed it as reimbursement from Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain for U.S. protection costs. Former Assistant Secretary of State David Schenker noted this approach is "not unprecedented" from the first Trump administration but not optimal.

Iran's position: Foreign minister agreed on "compensation" for security but stated "Iran has always been the GUARDIAN of the Strait and will remain so FOREVER."

IMO position: Transit through the strait should remain toll-free.

### Bears Ears / Grand Staircase-Escalante: Legal Context

Trump first reduced these monuments in 2017. Biden restored them in 2021. Trump's new 2026 proclamations:
- Bears Ears: ~121,096 acres (down from ~1.36M under Biden) — two units (Indian Creek, Shash Jáa)
- Grand Staircase-Escalante: ~181,541 acres (down from ~1.87M) — two units (Canyons and Escalante, Kaiparowits Horizon)

The new proclamations specifically address multiple-use management, livestock grazing, recreation, and hunting. Environmental groups are expected to challenge the president's authority to modify monument boundaries under the Antiquities Act — the same legal question that arose after 2017 but was never fully resolved.

### Federal Reserve / Warsh Context

Kevin Warsh was confirmed as Fed Chair after Trump's decision not to renominate Jerome Powell. This is his first semi-annual testimony.

Wall Street sentiment (per Bloomberg): Warsh has been so careful not to signal where rates are headed that he's obscured how he'd actually react to economic challenges. Investors want to know his "reaction function" — how he responds to data rather than what specific rate path he envisions.

The June CPI at 3.5% provides an interesting backdrop. The improvement was driven primarily by energy prices falling after the late-June U.S.-Iran MOU. With the MOU collapsed and oil prices rising again, the question is whether this improvement is sustainable.

### Section 232 Aircraft: Unusual Approach

Rather than imposing tariffs, Trump ordered negotiations with trading partners over aircraft imports found to threaten national security. This is a relatively restrained approach compared to previous Section 232 actions on steel/aluminum. The 180-day review window leaves room for tariffs if negotiations fail.
