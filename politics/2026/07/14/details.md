## 2. White House and President

### U.S.-Iran Conflict Escalates

**What happened (confirmed):**
- U.S. Central Command said it completed a third consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military targets on July 13-14. The five-hour mission struck Iranian coastal defense systems, missile and drone sites, and maritime capabilities.
- Trump submitted a War Powers Resolution notification to Congress Monday covering strikes beginning July 7, stating U.S. ground forces are not involved and strikes are "limited" and "measured."
- Trump ordered reinstatement of a naval blockade on Iranian ports effective Tuesday at 4 PM ET and floated a 20% toll charge for ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz, citing reimbursement for security provided to Gulf nations.
- The interim U.S.-Iran agreement signed last month (60-day ceasefire/negotiation window) has effectively collapsed.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz carried approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and gas before the conflict. Brent crude rose 2% to $85/barrel, WTI up 2.3% to $80. War risk premiums are rising sharply. Shipping traffic declined ~52% week-over-week.

**What to watch next:** Whether the blockade and toll plan triggers further escalation with Iran or Gulf allies; impact on global energy markets; congressional debate over war powers.

### National Monument Reductions

**What happened (confirmed):**
- Trump signed proclamations on July 13 shrinking Bears Ears National Monument from ~1.36 million acres to ~121,096 acres, and Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument from ~1.87 million acres to ~181,541 acres.
- This reverses Biden-era expansions and restores boundaries close to Trump's 2017 reductions.

**Why it matters:** Environmental groups are expected to sue. The reductions open land to potential energy development, mining, and grazing. Another flashpoint in public lands management.

### EPA Regulatory Relief

**What happened (confirmed):**
- Trump signed a proclamation granting 2-year exemptions from certain Biden-era EPA emissions rules (the HON Rule under Clean Air Act Section 112) for chemical manufacturers producing inputs for semiconductor production, medical device sterilization, advanced manufacturing, and national defense systems.
- The proclamation cites unavailability of compliance technology and national security interests.

## 3. Congress: Senate

### Senate Returns After Graham's Death

**What happened (confirmed):**
- Senate convened at 10 AM today with an uncertain agenda following the sudden death of Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC).
- South Carolina Gov. Henry McMaster appointed Graham's sister, Darline Graham Nordone, to temporarily fill the seat through January. Trump endorsed the appointment.
- A Republican special primary is scheduled for August to select the party's nominee for the subsequent full six-year term. Trump suggested Rep. Russell Fry (R-SC) could be a contender.
- Sen. Mitch McConnell remains absent due to prolonged illness (hospitalized for approximately one month).

**Why it matters:** With Graham's seat unfilled until Nordone is sworn in, and McConnell absent, Republicans' effective working majority narrowed to a razor-thin margin. Nordone's expected swearing-in this week restores the full 53-47 majority, but McConnell's absence still presents challenges for Thune's agenda.

### Senate Vote: Iran War Powers Resolution

**What happened (confirmed):**
- Senate voted 50-47 against advancing Sen. Tim Kaine's (D-VA) resolution to restrict Trump's war powers against Iran.
- Sens. Susan Collins (R-ME) and Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) broke with their party to support the measure.
- Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) abstained.
- Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), who initially supported restriction, reversed course after a White House briefing.

**Analysis:** The vote demonstrates continued GOP cohesion around Trump even as his presidency winds down. The narrow margin and defections indicate some unease in the conference.

## 4. Congress: House of Representatives

### Johnson's "Agenda 3.0" Push

**What happened (confirmed/reported):**
- House Speaker Mike Johnson announced basic tenets of a third GOP reconciliation bill ("Trump's Agenda 3.0") including Pentagon funding, cutting government fraud, and a grant program for election overhaul (the SAVE America Act).
- Johnson, House Budget Chair Jodey Arrington (R-TX), and White House officials held a two-day Camp David strategy session.
- Johnson reached a critical truce with Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL), who had been blocking floor proceedings. Luna agreed to GOP leadership's commitment to attach the SAVE America Act to upcoming spending legislation.
- Johnson met Monday with Trump and House campaign chief for November strategy discussions.

**Key challenges:**
- Senate Majority Leader Thune did not endorse the effort and warned about Senate limitations, particularly on healthcare or tax provisions.
- Thune cautioned that including healthcare/tax provisions could create "very challenging amendment votes" under budget rules.
- Graham's death complicates Senate budget work (he chaired the Budget Committee; Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI) is picking up duties).
- Trump continues to create complications: refused to sign the bipartisan housing bill; undercut Johnson on the elections bill; criticized Senate Republicans.

**Why it matters:** This may be Republicans' last major legislative push before the August recess and November midterms. Success or failure could significantly affect GOP electoral prospects.
