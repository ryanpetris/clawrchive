## 3. Congress: Senate

### Immigration Reconciliation Bill Stalls

**What happened:** Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.) abandoned plans to advance the $72 billion immigration enforcement reconciliation bill before the Memorial Day recess after GOP senators revolted over Trump's $1.776B DOJ "anti-weaponization" fund.

**Key senators involved:**
- **John Thune (R-SD)** — Punted the bill after realizing at least 20 GOP senators would vote for Democratic amendments restricting the fund.
- **Mitch McConnell (R-KY)** — Called the fund "utterly stupid, morally wrong." 
- **Thom Tillis (R-NC)** — Escalated criticism, calling it "politically tone deaf."
- **Katie Britt (R-AL)** — Also pushed back despite being a Trump ally.
- **Ted Cruz (R-TX)** — Predicted 20 Republicans would back restrictions on the fund.
- **Rick Scott (R-FL)** — Said he doesn't need compensation despite his phone records being subpoenaed in the "Arctic Frost" investigation.
- **Ron Johnson (R-WI)** — Defended the fund conceptually but said he personally "would not apply" for a settlement.

**Why it matters:** The bill would fund ICE and Border Patrol through the end of Trump's term. The delay means Congress will blow past Trump's June 1 deadline. It also threatens the timeline for a third reconciliation package (potentially bundling Iran military funding, defense, healthcare reform, and fraud prevention) that Republicans hoped to pass before the August recess.

**Expected next step:** When the Senate returns the week of June 1, Thune will need to find consensus language on the DOJ fund — or the bill could collapse entirely.

**Sources:** [Punchbowl News](https://punchbowl.news/article/senate/woes-getting-worse/), [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/politics/house-and-senate-republicans-are-at-odds-over-trumps-anti-weaponization-fund/), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5892237-republican-reconciliation-bill-concerns/), [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4579639/gop-senators-spurn-anti-weaponization-fund-phone-data/), [AP via EuropeSays](https://www.europesays.com/people/87330/)

### DOJ Fund Creates Intraparty Crisis

**What happened:** The Trump administration created the $1.776B fund as part of a settlement resolving Trump's lawsuit against the IRS over the leak of his tax returns. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche held a contentious 2.5-hour closed-door meeting with GOP senators that erupted into a shouting match.

**Key details:** A DOJ one-pager confirmed the fund would be overseen by five commissioners (four appointed by Blanche, one in consultation with congressional leaders). It noted Trump and his sons would receive apologies rather than monetary payments. However, it contained no language barring payments to Jan. 6 defendants convicted of assaulting police.

**Why it matters:** Senate Republicans want binding legislative language, not assurances. The fund has no statutory limits, and Democrats can force votes on it during the reconciliation vote-a-rama.

**What to watch next:** Whether Thune can negotiate a compromise — possibly including independent oversight of commissioners — that satisfies enough Senate Republicans while not alienating House conservatives and Trump.

**Sources:** [Punchbowl News](https://punchbowl.news/article/senate/woes-getting-worse/), [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/politics/house-and-senate-republicans-are-at-odds-over-trumps-anti-weaponization-fund/), [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/senate/4579639/gop-senators-spurn-anti-weaponization-fund-phone-data/)

## 6. Federal Agencies: Official Announcements

- **Type:** Policy action / record-keeping
- **What happened:** DOJ removed all Jan. 6-related prosecution press releases from its website, calling them "partisan propaganda." DOJ Rapid Response account: "We are proud to reverse the DOJ's weaponization under the Biden administration."

### Department of Justice — Jan. 6 Case Dismissals

**What is being reported:** A federal appeals court granted DOJ's unopposed motion to vacate seditious conspiracy convictions against leaders and members of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers on Thursday, May 22. On Friday, May 23, DOJ moved to formally dismiss all remaining cases against those group members.

**Confirmed/status:** Confirmed. Court filings and DOJ statements confirm the actions.

**Why it matters:** This goes beyond pardons (which forgive) — vacatur erases the convictions entirely, as if they never happened. Combined with the website scrubbing, the administration is systematically dismantling the legal and historical record of the Jan. 6 prosecutions.

**What to watch next:** Whether vacatur efforts extend to additional Jan. 6 defendants. The DOJ has indicated "similarly situated defendants" may also be covered.

## 14. Bottom Line

1. **The biggest story:** The GOP is in open civil war — Senate Republicans blocked their own party's signature immigration bill over Trump's DOJ fund. This is the most significant intraparty fracture since the 2025 budget shutdown. The outcome will define the rest of the legislative calendar.

2. **The biggest opportunity:** A US-Iran peace deal could reshape the Middle East, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, lower oil prices, and boost Trump's approval ahead of the midterms. But significant gaps remain, and hawks in both parties are skeptical.

3. **The biggest legal development:** The DOJ is systematically erasing the Jan. 6 prosecutions — first pardons, then vacated convictions, now the website scrubbing. The legal and historical record of the largest criminal investigation in DOJ history is being dismantled.

4. **What's solid:** Congress is in recess until June 1. The immigration bill and DOJ fund are the top items when they return. FISA expires June 12. Midterm polling consistently shows Democrats with a House advantage and the Senate as a toss-up.

5. **What's uncertain:** Whether a US-Iran deal materializes, whether Trump's physical reveals new health concerns, and whether Senate Republicans can find language to constrain the DOJ fund without losing conservative House support.
