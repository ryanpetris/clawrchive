## 2. White House and President

### Iran Deal Negotiations

**What happened:** Over the weekend, President Trump sent highly conflicting signals on a potential deal with Iran to end the war that began February 28. On Saturday, Trump posted that a "Memorandum of Understanding pertaining to PEACE" had been "largely negotiated," with final details to be "announced shortly." He held calls with leaders from Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Jordan, Egypt, and Bahrain, and separately with Israeli PM Netanyahu. On Sunday, Trump reversed course, posting that he had instructed negotiators "not to rush into a deal" and that "time is on our side." He dismissed critics as "losers" and said "it isn't even fully negotiated yet."

A senior administration official briefed reporters Sunday, laying out a two-step framework:
1. Iran opens the Strait of Hormuz → US lifts the naval blockade
2. Iran commits to giving up highly enriched uranium ("nuclear dust") → 30-day negotiation on mechanism
The official stressed a "no dust, no dollars" policy: "they don't get a dime unless they deliver on their commitments."

Meanwhile, Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said Monday the sides are "very close and very far," and flatly stated a deal "is not imminent." He confirmed progress on "a large portion of issues" but said "no one can make such a claim" that signing is imminent. Iran's demands reportedly include ending the war on all fronts (including Lebanon), releasing frozen assets, lifting the blockade, withdrawing US forces, and freedom to sell oil.

**Why it matters:** The Iran war has cost at least $25-29 billion and 13 American lives. The Strait of Hormuz closure has driven up global energy prices. Treasury Secretary Bessent has warned the US economic "shock absorbers" are nearly exhausted. Trump faces political pressure from both GOP hawks (Cruz, Wicker, Graham, Tillis) who say any deal short of unconditional surrender is a mistake, and from Democrats and a war-weary public who opposed the conflict from the start.

**What to watch next:** Whether an MOU is announced this week. Rubio said in New Delhi there's a chance of an announcement "later today, tomorrow, in a couple days." The Eid holiday ends Friday, which could be a target. Pakistani mediators are pressing for a resolution. Trump has also threatened to resume military strikes ("50/50" chance, he told Axios).

## 3. Congress: Senate

### GOP Reconciliation Bill in Crisis

**What happened:** The Senate GOP's $72B ICE/CBP party-line funding bill was derailed last week when Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) pulled it from the floor after a contentious Senate Republican Conference meeting with Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche. Over 20 GOP senators spoke out against the administration's $1.776B "anti-weaponization" fund. Blanche, Trump's former personal attorney, defended the fund despite warnings about political backlash.

Multiple GOP senators indicated they would vote for Democratic amendments targeting the fund during vote-a-rama, forcing Thune's hand. The fund would compensate people the DOJ deems victims of "lawfare and weaponization" — including GOP senators whose phone records were subpoenaed in the Arctic Frost investigation, and potentially January 6 defendants.

**Key Republican positions:**
- Sen. John Curtis (R-UT): Proposed restrictions on the fund would be "not enough."
- Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA): Called the delay "very smart," wants funding done "in the appropriate way."
- Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX): Confirmed on his podcast that more than 20 senators spoke during the meeting.
- Sen. Rick Scott (R-FL): Said he doesn't "need any compensation" — though his phone records were subpoenaed.
- Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI): Defended the fund's purpose but said he personally "would not apply."
- Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY): "Justice is when things occur that aren't different for you because you're in elected office."
- Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC): The most vocal advocate for payouts, has pushed for upwards of $500K per lawmaker.

**Ideas under discussion:** Imposing eligibility requirements (e.g., barring payouts to those convicted of assaulting police on Jan. 6); ensuring independent commission oversight; congressional confirmation of commissioners. Trump and the administration oppose any changes.

**Why it matters:** This is the most serious open breach between Trump and Senate Republicans since he took office. The reconciliation bill is a top legislative priority. Delays affect the rest of the GOP agenda, including the June 12 FISA deadline. Democrats have drafted 13 amendments targeting the fund and the White House ballroom.

**What to watch next:** Senate returns from Memorial Day recess June 1. Thune must find consensus language that passes muster with the parliamentarian, the House, and enough GOP senators. Some Republicans want the fund eliminated entirely.

## 6. Federal Agencies: Official Announcements

### Department of Homeland Security / USCIS

**Action:** USCIS memo dated May 21, publicly confirmed May 22/23, effectively ends "adjustment of status" for most green-card applicants. The policy requires most foreign nationals already living legally in the US to leave the country and apply for permanent residency from their home nations through consular processing — reversing a 50-year practice.

**Exceptions:** Narrow — approved refugees/asylees, and applicants who provide economic benefit or serve national interest (criteria undefined).

**Impact:** Over 1.2M people waiting for adjustment of status (Cato estimate), including 300K Cubans with frozen applications. Affects spouses/relatives of US citizens, professionals, students. Countries without functioning US consulates (Haiti, Venezuela, Russia) face impossible compliance.

**Why it matters:** One of the most sweeping changes to legal immigration in decades. Expected to face immediate litigation. The policy's broad language gives USCIS officers significant discretion.

**Source:** [Foreign Policy Journal](https://www.foreignpolicyjournal.com/2026/05/24/trump-administration-ends-half-century-green-card-tradition-forcing-applicants-to-return-home/); [Jefferson City News Tribune](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/may/24/trump-deals-major-blow-to-legal-migration-forcing/)

## 8. Courts and Legal/Regulatory Developments

### Presidential Records Act — Preliminary Injunction Against White House ⚖️

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (Judge John D. Bates)
**Cases:** *American Historical Association v. Trump* (No. 26-1169) and related *Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington v. Trump* (No. 26-1402)
**What happened:** On May 20, Judge Bates granted preliminary injunctions in two related cases, ordering the Executive Office of the President, White House Office, Office of the Vice President, and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles to:
1. Comply fully with the Presidential Records Act
2. Preserve all presidential/vice presidential records
3. Stop using Signal, WhatsApp, or other non-official messaging apps for presidential records unless copied to official accounts within 20 days
4. Establish compliant records retention policies

**Effective date:** 9:00 AM on May 26, 2026. Compliance report due May 28.

**Key legal finding:** The court rejected the administration's argument that the President has inherent authority to manage executive branch records contrary to the PRA. The OLC opinion underlying the 2026 Records Guidance "likely does not comply" with the PRA, and the President's Article II authority is "at its lowest ebb" when Congress has legislated on the matter.

**Why it matters:** Directly limits how the White House communicates. The court specifically named Signal, a widely reported communication tool among administration officials. The order applies government-wide to all "covered employees" under the PRA.

**Source:** Court orders [PDF - AHA case](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.291186/gov.uscourts.dcd.291186.23.0.pdf), [PDF - CREW case](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.291744/gov.uscourts.dcd.291744.14.0_2.pdf)

### FDA Mifepristone — Fifth Circuit Stay ⚖️

**Court:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit
**Case:** *Louisiana v. FDA* (No. 26-30203)
**What happened:** The Fifth Circuit granted Louisiana's motion to stay the 2023 REMS changes that allowed remote prescribing and mail dispensing of mifepristone. The panel (Southwick, Duncan, and a third judge) found Louisiana likely to succeed on its APA claim that FDA's justifications were "based on flawed or nonexistent data." The district court had found Louisiana likely to succeed but denied a stay on equitable grounds.

**Status:** Stay granted pending appeal. The 2023 REMS changes are frozen while the case proceeds.

**Why it matters:** Directly restricts access to medication abortion nationwide. The case challenges FDA's 2023 decision to eliminate in-person dispensing requirements. FDA had conceded it "failed to adequately study whether remotely prescribing mifepristone is safe."

**Source:** [Court document](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.lawd.213952/gov.uscourts.lawd.213952.263.0.pdf)

### DOJ Voter Roll Lawsuits — Two More Losses ⚖️

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the Western District of Wisconsin
**Case:** *United States v. Wisconsin Elections Commission* (No. 25-cv-1036)
**What happened:** On May 21, Judge James D. Peterson dismissed the DOJ's lawsuit seeking Wisconsin's unredacted voter registration list, ruling that a voter registration list is "not a record that can be demanded under Title III" of the Civil Rights Act of 1960. The court joined earlier rulings in *Benson* (W.D. Mich.) and *Fontes* (D. Ariz.) reaching the same conclusion. Dismissed with prejudice.

**While this is one of more than two dozen similar lawsuits,** the Wisconsin ruling is significant in joining a growing consensus against the DOJ's legal theory.

**Parallel:** On May 21, the U.S. District Court for the District of Maine also dismissed *United States v. Bellows*, rejecting the DOJ's demand for Maine's unredacted voter file. The court wrote that allowing such a construction of the Civil Rights Act "would take a sledgehammer to the balance Congress struck."

**Why it matters:** The Trump administration has filed over two dozen lawsuits nationwide seeking voter roll data. Courts are consistently rejecting the claim that Title III authorizes these demands. This represents a significant legal setback for the DOJ's voting-related enforcement strategy.

**Source:** [Court document - Wisconsin](https://cms.stateaffairs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/260521Voter.pdf); [ACLU - Maine](https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/federal-court-dismisses-doj-attempt-to-access-maines-unredacted-voter-file)

### ICE Courthouse Arrests — Partial Stay ⚖️

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York (Judge P. Kevin Castel)
**Case:** *The Door v. ICE* (No. 25-cv-06366)
**What happened:** On May 18, the court stayed the 2025 ICE Courthouse Arrest Policies as applied to immigration courts, after the government belatedly conceded the policies "do not and have never applied to civil immigration enforcement actions in or near immigration courthouses." The government had argued the opposite as recently as January 2026.

**Scope:** Applies to immigration courthouses in Manhattan (26 Federal Plaza, 201 Varick St., 290 Broadway).

**Source:** [NYCLU](https://www.nyclu.org/uploads/2025/08/2026-05-18-Opinion-And-Order-dckt-90_0.pdf)

### Anthropic v. Department of War — Preliminary Injunction ⚖️

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California
**Case:** *Anthropic PBC v. U.S. Department of War* (No. 26-cv-01996)
**What happened:** The court granted Anthropic's motion for a preliminary injunction against the Presidential Directive and Hegseth Directive that barred federal agencies from using Anthropic technology and designated the company a supply chain risk. The court found the directives were issued without cited statutory authority.

**Note:** This ruling appears separate from the classified NSA waiver reported May 25.
