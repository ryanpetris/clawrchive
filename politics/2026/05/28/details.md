## 2. White House And President

### Anti-Weaponization Fund sparks Senate revolt
- **What happened:** Republican senators — including close Trump allies like Tom Cotton (R-AR) and Ted Cruz (R-TX) — joined a bipartisan backlash against the $1.776B "Anti-Weaponization Fund" created by Trump's settlement of his IRS lawsuit. At a hastily organized meeting near the Senate chamber, "at least half" of the ~45 attending senators blasted Acting AG Todd Blanche. Cruz described it as "one of the roughest meetings I've seen in my entire time in the Senate" with senators "screaming at Blanche." (Source: [WSJ via tovima.com](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-week-that-broke-trumps-control-over-congress/), [Reason](https://reason.com/2026/05/27/even-republicans-are-rebelling-at-trumps-blatantly-corrupt-anti-weaponization-fund/))
- **Why it matters:** The fund has paralyzed the $70B immigration enforcement bill — a top GOP priority that was supposed to pass easily. Critics including Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY) called it "a slush fund to pay people who assault cops." Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) called it "stupid on stilts" and a "payout pot for punks." The fund would be controlled by a 5-member board appointed by the AG, removable by the president without cause, and stops processing claims 45 days before Trump leaves office.
- **Trump's response:** He said he "could have settled my case for an absolute fortune" and is "helping others." He lashed out at Tillis, calling him a RINO. Trump's social media post said the fund delivers "JUSTICE" to those "badly abused by an evil, corrupt, and weaponized Biden Administration." (Source: [Reason](https://reason.com/2026/05/27/even-republicans-are-rebelling-at-trumps-blatantly-corrupt-anti-weaponization-fund/))
- **What to watch next:** Cruz predicted the administration will modify the fund or face "a full-on revolt." Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) said the administration "is going to have to come up with some suggestions and ideas." (Source: [WSJ via tovima.com](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-week-that-broke-trumps-control-over-congress/))
- **Policy or political implications:** This marks the most significant break between Senate Republicans and Trump since his second term began. The fight has delayed action ahead of Trump's June 1 deadline. Senators wanted the immigration bill passed last week but were "forced to punt until next month." (Source: [ABC News / AP](https://abcnews.com/US/wireStory/republicans-recent-stumbles-congress-highlight-difficult-road-ahead-133376435))

### Iran deal talks
- **What happened:** Trump's public messaging on a potential Iran peace deal has been contradictory. On May 23 he declared a deal "largely negotiated." On May 24 he walked it back, saying it's not "even fully negotiated yet." On May 27, at a cabinet meeting, he said Iran "wants very much to make a deal" but the US is "not satisfied." Iranian state TV published what it claimed was a 14-point draft MOU; the White House called it a "complete fabrication." (Sources: [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/23/middleeast/iran-us-progress-framework-diplomacy-intl), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/white-house/deal-no-deal-trumps-social-media-posts-add-confusion-iran-conflict-rcna346953), [ABC Australia](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-28/iran-war-us-draft-peace-deal-memorandum-hormuz-blockade/106730174))
- **Why it matters:** The Iran war has caused the worst global energy crisis in decades. The Strait of Hormuz, through which ~20% of global oil and gas flows, has been effectively closed since February. Oil prices have fallen and stocks rose on deal prospects. Higher energy prices have directly contributed to Trump's record-low approval on prices. (Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/23/us-iran-war-talks.html))
- **What to watch next:** Whether a framework agreement can be finalized. The potential two-phase deal would reopen the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping and end the US naval blockade, with broader talks within 30-60 days.

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Biden v. DOJ (D.D.C.)
- **Parties:** Former President Joe Biden (plaintiff) v. Department of Justice (defendant); also Biden v. Heritage Foundation (separate case)
- **What happened:** Biden filed suit Tuesday night seeking to block DOJ from releasing audio recordings and transcripts of 2016-2017 conversations with his memoir ghostwriter Mark Zwonitzer. The materials were obtained during the 2023-2024 classified documents investigation by Special Counsel Robert Hur, which resulted in no charges. (Source: [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/27/politics/biden-sues-to-stop-justice-department-from-releasing-interview-recordings), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/27/why-biden-is-suing-the-department-of-justice-trump-response-lawsuit/))
- **Legal status:** Fresh filing. Biden argues the DOJ under Trump "reversed" its earlier position that materials were exempt from disclosure "without any formal explanation." The DOJ has agreed to let Biden argue against Heritage's access.
- **Next step:** Court will consider whether to issue a temporary restraining order or injunction. Release is currently scheduled for June 15.

### Trump v. IRS settlement — Judge's dismissal order
- **What happened:** Judge Kathleen Williams dismissed Trump's $10B lawsuit against the IRS. Her order noted that because Trump's dropping of the suit "does not reference any settlement or include a stipulation of settlement, there is no settlement of record." Despite this, the DOJ and Trump proceeded with a purported settlement creating the $1.776B Anti-Weaponization Fund. (Source: [The Hill](https://thehill.com/opinion/white-house/5895817-trump-settlement-corruption-analysis/))
- **Policy or political impact:** Legal experts have described the arrangement as potentially unprecedented in U.S. history. An addendum to the purported settlement includes a general release barring the DOJ from pursuing audits of Trump or affiliates.

### Alabama redistricting — federal court ruling
- **What happened:** A federal court blocked a Republican-backed congressional map in Alabama the same day the SC Senate rejected Trump's redistricting push. (Source: [PBS News / AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/south-carolina-senate-rejects-trumps-push-to-redraw-congressional-maps))
- **Why it matters:** Republican efforts to leverage a recent Supreme Court Voting Rights Act ruling are facing judicial pushback alongside state-level legislative defeats.
