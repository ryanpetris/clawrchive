## 2. White House And President

### Trump Endorses $250 Bill with His Face

**What happened:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed at a White House press briefing Thursday that the administration is preparing designs for a $250 bill featuring President Trump's portrait. Current law prohibits living persons from appearing on U.S. currency (since 1866). Bessent said legislation has been proposed in the House and Senate to change that requirement. Treasury is "preparing things in advance" so if legislation passes, the design is ready.

**Why it matters:** No living person has appeared on U.S. currency in over 160 years. The proposal is unprecedented and has drawn bipartisan criticism. CNN's Kaitlan Collins pressed Bessent on whether it's politically wise to push this symbolically charged initiative while Americans are struggling with gas prices. The Washington Post reported that US Treasurer Brandon Beach and senior adviser Mike Brown have been pushing Bureau of Engraving and Printing workers to produce prototypes, despite the legal prohibition.

**Policy or political implications:** The $250 bill proposal is primarily symbolic and political rather than practical economic policy. It reinforces Trump's brand-focused approach to the presidency and tests norms around the separation of political and governmental institutions. Bessent's public defense suggests the White House sees it as a net positive political message, though the CNN confrontation indicates it may backfire with voters feeling economic strain.

**What to watch next:** Whether legislation advances in Congress. The proposal faces a very uncertain path given narrow Republican majorities and potential opposition from fiscal conservatives and institutionalists.

**Source:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-28/bessent-defends-proposal-for-trump-s-face-on-special-250-bill), [Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/cnns-kaitlan-collins-confronts-bessent-is-it-a-good-idea-politically-to-put-trumps-face-on-250-bill-when-people-are-struggling-to-afford-gas/)

### Trump Cabinet Meeting / Iran Comments

**What happened:** At a Cabinet meeting Wednesday, Trump expressed confidence that a deal to end the Iran conflict is within reach while insisting midterm election pressures won't rush him. He said Iran is "negotiating on fumes" and "they want very much to make a deal." However, he acknowledged "we're not satisfied with it, but we will be — either that or we'll have to just finish the job."

**Why it matters:** The Iran war — now approaching three months — remains a political vulnerability for Trump and Republicans. Yale Youth Poll data shows the Iran war is a top concern for young voters, and some young men who voted for Trump in 2024 are expressing disillusionment.

**Policy or political implications:** Trump faces a cross-pressure: hawkish Republicans (Wicker, Graham, Cruz) say the emerging deal terms are too favorable to Iran, while the broader electorate is war-weary with gas price sensitivity. Deal terms reportedly include Iran giving up highly enriched uranium in exchange for sanctions relief, with a 60-day implementation period.

**What to watch next:** Whether negotiators can resolve the uranium custody issue (Trump rejected Russia or China as custodians) and whether the ceasefire covers Israeli operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon as Iran demands.

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Mail-In Voting Executive Order — Judge Nichols Decision

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (Judge Carl Nichols, Trump appointee)
**Parties:** Democratic congressional leaders and civil rights groups v. Trump administration
**What happened:** Judge Nichols denied a motion for preliminary injunction to block Trump's March 31 executive order on mail-in voting. He ruled the challenge is premature because the order has not been implemented — DHS has not compiled citizenship lists and USPS has not issued rules.
**Legal status:** Challenge denied without prejudice. Plaintiffs can renew if implementation begins.
**Policy or political impact:** The ruling allows the administration to continue developing the voter list system. It does not validate the order's constitutionality. A separate challenge in Massachusetts (Judge Indira Talwani, Obama appointee) has a June 2 hearing.
**Next deadline:** June 2, 2026 — Boston hearing on similar challenge.
**Source:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-28/democrats-lose-court-bid-to-block-trump-s-mail-in-ballot-limits), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/28/us-judge-rejects-immediate-block-on-trumps-mail-in-voting-executive-order)

### Anti-Weaponization Fund — 35 Former Judges Motion

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida (Judge Kathleen Williams)
**Parties:** 35 former federal judges (not parties to original suit — filed as interested parties seeking to reopen)
**What happened:** 35 former federal judges, including prominent conservative J. Michael Luttig, filed a motion asking Judge Williams to reopen the dismissed Trump-IRS lawsuit and investigate whether the $1.776 billion settlement was a "fraud on the Court." They argue the case was collusive — Trump sued his own administration, then settled with his own DOJ to create a fund with minimal guardrails — and that the Court was "deceived."
**Legal status:** Motion pending. Judge Williams had previously signaled concern about whether there was a genuine "case or controversy."
**Policy or political impact:** If the judge grants the motion, it could unravel the entire settlement and the fund. Bipartisan criticism of the fund continues; two Capitol Police officers have separately sued to block it.
**Next deadline:** Awaiting Judge Williams' ruling on the motion.
**Source:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/dozens-of-ex-judges-push-look-into-trump-anti-weaponization-fund-fraud-on-the-court/), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/05/27/former-federal-judges-trump-irs-weaponization-fund/90283762007/)

**Parties:** Former President Joe Biden v. Department of Justice (and intervenors House Judiciary Committee, Heritage Foundation)
**What happened:** Biden sued to block the DOJ from releasing audio recordings and transcripts of his conversations with ghostwriter Mark Zwonitzer from the classified documents investigation. DOJ has said it will release the materials on June 15 unless blocked by court order.

Biden argues the recordings contain deeply personal discussions about his son Beau's death that deserve protection. **Next deadline:** June 15, 2026 — current DOJ target release date unless court intervenes.

## 14. Bottom Line

1. **The administration's legal strategy of acting now, litigating later** is working for now on the voting EO, but the constitutional question remains unresolved and a Boston challenge looms June 2.

2. **The DOJ's pursuit of Trump's perceived adversaries** continues to escalate with the Carroll criminal investigation, but past efforts have stalled in courts and grand juries — this one may too.

3. **The anti-weaponization fund is emerging as a major legal and political vulnerability** for the White House. Bipartisan former judges, Capitol Police officers, and Democratic-led lawsuits all threaten it simultaneously.

4. **The Iran war defines the midterm landscape** more than any domestic policy fight — it affects gas prices, young voter sentiment, GOP unity, and Trump's approval. A deal before November would reshape the political environment; failure to secure one would keep a major vulnerability exposed.

5. **Ethics questions are accumulating** — the $250 bill branding, Dell contract proximity to Trump's finances, Philip Alito's Treasury job, the anti-weaponization fund's lack of guardrails — producing a cumulative narrative that may become difficult for the administration to manage in a midterm environment.
