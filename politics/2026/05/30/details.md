## 2. White House And President

### Anti-Weaponization Fund Suffers Dual Legal Blows

**What happened:** The Trump administration's $1.776 billion "Anti-Weaponization Fund" was hit by two federal court rulings on Friday:

1. **Temporary restraining order:** U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema (E.D. Va.) blocked the Justice Department from forming the fund, transferring money into it, considering claims, or disbursing any funds for at least two weeks. A June 12 hearing will determine whether to extend the order while litigation proceeds. ([Source](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2026/may/30/us-judge-blocks-payouts-from-trumps-18b-fund/))

2. **Fraud investigation:** U.S. District Judge Kathleen Williams (S.D. Fla.) reopened Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the IRS — which Trump had voluntarily dismissed — and ordered his attorneys to respond by June 12 to allegations that the dismissal and settlement that created the fund "was premised on deception." The order was prompted by a filing from 35 former federal judges who argued the case was "collusive from the start" and that the court was the "victim of a fraud." ([Source](https://www.dailyherald.com/20260529/nation-and-world-politics/judge-probes-whether-deal-creating-trumps-1-8-billion-fund-constitutes-fraud/))

**Why it matters:** The fund is the legal foundation for Trump's claim to compensate allies who say they were victims of a "weaponized" Biden administration. If both judges' orders hold — and if Williams determines the IRS lawsuit was collusive — the entire fund could be dismantled. The orders also threaten the audit protection Trump and his family received as part of the deal.

**What to watch:** June 12 is the key date — both the Brinkema extension hearing and the Williams response deadline. The DOJ has said it is "extremely confident" in the fund's legality, citing Obama-era settlement precedent.

**Source:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/senate-republicans-face-political-knife-edge-over-trumps-anti-weaponization-fund-2026-05-30/), [The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/judge-cries-deception-to-blow-up-donald-trumps-wild-18b-grift/), [NYT via DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/30/judge-reopens-trumps-i-r-s-suit-and-questions-his-weaponization-fund/)

### Executive Order: Childhood Vaccine Realignment

**What happened:** Trump signed an executive order directing the CDC and its Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) to align with the January 2026 HHS scientific assessment recommending fewer childhood vaccines. The HHS assessment had found the U.S. recommends 17 vaccines universally for children versus 11 in the new framework. The order directs agencies to "provide maximum flexibility to parents and doctors." ([Source](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-executive-order-cdc-childhood-vaccines/))

**Why it matters:** This represents a significant policy shift driven by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a longtime vaccine skeptic. The White House fact sheet frames it as "gold-standard science" and "empowering patients and doctors." However, a federal judge in Massachusetts already blocked a prior attempt to implement narrower CDC recommendations, finding Kennedy's replacement of the ACIP panel violated federal law. The administration is appealing that decision.

**What to watch:** The American Academy of Pediatrics and other medical organizations are expected to continue legal challenges. States control school vaccination requirements, and some have formed multistate alliances to counter federal guidance.

**Sources:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-executive-order-cdc-childhood-vaccines/), [AP](https://kfor.com/news/health/ap-health/ap-trump-tells-agencies-to-align-with-study-calling-for-narrower-childhood-vaccine-recommendations/), [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/05/realigning-united-states-core-childhood-vaccine-recommendations-with-best-practices-from-peer-developed-countries/)

### Kennedy Center: Judge Orders Trump's Name Removed

**What happened:** U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper (D.D.C.) ruled that the Kennedy Center board "overstepped its statutory bounds" by adding Trump's name to the venue. He ordered all signage and official materials bearing "Trump Kennedy Center" to be removed within 14 days. He also blocked the planned two-year closure for renovations, finding the board's closure vote was "ill-informed and seemingly preordained." Trump responded on Truth Social by saying he would "transfer" the center to Congress and "cannot be involved with a situation where danger to the Public is allowed to flourish." ([Source](https://www.informnny.com/entertainment-news/ap-judge-says-kennedy-center-board-broke-law-putting-trumps-name-on-building-blocks-closure/))

**Why it matters:** This is the latest in a string of court losses for Trump's efforts to reshape Washington's monumental core — the East Wing ballroom, the proposed 250-foot arch, the renaming of federal buildings. Cooper's ruling reaffirms that only Congress can change the Kennedy Center's name, and that the board cannot unilaterally close it.

**What to watch:** Trump claimed he's instructing the Commerce Department to arrange a transfer. The mechanism for such a transfer is legally unclear. The Kennedy Center board could still vote to close for renovations if it reconsiders independently, per Cooper's ruling.

**Sources:** [AP](https://www.informnny.com/entertainment-news/ap-judge-says-kennedy-center-board-broke-law-putting-trumps-name-on-building-blocks-closure/), [Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/05/30/trump-says-he-will-039transfer039-kennedy-center-to-congress-after-court-setback)

**What to watch:** Trump's red lines include Iran never developing nuclear weapons and reopening the Strait of Hormuz (with Iran removing mines and ending tolls, while the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports). The next 24-48 hours could produce a decision.

### The Political Knife-Edge: Anti-Weaponization Fund and Immigration Bill

**What happened:** Senate Republicans face their most consequential test since returning from recess: how to handle the $1.8B anti-weaponization fund that has become entangled with a $71B reconciliation bill to fund ICE and Border Patrol. Majority Leader John Thune has called on DOJ and the White House to provide guardrails in writing, but aides say there has been "only silence." ([Source](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/senate-republicans-face-political-knife-edge-over-trumps-anti-weaponization-fund-2026-05-30/))

**Key demands from Senate Republicans:**
- Eligibility requirements in writing (Blanche assured privately that no Trump family members or violent-crime convicts would be eligible)
- Greater congressional control in selecting fund commissioners
- Some form of judicial oversight

**Why it matters:** Democrats are expected to force repeated amendment votes designed to embarrass Republicans. The fund dispute has already blown past Trump's original June 1 deadline for the immigration package. Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley has called for congressional oversight.

**Senators involved:** Sen. John Thune (R-SD), Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA), and numerous others who are publicly uneasy.

### FISA Section 702 Reauthorization Looming

The FISA Section 702 surveillance authority expires June 12 — a hard deadline, unlike Trump's immigration bill target. Congress failed to reauthorize it before the recess. The debate pits privacy advocates (uniting conservative Republicans and progressive Democrats) against intelligence agencies warning that warrant requirements could slow investigations. ## 7. Federal Agencies: News And Reported Developments

### DHS/ICE: Mega-Warehouses Under Review

**What's being reported:** The Trump administration is considering selling several of the 11 large warehouses ICE purchased under former Secretary Kristi Noem, which were intended to serve as mega-detention centers holding up to 8,000 immigrants each. ICE is also considering selling deportation aircraft purchased under Noem, including a luxury Boeing 737 Max 8. Total cost of the warehouse purchases was estimated at over $38 billion. ([Source](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/ice-selling-mega-warehouses-purchased-for-mass-detention/6507068/))

**Confirmed/Partially confirmed:** DHS confirmed the review. A spokesperson said "Secretary Mullin is 100% focused on ensuring the needs of our department are met while being the best possible steward of taxpayer dollars." No final decisions made.

**Why it matters:** This represents a significant cultural shift at DHS since Markwayne Mullin took over. Maryland successfully sued to block one warehouse in Hagerstown. Social Circle, Georgia sued alleging ICE paid 5x assessed value. The DHS IG is auditing whether purchases were "cost-effective."

**What to watch:** Whether ICE actually follows through on selling assets, or stays the course. The Social Circle lawsuit and IG audit could produce damaging findings.

### White House: "Aliens.gov" Website Launch

**What's being reported:** The White House launched Aliens.gov, a space-themed website touting immigration arrest numbers. The site plays on the dual meaning of "alien" (extraterrestrial and undocumented immigrant), listing arrest data by city with charges including "immigration" and "general crimes." ([Source](https://www.the-independent.com/news/world/americas/us-politics/alien-website-immigrants-white-house-b2985987.html))

**Confirmed:** The website is live. The administration had previously teased UFO disclosure.

**Why it matters:** The provocative branding signals the administration intends to keep immigration enforcement visible and political, even as DHS reassesses detention capacity.

## 8. Courts And Legal Or Regulatory Developments

### Mail Voting Executive Order: TRO Denied

**What happened:** U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols (Trump appointee, D.D.C.) declined to halt Trump's executive order creating a federal voter list and limiting mail voting. He agreed with the administration that it was too early to block the order because it hasn't been implemented yet. ([Source](https://www.dailypress.net/news/2026/05/judge-refuses-to-block-trump-order-to-limit-mail-voting-theres-no-immediate-effect-on-the-midterms/))

**Legal status:** The ruling leaves the door open for later challenges when the administration moves to implement. A separate lawsuit is pending in Boston.

**Why it matters:** The order, issued in March after a voting bill stalled in Congress, would direct USPS to deliver mail ballots only to those on the federal list. Election officials say it could cause chaos. No voting changes expected during primary season.

**What to watch:** The Boston lawsuit and any implementation steps by the administration.

### Louisiana Congressional Map

**What happened:** The Louisiana Senate voted 28-10 to pass a new congressional map eliminating one of the state's two majority-Black districts. Gov. Jeff Landry is expected to sign it. The ACLU of Louisiana called it a "racial gerrymander hiding behind the thin veneer of partisanship." ([Source](https://wtxnews.com/louisiana-lawmakers-approve-congressional-map-that-favours-republicans/))

**Legal status:** Follows the Supreme Court's April 30 ruling in *Louisiana v. Callais* striking down the prior map. More litigation is expected from voting rights groups.

**Why it matters:** Republicans are gaining seats through redistricting in multiple Southern states after the weakened Voting Rights Act ruling. Nationally, GOP sees a potential 15-seat gain from redistricting; Democrats see 6 seats from California and Utah.

### Wisconsin Redistricting

**What happened:** The liberal-controlled Wisconsin Supreme Court agreed to hear an appeal seeking to redraw the state's Republican-friendly congressional districts, where Republicans hold 6 of 8 seats. A ruling is not expected in time for the 2026 election but could affect 2028.
