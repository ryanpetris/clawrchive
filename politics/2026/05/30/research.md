## Anti-Weaponization Fund — Deeper Analysis

### Legal Theories at Play
The fund faces challenges on multiple legal fronts:

1. **EDVA (Brinkema)** - Temporary restraining order based on likelihood of success on merits that Judgment Fund cannot be used for purposes Congress did not appropriate. Plaintiffs are fired prosecutor and college professor acquitted of assault charges.

2. **SDFL (Williams)** - Fraud-on-the-court investigation under Rule 60(b) and court's inherent authority. Key question: Was the IRS lawsuit collusive from the start? Williams had already questioned whether parties were "sufficiently adverse" before dismissal.

3. **Congressional** - Republicans want guardrails in writing. Thune has requested DOJ/WH clarity. Silence from DOJ so far adds to tension.

### The IRS Defense Memo Detail
The NYT reported that IRS prepared a 25-page memorandum outlining defenses against Trump's $10B suit that DOJ never used. Potential defenses included:
- Statute of limitations (suit filed years after 2020 leak)
- Fault lies with contractor (Booz Allen employee Charles Littlejohn), not IRS itself
- No government attorney entered appearance in the case

### Audit Protection Scope
Judge Williams flagged in a footnote that the audit protection provision:
- Bars IRS from pursuing unpaid tax claims arising before the deal
- Was signed only by Todd Blanche, not countersigned by IRS counsel
- May violate DOJ settlement rules requiring direct relation to suit issues

### 35 Former Federal Judges
The bipartisan group of 35 former federal judges who filed the brief urging investigation is notable. Their argument: the settlement was "not, and never will be, legally justified" because the Judgment Fund and AG settlement authority "require the existence of a legitimate litigation and not, as here, one that is collusive, feigned, or fraudulent."
