## AI Infrastructure & Capex Cycle

- **The "depreciation wall" thesis:** Morgan Stanley highlights that big tech is in a "golden window" where revenue is climbing but the true cost of AI chips hasn't fully hit earnings yet. When depreciation from massive capex shows up on income statements, earnings could compress, and the Meta/Amazon/etc capex spigots could slow.
- **Nvidia's $1 trillion backlog:** Jensen Huang says order backlog could hit $1T by year-end. Even if true, stock prices can re-rate before orders are fulfilled.
- **GPU rental pricing is the leading indicator:** B200 rental costs fell ~31% in 3 weeks. Not a structural demand bust — likely temporary oversupply as hyperscalers bring capacity online faster than projects can absorb it. But worth monitoring weekly.
- **Custom silicon threat:** OpenAI's "Jalapeño" chip (Broadcom + TSMC 3nm) specifically targets inference cost reduction. This segment is where NVDA has been most dominant, and also where the industry is growing fastest. Broadcom's custom XPU business with Google is already showing results.
- **AMD's positioning:** The MEXT acquisition (memory optimization + AI) combined with ZT Systems acquisition creates an end-to-end inference server story. Two $100B GPU deals. Agentic AI could require 1:1 GPU:CPU ratio (vs 8:1 for training), playing to AMD's CPU strength.

## Memory & Costs

- **DRAM/NAND costs have more than quadrupled** over the last year (TechInsights data). Microsoft says component costs are up 2.5x since January and could double again in 12 months.
- **Micron's strategic customer agreements:** Multi-year take-or-pay contracts provide revenue visibility. The Anthropic agreement (June 22) and $100B in take-or-pay commitments suggest memory demand is locked in.
- **TSMC's price hikes compound the problem.** 5-10% across all advanced nodes. This affects NVDA, AMD, AAPL, QCOM, and AVGO's manufacturing costs on top of memory costs.
- **Downstream implications:** Apple was the first to pass through costs. Microsoft followed with Xbox. If consumer demand softens in response, the cycle could stall. If demand holds, margins at memory/semi suppliers continue to expand.
