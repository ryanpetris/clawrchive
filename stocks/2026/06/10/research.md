# Research Notes - June 10, 2026

## Deep dive on the SpaceX IPO and tech liquidity

The "SpaceX liquidity squeeze" narrative is gaining serious traction. Let's question it a bit:

**For the theory:**
- $250B in indications of interest (IOIs) — that's real money.
- The Nasdaq had its worst session of 2026 last Friday (roughly -4%). The only "event" that week that can explain such a coordinated selloff is the SpaceX roadshow news breaking.
- Crypto has shed ~$180B in market cap in the same period. Bitwise adviser Jeff Park specifically argued bitcoin was being sold to fund SpaceX and other AI/space deals.
- Strategy (MSTR) chairman Michael Saylor estimated markets soaked up ~$400B over six months for AI buildouts.

**Against the theory:**
- The Broadcom AI outlook disappointment and strong jobs data are sufficient to explain most of the move.
- Institutional demand for IPO (IOIs) doesn't equal immediate cash outflows — allocations happen at pricing.
- Some of the selling is genuine: AI valuations are stretched, and a 40%+ weight of AI in the market (as noted by Geiger Capital) means any rotation hits hard.

**Conclusion:** The squeeze is real, but it's amplifying fundamentals-driven selling, not causing it.

## CPO (Co-Packaged Optics) analysis

SemiAnalysis published a report suggesting CPO production will significantly lag market expectations. This is important because:
- CPO is seen as the next evolution in datacenter interconnect — reducing power consumption and latency.
- If CPO is delayed, the current generation of optical transceivers (and related supply chain) stays relevant longer.
- Companies with CPO exposure: NVDA (has CPO in future roadmaps), MRVL (silicon photonics), LITE, etc.
- The A-share CPO index fell 2.83% on the news, confirming the cross-border impact.

**Verdict:** This is a real concern for the supply chain thesis but likely a near-term trading excuse rather than a structural impairment for AI infrastructure spending.

## Micron (MU) valuation analysis

GuruFocus's GF Value analysis flagged MU as 152.8% overvalued at $935.89 vs GF Value of $370.19. But...
- GF Value is a proprietary metric combining historical multiples, analyst estimates, and past returns.
- The memory cycle is structurally different now — HBM demand creates a secular growth driver that historical multiples don't capture.
- P/E of 44.19x is above the 5-year median of 20.72x, but HBM-driven earnings growth could compress that rapidly.
- Insider selling of $92.5M over 3 months is a legitimate concern.

**Verdict:** MU is expensive on historical metrics, but the HBM thesis changes the comp. The stock is vulnerable to sentiment shifts (like CPO concerns) but not necessarily structurally overvalued.

## Salesforce (CRM) — deeper look

The market is asking whether AI makes CRM software more or less valuable. Salesforce's response: Agentforce at $1B run rate. The argument:
- **Bear case:** AI agents reduce the need for human CRM users → fewer seats → lower revenues.
- **Bull case:** AI agents increase the value of CRM platforms → higher pricing per user/agent → higher revenues.
- The market is pricing the bear case aggressively. CRM at $175.35 (avg PT $254) implies 45% upside if the bull case wins.
- The 86 layoffs are small (~0.1% of workforce) but symbolic of restructuring around AI.

**Verdict:** The market is pricing a worst-case scenario that is not guaranteed. CRM is interesting as a contrarian play if you believe AI enhances rather than destroys enterprise software value.
