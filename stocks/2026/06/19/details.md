## 2. Week Ahead Summary

**Expected Market Tone:** Event-driven, semiconductor-led, with macro crosscurrents from Fed rate-hike risk and Iran deal follow-through.

**Major themes expected to drive tech stocks this week:**

1. **Micron Earnings (Wednesday, June 24):** The most important tech catalyst of the week. As the leading high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supplier for AI accelerators, Micron's quarter and guidance will serve as a pulse check for the entire AI hardware cycle. The stock has more than tripled this year. Revenue is expected to be a record.

2. **Iran Deal Execution Risk:** While the interim peace framework sent oil prices sharply lower (a tailwind for tech multiples), the postponement of nuclear talks introduces uncertainty. Any setback could push oil back up, re-stoking inflation fears and rate-hike expectations.

3. **Fed Hawkish Aftermath:** The June FOMC held rates at 3.50%–3.75%, but nine of 19 policymakers projected a rate hike before year-end. Markets price ~50% chance of a 25bp hike in September. Warsh's debut sent a distinctly hawkish message, and this week's housing, durable goods, and PCE data will be scrutinized for inflation signals.

4. **Intel-Apple Chip Deal Ripple Effects:** Trump's announcement that Intel will design and build chips for Apple in the U.S. sent INTC up 10.6% on Thursday. The implications for Intel's foundry turnaround, Apple's supply chain, and the broader domestic semiconductor ecosystem will be analyzed all week.

5. **Semiconductor Resilience vs. Profit-Taking Risk:** The SOX surged 6.4% Thursday but had a volatile week. The group remains the market's leadership cohort but is vulnerable to profit-taking after its near-doubling since January.

6. **Key Economic Data:** May new home sales (Wed June 24), durable goods orders (Thu June 25), and the PCE price index (Thu June 25) — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge.

**Highest-conviction catalysts for the week:** Micron earnings, PCE inflation data, Iran nuclear talks progress.

## 4. Earnings Preview

### Micron Technology (MU) — Q3 FY2026 — Reports Wednesday, June 24 (after close)

**Consensus Expectations:**
- Revenue: Record levels (company previously guided for record revenue)
- EPS: Significant year-over-year growth expected
- Segment: DRAM and NAND both benefiting from AI-driven HBM demand

**Key Metrics to Watch:**
- High-bandwidth memory (HBM) revenue and margin
- Data center vs. consumer mix
- DRAM and NAND pricing trends for FQ4 guidance
- CapEx plans for additional HBM capacity

**What the Market Expects:**
- Strong AI-related demand for HBM3E and next-gen memory
- Tight supply conditions supporting pricing power
- Guidance that continues to reflect HBM supply constraints through H2 2026

**What Would Be Considered a Beat or Miss:**
- **Beat:** Higher-than-expected HBM revenue, raised FQ4 guidance, positive commentary on 2027 HBM pipeline
- **Miss:** Signs of DRAM pricing softening, HBM yield issues, cautious enterprise/customer commentary
- **Key risk:** The stock has tripled this year — expectations are elevated. Some analysts flag MU as overvalued at current levels (source: [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/wall-street-buy-nvidia-sell-micron-ai))

**Read-Throughs:**
- Bullish MU → positive for NVDA, AMD, AVGO, MRVL, SMCI (confirms AI demand cycle intact)
- Bearish MU → negative across AI semiconductor complex, particularly HBM-dependent names
- Memory pricing signals also affect WDC, STX, and the broader SOX index

## 5. Sector Outlook

### Semiconductors (SOX, SMH)
- **Bull case:** AI-driven demand for GPUs, HBM, and networking silicon remains insatiable. The Intel-Apple deal validates U.S. onshoring thesis. MU earnings could catalyze another leg higher.
- **Bear case:** The sector has nearly doubled YTD. Profit-taking risk is elevated. Fed rate-hike risk compresses growth-stock multiples. Export-control uncertainty (ASML/China) adds headline risk.
- **Key tickers:** NVDA, AMD, INTC, AVGO, MU, MRVL, QCOM, ASML
- **Likely catalysts:** MU earnings, COMPUTEX announcements, Iran deal follow-through

### AI Infrastructure (SMCI, DELL, HPE, ANET, VRT)
- **Bull case:** Hyperscaler CapEx remains elevated. Power and cooling infrastructure demand is accelerating. COMPUTEX keynotes reinforce the AI infrastructure buildout theme.
- **Bear case:** Valuations are extended. Any CapEx slowdown signal from hyperscalers would hit these names hard. Supply chain normalization could compress margins.
- **Key tickers:** SMCI, DELL, HPE, ANET, VRT, GE Vernova
- **Likely catalysts:** MU earnings read-through, durable goods orders (CapEx proxy)

### Mega-Cap Platforms (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META)
- **Bull case:** The Intel-Apple deal could strengthen AAPL's domestic supply chain narrative. MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META continue competing aggressively for AI capacity, driving data center CapEx. All five are cash-flow machines resilient to rate pressure.
- **Bear case:** Extended multiples. Anti-trust and regulatory risk (DOJ cases against AAPL, GOOGL). Fed hawkishness could trigger rotation out of mega-cap growth.
- **Key tickers:** AAPL (Intel deal read-through), MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META
- **Likely catalysts:** MU earnings (demand read-through), PCE data, regulatory headlines

### Cloud and Software (ORCL, CRM, ADBE, NOW, SNOW, DDOG)
- **Bull case:** AI workloads driving cloud migration acceleration. Enterprise AI adoption remains early-cycle.
- **Bear case:** Accenture's ~18% drop Thursday on cautious consulting demand is a warning for enterprise IT spend. Budgets are tightening for non-AI projects. Software valuations remain elevated.
- **Key tickers:** ORCL, CRM, NOW, SNOW, DDOG, ACN (watch for further IT services weakness)
- **Likely catalysts:** MU earnings (AI spend read-through), PCE data

### Cybersecurity (PANW, CRWD)
- **Bull case:** Persistent threat environment and AI-driven security demand. Spending remains a priority even in budget-constrained environments.
- **Bear case:** Elevated multiples; crowded positioning.
- **Key tickers:** PANW, CRWD
- **Likely catalysts:** Earnings reports still weeks away; sector may trade on macro

### Consumer Hardware
- **Bull case:** AI PC refresh cycle starting. Intel-Apple deal could accelerate U.S. chip production for consumer devices.
- **Bear case:** Consumer spending pressure from inflation and rates.
- **Key ticker:** AAPL (watch for Intel deal details)

### EV and Autonomy (TSLA)
- **Bull case:** Energy storage and AI/autonomy narrative. Lower oil prices reduce input costs.
- **Bear case:** Competition intensifying. Demand softening in key markets. Valuation disconnected from auto fundamentals.
- **Key ticker:** TSLA
- **Likely catalysts:** Delivery numbers (early July), AI/robotaxi event updates

### Tech ETFs

- **XLK (Technology Select Sector SPDR):** Weaker breadth than QQQ recently; underperforming.

## 9. Final Weekly Outlook

**Best-looking setups:**
- **MU** — earnings catalyst with strong fundamental tailwinds. Short-term positioning risk exists, but HBM demand remains structurally bullish.
- **INTC** — the Apple deal represents a genuine inflection point for the foundry narrative, though execution risk is high.
- **Semiconductor ETF (SMH/SOXX)** — AI demand theme remains intact; dips have been bought consistently.

**Biggest risks:**
- **PCE inflation data (Thu June 25):** A hot print would reinforce September rate-hike expectations and hit growth stocks hard.
- **Iran deal execution:** Nuclear talks postponement introduces downside risk for oil-sensitive market positioning.
- **MU "sell the news":** The stock has tripled. Even a solid report could lead to profit-taking if guidance doesn't blow away elevated expectations.
- **Fed narrative:** Warsh's hawkish tone could keep a lid on valuations even with positive earnings.

**Setup for the first market open (Monday, June 22):**
- Futures were slightly lower late Thursday after-hours as Iran nuclear talks faced postponement.
- Asian markets mixed on Friday in thin holiday trading.
- Expect a relatively quiet open as traders ease back from the long weekend and position for Wednesday's MU earnings and Thursday's PCE data.
- Key levels: S&P 500 at 7,500; Nasdaq at 26,518.

**Key data or events that could change the outlook:**
- **Hot PCE print:** Would raise September rate-hike probability above 50%, likely causing tech selloff.
- **Iran nuclear talks breakdown:** Oil spikes, inflation fears return, multiples compress.
- **MU earnings beat + raised guidance:** Could extend the AI rally into year-end.
- **Weak durable goods:** Would signal CapEx slowdown, hitting AI infrastructure names.

### 5 Concise Takeaways

1. **Micron earnings (Wed) is the week's defining event** — it's a pulse check for the entire AI hardware cycle. The stock has tripled this year; expectations are high.
2. **The Iran peace deal is a genuine macro positive** — lower oil reduces inflation pressure and supports growth-stock multiples — but execution risk remains (nuclear talks postponed).
3. **The Fed under Warsh is hawkish** — nine policymakers see a 2026 rate hike. Thursday's PCE data is the most important macro print of the week for tech.
4. **Intel's Apple deal is a significant narrative shift for INTC's foundry story** — but the details are sparse, and INTC is still years behind TSM on process technology.
5. **Expect a relatively quiet Monday open** as markets return from Juneteenth, with positioning for macro data and earnings building through the week.
