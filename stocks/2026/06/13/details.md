## 5. Sector Outlook

### Semiconductors (SOXX, SMH)
**Bull case:** Thursday's 7.9% SOXX surge showed immense pent-up demand for chip exposure on any positive catalyst. The Broadcom selloff from the prior week appears mostly washed out. Vera CPU momentum, HBM demand, and data center buildout remain fundamentally intact. Valuations have reset from June 3 highs.

**Bear case:** The semiconductor index is still down ~9% from its June 2 peak. Rate hike risk directly pressures high-P/E names. AMD at 68x forward earnings is expensive. NVDA at 24x forward is cheap on earnings but could compress further if macro deteriorates.

**Key tickers:** NVDA, AMD, INTC, AVGO, MU, MRVL, ARM, TSM, ASML
**Catalysts:** FOMC decision (Wed), any export control headlines, Vera CPU China adoption news

### AI Infrastructure
**Bull case:** S&P Global upgraded NVDA to AA, projecting $544B revenue by FY28. The Apple Private Cloud Compute deal with NVDA/Google Cloud validates enterprise AI infrastructure demand. Oracle raising $20B for AI buildout is a positive for the capex cycle.

**Bear case:** Capex-dependent. If rising rates squeeze capital availability, hyperscaler buildout could slow. NVDA's China revenue has already collapsed; Vera CPU is a partial offset but unproven at scale.

**Key tickers:** NVDA, SMCI, DELL, ANET, VRT, AMD

### Mega-cap Platforms (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META)
**Bull case:** MEGA caps are showing pricing power. AAPL got multiple analyst PT raises despite WWDC not wowing. META's 6 GW AMD deployment shows AI capex is accelerating. AMZN and GOOGL continue building custom silicon.

**Bear case:** Hedge funds were selling Mag-7 to free up cash for SpaceX (per JPMorgan). AAPL WWDC was a "buy the rumor, sell the news" event. Siri AI delayed in EU and China (35% of iPhone shipments per Morgan Stanley). Rate hike risk directly pressures mega-cap multiples.

**Key tickers:** AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META

### Cloud and Software
**Bull case:** Enterprise AI adoption is driving a multi-year upgrade cycle. Adobe beat but fell on CFO departure — operational trends remain intact.

**Bear case:** Oracle's -8.5% drop despite a beat shows the market is punishing any aggressive capex plans. Software valuations are still extended.

**Key tickers:** ORCL, CRM, ADBE, NOW, SNOW

### Cybersecurity
No major developments this past week. The sector remains a relative safe haven within tech given its secular growth drivers. FOMC decisions may affect the group's multiples.

**Key tickers:** PANW, CRWD, DDOG, NET

### Consumer Hardware
Apple WWDC was the big event. The Siri AI rollout was underwhelming to investors. The next major catalyst is the September iPhone event (the first under incoming CEO John Ternus).

**Key tickers:** AAPL

### EV and Autonomy

**Key tickers:** TSLA

## 7. Macro Setup For Tech

### Rates
- **10-year Treasury yield:** ~4.05% after dropping 7.3 bps Thursday on the Iran de-escalation and oil price decline. The yield had been above 4.15% intraday Wednesday.
- **30-year yield:** Broke below 5% after the Iran news.
- **Fed funds rate:** 3.50–3.75%. No change expected at this meeting, but the dot plot and Warsh's tone are critical.

### Inflation Expectations
- **May CPI:** 4.2% YoY — highest in 3 years. Core CPI at 2.9%.
- **May PPI:** +0.7% MoM, +6.0% YoY — hot enough to reinforce the "no cuts, possible hike" narrative.
- **May NFP:** 172K vs 85K expected — labor market remains too strong for the Fed to ease.

### Fed Expectations (CME FedWatch)
- **Next FOMC (June 17):** 98% probability of **no change** at 3.50–3.75%.
- **December 2026:** Slight majority pricing a **25bp rate hike** — a stark reversal from January when three cuts were expected.
- **Key risk:** If the dot plot shows a median of one or more hikes in 2026, growth/tech stocks will sell off.

### Dollar (DXY)
- The dollar was mixed. The Iran de-escalation weakened safe-haven demand for USD. A hawkish Fed next week would strengthen the dollar and pressure tech.

### Oil
- **WTI crude:** Fell ~7% to ~$86/bbl on the Iran truce news. Lower oil is a net positive for tech (lower input costs, consumer spending boost), but also signals weaker growth demand if driven by recession fears. In this case, the move was geopolitical, not demand-driven.

### Geopolitical Factors
- **Middle East:** The "imminent deal" pattern has proven unreliable multiple times since March. Any setback in Iran negotiations would reverse the risk-on trade immediately.
- **G7 Summit (Mon-Wed):** Trade, tariffs, and China policy are on the agenda. Any new semiconductor export controls targeting China would be a negative for NVDA, AMD, and equipment makers.

3. **MU** — Memory cycle leader; erased weekly losses with Thursday's +11.7%. HBM demand remains structurally strong.

### Biggest risks
1. **Hawkish FOMC surprise** — If the dot plot shows a median rate hike in 2026 or Warsh signals concern about inflation persistence, the semiconductor rally will likely reverse.
2. **Middle East re-escalation** — The "imminent deal" pattern has failed before. Any return to Iran tensions would crush the risk-on trade.
3. **G7 export controls** — Any new China chip restrictions from the G7 Summit would specifically hit NVDA, AMD, ASML and equipment makers.
4. **Inflation data hangover** — CPI at 4.2% + PPI at 6.0% may reset rate expectations higher regardless of the dot plot.

### 5 Concise Takeaways

1. **FOMC is everything this week.** Wednesday's rate decision, dot plot, and Warsh presser will determine whether the Thursday semi rebound is the start of a new leg higher or a dead-cat bounce. No rate change is expected, but the dots and tone are live.
2. **The semiconductor recovery trade is real but fragile.** SOXX surged 7.9% Thursday, but the index is still 9% off its June 2 peak. The recovery is positioning-driven rather than fundamentally broken — AI infrastructure demand remains structurally intact.
3. **Inflation is the 800-lb gorilla.** CPI at 4.2% and PPI at 6.0% mean the Fed is trapped. Markets now price a hike by December. Any confirmation of this view from the dot plot will pressure the entire tech complex.
4. **INTC has the strongest setup in years.** The BofA double upgrade and CPU order cycle narrative have created momentum not seen in Intel for years. The Apple/Tesla deal speculation adds optionality.
5. **Juneteenth closes markets Friday.** The four-day trading week could amplify moves around the Wednesday FOMC — expect positioning to be front-loaded into Monday and Tuesday, with residual volatility into Thursday.
