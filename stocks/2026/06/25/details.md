## 1. Executive Summary

- **Micron (MU) saved the week.** After a brutal three-day selloff that wiped over $1 trillion from the Nasdaq 100, Micron's after-hours earnings report on Wednesday delivered a staggering beat: Q3 revenue of $41.5B (vs. $35.7B consensus), EPS of $25.11 (vs. $20.49), and Q4 guidance of ~$50B (vs. $43.2B consensus). Shares surged ~18% in premarket — potentially adding ~$200B in market cap.

- **Qualcomm (QCOM) added fuel.** At its Investor Day, Qualcomm unveiled a $15B data center revenue target by FY2029 and a multi-generation deal to supply data center CPUs to Meta. QCOM surged ~12-15% premarket.

- **The AI narrative shifted decisively.** The market entered the week gripped by AI bubble fears, triggered by SK Hynix concerns and leveraged ETF dynamics in Korea. Micron's results and its 16 strategic customer agreements (SCAs) locking in pricing through 2030 provided the first concrete evidence that AI memory demand is structural, not cyclical.

- **PCE inflation data drops today.** The May PCE report (8:30 AM ET) is expected to show headline PCE rising to 4.1% YoY — the highest since April 2023 — and core PCE at 3.4% YoY. A hot print could reignite rate-hike fears, but falling oil prices suggest May may be the inflation peak.

- **Oil continues to slide.** Brent crude is back to pre-war levels around $69/barrel, providing a tailwind for consumer spending and corporate margins while reducing near-term inflationary pressure.

- **Asian and European markets rallied on the Micron read-through.** South Korea's SK Hynix surged 13%, Samsung Electronics rose 5.3%, Japan's Nikkei gained 4.6%, and European semiconductor names (ASML +4%, Infineon +3-6%) all moved higher.

- **Stocks most likely to move today:** MU (earnings blowout), QCOM (Investor Day AI targets), NVDA (halo effect from memory demand read-through), WDC/SNDK (memory peer rally), and ASML (semiconductor equipment read-through).

## 3. Market-Moving News From The Last 48 Hours

### AI and Data Center

- **Micron (MU) reports record Q3: $41.5B revenue, 346% YoY growth.** Net income of $28.9B, gross margin of 84.9%. The company signed 16 "strategic customer agreements" (SCAs) locking in pricing through 2030, with customers committing $22B in deposits. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said there is "no line of sight" to AI memory supply catching up with demand, with shortages expected past 2027. Guided Q4 revenue of ~$50B (vs. $43.2B consensus) and EPS of ~$31.
  - **Impact: Bullish** — Source: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/25/micron-tech-stock-rally-ai-bubble/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/06/25/micron-locks-in-historically-high-memory-prices-for-five-years/5261854/), [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/06/24/micron-just-guided-for-a-staggering-50-billion-in/)

- **Qualcomm (QCOM) Investor Day: $15B data center revenue target by 2029.** QCOM announced a multi-generation deal to supply data center CPUs to Meta (META), targeting AI inference workloads with its Dragonfly portfolio. The previous non-handset revenue target was $22B by 2029; the new $40B target more than doubles it, with $15B specifically from data center.
  - **Impact: Bullish for QCOM; competitive signal for NVDA/AMD** — Source: [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/stocktwits:1f0867e1e094b:0-nasdaq-s-p-500-futures-jump-as-micron-qualcomm-reignite-ai-rally-all-eyes-on-pce-data-why-spcx-baba-bb-frmi-wen-adtx-are-in-focus/)

- **Nvidia (NVDA) and AWS deepen AI infrastructure partnership.** New EC2 G7 instances with RTX PRO 4500 GPUs offer up to 4.6x improvement in AI inference performance. GPU-accelerated vector search in Amazon OpenSearch Serverless delivers up to 10x faster performance at 1/4 the cost.
  - **Impact: Bullish for NVDA (ecosystem widening)** — Source: [Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/news/nvidia-aws-ai-infrastructure-production)

- **Nvidia unveils six-part agentic AI stack at Computex.** Products include Vera CPU (purpose-built for agentic/reinforcement-learning workloads), Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B parameter open model), and OpenShell (policy-driven sandbox). Early adopters: OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX.

## 7. Outlook For Today

**Trading Setup:** The stage is set for a strong risk-on open, led by semiconductors. Nasdaq 100 futures are pointing to the strongest opening in weeks (+2.1%). The key variable is the PCE data at 8:30 AM ET — an in-line print (4.1% YoY) would likely be dismissed as "peak inflation" and support the rally; a hot surprise could drive intraday reversal.

**Main Upside Catalysts:**
- Micron's earnings fundamentally reshaped the AI narrative from "bubble fears" to "structural demand confirmed"
- Qualcomm's data center ambitions open a new growth vector beyond smartphones
- Falling oil prices (back to ~$69) ease macro pressure on consumer spending and input costs
- Deep value rotation: the Mag 7 correction (-11.6% from peak) has reset valuations on names like NVDA (fwd P/E ~24x)

**Main Downside Risks:**
- Hot PCE print could spike yields and reignite rate-hike fears, overwhelming the Micron tailwind
- FedEx's operating margin decline (7.7% from 8.4%) signals cost pressures beyond tech
- The Mag 7 remains in correction territory; recovery may take time
- MU at +18% premarket: risk of profit-taking after an already massive move

**Strongest Subsectors:** Memory/HBM (MU, SNDK, WDC, STX), AI semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, AVGO), data center CPU plays (QCOM, ARM)

**Weakest Subsectors:** Traditional software (CRM still beaten down despite upgrade), transports (FDX margin pressure), energy (oil decline)

**Nature of Moves:** Today's rally is fundamentally driven (Micron earnings) after several days of positioning-driven selling. This gives it more durability than a short-covering bounce.

**What Would Change This View:** A PCE print substantially above 4.1% (spiking yields, tanking futures); profit-taking on MU that spreads to the broader sector; or unexpected negative regulatory news (China export controls, antitrust).

## 8. Risk Notes

- **Micron's SCAs cut both ways.** While the floor price guarantees margins, the ceiling price means customers are protected if prices rise further. Micron is reserving 60% of inventory for non-SCA spot pricing — any demand softening would hit that portion hardest.
- **PCE consensus is for a peak.** The "peak inflation" call has been wrong before (2021-2023). If May data surprises to the upside, the Fed-hike narrative could accelerate.
- **Korea KOSPI leveraged ETF risk.** The Independent and multiple sources flagged leveraged single-stock ETF structures in Korea as having amplified the selloff. The unwind of those positions may not be complete.
- **Mag 7 technical damage.** At -11.6% from May 28 peak, the Mag 7 is in correction. The equal-weight S&P 500 held up better (+0.71% on Wednesday), suggesting the selloff was tech-specific.
- **Qualcomm's $15B data center target is aspirational.** The company has no meaningful data center GPU presence today. Execution risk is significant, and Nvidia's ecosystem moat (CUDA, networking, full-stack) is formidable.
- **AMD valuation risk.** At ~170x trailing P/E, AMD prices in years of flawless execution and major market share gains — outcomes that are possible but far from guaranteed.

## 9. Bottom Line

1. **Micron's results were the most important earnings of the quarter for AI.** The $50B Q4 guide, 86% gross margin, and 5-year contracted revenue structure provide the strongest evidence yet that AI memory demand is structural and durable. The "AI bubble" narrative suffered a major blow.

2. **Semiconductors lead today, but breadth matters.** If the PCE data cooperates and the rally broadens beyond chips, today could mark a turning point for the entire tech sector after the week's steep losses.

3. **Memory names are the clear near-term winners.** MU, SNDK, WDC, and STX all have direct earnings validation. The SK Hynix US listing adds a new vector for capital flowing into the memory space.

4. **Watch for QCOM as a structural AI beneficiary.** The Meta CPU partnership and $15B data center target signal that Qualcomm is no longer just a smartphone chip company. If Morgan Stanley does upgrade, the sentiment shift would be significant.

5. **Macro is still the wildcard.** The PCE data, Fed expectations (65% odds of a September hike), and elevated yields (10-year at 4.415%) all constrain how far tech multiples can expand regardless of earnings momentum.
