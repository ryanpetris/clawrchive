## 1. Executive Summary

- **Futures surge:** Nasdaq-100 futures +1.1-1.2%, S&P 500 futures +0.7-0.8%, Dow futures +0.5% as markets reopen after the Memorial Day holiday. Retail sentiment on SPY and QQQ remains "bullish" but has cooled slightly (Stocktwits).

- **Semiconductor-led rally:** Chip stocks are the standout premarket leaders. Micron (MU) +6% on FTSE Russell reclassification to Growth indexes + Iran-peace optimism. Marvell (MRVL) +5.7% premarket ahead of Wednesday earnings. AMD and Qualcomm both +3%.

- **Michael Burry warns on AI:** "The Big Short" investor renewed criticism of AI spending over the weekend, warning hyperscalers and chipmakers may be overextending into "temporary" demand.
- **Macro crosscurrents:** 10-year Treasury yield at ~4.40-4.50%, 30-year above 5%. The Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh is expected to maintain a hawkish bias. CPI at 3.8% YoY, with rate cuts no longer expected in 2026.
- **Market tone:** AI-led, semiconductor-led bullish open with event-driven and geopolitical overlay. Risk-on but fragile.

**Top stocks likely to move today:** MU (earnings catalyst + index reclassification), MRVL (earnings preview + analyst upgrades), AMD (inference AI narrative + premarket strength), NVDA (post-earnings digestion), TSLA (continued upward drift).

The market returns after a three-day weekend (Memorial Day Friday close + Monday holiday) and will process accumulated news from the weekend: US-Iran deal optimism, Nvidia earnings afterglow, fresh US strikes on Iran, and PCE/growth data looming later this week.

**Main Upside Catalysts:**
- US-Iran peace deal narrative (Trump says talks "proceeding nicely") — even if fragile, the direction lowers the tail risk premium
- Nvidia earnings beat still providing AI-sector tailwinds
- FTSE Russell reclassification of MU/SNDK to Growth indexes forces passive buying
- MRVL Street-high PTs and earnings momentum into Wednesday report

**Main Downside Risks:**
- Fresh US-Iran strikes could escalate; IRGC has vowed retaliation — Brent crude bouncing 3% to ~$99
- 10-year yield at 4.4-4.5%, 30-year above 5% — Morgan Stanley says "danger zone"
- Fed pivot to potential rate hikes under Warsh could pressure AI capex narrative
- Michael Burry's AI skepticism adds to lingering valuation concerns

**Strongest Subsectors:** AI semiconductors (MU, MRVL, AMD), memory (MU, SNDK), AI infrastructure, data center networking.

**Weakest Subsectors:** Energy (Exxon, Chevron falling on peace deal hopes), consumer discretionary if yields keep rising.

**Move Drivers:** Today's moves appear primarily **geopolitical event-driven** with a strong **AI/narrative overlay**. The semiconductor leadership is consistent with both themes.

**What would change this view?** A significant Iranian retaliation (breaking the fragile ceasefire), a sharp spike in yields above 4.75%, or signs of demand destruction in memory/GPU end markets. Conversely, confirmed Iran deal framework with Strait of Hormuz reopening would be a massive equity tailwind.
