## 7. Outlook For Today

**Expected trading setup:** Positive open extending Tuesday's record closes. AI/semiconductor-led, but three major earnings after the close (MRVL, CRM, SNOW) and PCE on Thursday create an event-driven tape for the afternoon.

**Main upside catalysts:**
- Continued AI rotation into memory (MU), networking (MRVL optical), and CPU plays (AMD, INTC)
- Falling oil prices and declining Treasury yields supporting growth stock valuations
- Record S&P 500 earnings season momentum and Goldman's target raise providing sentiment tailwind
- MRVL beat-and-raise could ignite another AI networking rally

**Main downside risks:**
- Zscaler's 20%+ premarket drop on modest guidance miss signals low tolerance for any weakness
- MRVL priced for perfection after 130% YTD surge — any disappointment could trigger sharp reversal
- PCE Thursday: hot inflation print would reset rate expectations and pressure multiples
- Nvidia continues to struggle to hold gains despite record results — leadership concern
- US-Iran escalation could spike oil and pressure risk appetite

**Strongest subsectors:** Memory/semiconductors (MU-led), AI networking/optics (MRVL-led), AI CPUs (AMD, INTC)
**Weakest subsectors:** Cybersecurity (ZS cautionary signal), legacy enterprise software (CRM/SNOW as test)

**Move characterization:** Fundamentals-driven in semiconductors/AI infrastructure, with positioning-driven dynamics in high-momentum names (MRVL). Macro risk (PCE, geopolitics) remains the wild card.

**What would change this view:**
- MRVL guidance disappointment → risk-off in AI networking names
- PCE > 0.30% MoM → rates-driven tech sell-off, especially for high-duration growth
- US-Iran ceasefire breakthrough → further oil decline, broad risk rally
- NVDA breach of $200 → structural concern could spread across AI complex

## 8. Risk Notes

- **MRVL is priced for perfection.** After a 130% YTD surge, even a solid beat may not satisfy the market. Insider selling of $29.9M over 3 months with zero insider buying is a cautionary signal.
- **Zscaler's 20%+ drop** despite beating Q3 estimates (EPS $1.08 vs $1.01) shows the market is punishing even modest guidance misses. Watch for read-through to other growth/cybersecurity names.
- **Nvidia's muted post-earnings reaction persists.** The stock remains roughly 9% below its May high despite record revenue and guidance. Technical risk if $212 support (20-day SMA) breaks — potential path to $200.
- **Micron's 19% surge** is vulnerable to profit-taking. UBS target of $1,625 may have already been partially priced in by Tuesday's move.
- **Michael Burry issued a public warning** on AI exuberance and crowded momentum trades. While one bearish view, it adds to the "too far, too fast" narrative for some AI names.
- **PCE Thursday is binary for rates.** Market prices ~62% chance of a September cut; a hot print would reset those odds and pressure the entire tech sector.

## 9. Bottom Line

1. **The AI rally is rotating, not ending.** The leadership shift from pure GPU plays (NVDA) into memory (MU), networking (MRVL), and CPUs (AMD, INTC) suggests broadening AI infrastructure demand rather than peaking. This is healthy for the sector overall.
2. **Today is event-driven.** MRVL, CRM, and SNOW earnings after the close dominate. MRVL is the most consequential — a beat-and-raise validates the AI networking theme; a miss would be a sector-level negative. CRM and SNOW matter for enterprise software and AI data demand signals.
3. **PCE Thursday is the macro test.** A benign print keeps the "strong earnings + stable rates" narrative intact. A hot print is the most likely catalyst for a meaningful tech pullback after the recent record run.
4. **Nvidia at $215 is the most important stock to watch.** It's not in freefall, but its inability to rally on blowout results raises a yellow flag. The stock needs to hold $212 support to avoid wider AI-complex contagion.
5. **Position for breadth, not just momentum.** The AI trade is broadening into memory, networking, and infrastructure. MU at $1T market cap and AMD at $500+ reflect genuine demand expansion, not just NVDA multiple expansion.
