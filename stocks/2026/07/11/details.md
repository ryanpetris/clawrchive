## 2. Week Ahead Summary

The week of July 13 is the most consequential macro week in months. **Three binary events** converge: the June CPI report (Tue), TSMC Q2 earnings (Thu), and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's testimony before the House Financial Services Committee. Q2 earnings season kicks off in earnest with big bank results and ASML's report.

**Overall expected tone:** Event-driven, AI-led, with elevated macro sensitivity.

**Highest-conviction catalysts:**
1. **June CPI (Tue 8:30 AM ET)** — Consensus expects headline CPI -0.1% MoM (first decline in months, driven by lower gasoline) and core CPI +0.3% MoM. Year-over-year, headline expected at ~3.9% and core at ~2.9%. A downside surprise would fuel rate-cut speculation; an upside surprise could cement rate-hike expectations.
2. **TSMC Q2 earnings (Thu)** — The definitive AI supercycle temperature check. TSMC is the sole manufacturer of Nvidia's and AMD's most advanced AI chips. Any guidance change ripples through the entire semiconductor supply chain.
3. **Fed Chair Warsh testimony (Thu)** — First major public appearance since the Iran ceasefire collapse. Markets will parse every word on the rate path, inflation outlook, and Middle East economic risks.
4. **ASML Q2 results (Wed)** — Semiconductor equipment bellwether. ASML's order backlog and EUV lithography machine shipments are leading indicators for chip industry investment.
5. **Netflix Q2 earnings (Thu after close)** — First major tech platform earnings of the season. Consumer spending trends, ad-tier growth, and content investment signals will set the tone for consumer tech.

## 5. Sector Outlook

### Semiconductors

**Bull case:** AI infrastructure spending shows no signs of slowing. Global cloud and AI capex projected to approach $1.5T by 2027. SK Hynix's successful $26.5B IPO validates the memory chip thesis. TSMC and ASML results will likely confirm strong demand.

**Bear case:** The "most crowded trade in the world" (per Great Hill Capital). Custom AI chips (Meta's Iris, Google's TPU) could reduce dependency on merchant silicon. Memory oversupply fears. Divergence between winners (NVDA, SK Hynix) and losers (MU losing share).

**Key tickers to watch:** NVDA, AMD, AVGO, TSM, ASML, MU, ARM, INTC

**Likely catalysts:** TSMC earnings, ASML earnings, SOXX technical levels

### AI Infrastructure

**Bull case:** Meta's custom Iris chip (production Sept 2026) plus Broadcom's $30B Apple chip deal demonstrate that AI infrastructure buildout spending is durable. Hyperscaler spending to "still more than double" (Benchmark Research).

**Bear case:** Meta's cost efficiency (half of Street estimates) may mean lower spending growth ahead. SpaceX down 25% from IPO peak signals caution on AI hype names.

**Key tickers to watch:** NVDA, META, AVGO, SMCI, DELL, ANET, VRT

### Mega-Cap Platforms

**Bull case:** META +14% in 5 days on AI chip news. Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet all benefiting from cloud AI workloads. Tech earnings growth expected to lead the S&P 500.

**Bear case:** European regulators accuse Meta of "addictive design" — regulatory risk remains. Alphabet and Amazon underperforming. High expectations leave little room for error.

**Key tickers to watch:** META, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, AAPL

## 7. Macro Setup for Tech

**Rates:** The 10-year yield at 4.57% is a headwind for growth stocks, but the market has been looking through it. Kalshi sees ~50% odds of a Fed hike in 2026. Fed Chair Warsh's testimony Thursday will be the key event.

**Inflation:** The June CPI report (Tuesday) is the most important data point this week. A headline decline (consensus -0.1% MoM) would be the first monthly drop in months, driven by lower gasoline prices after the U.S.-Iran MOU in mid-June. However, core inflation remains sticky at 2.9% YoY. RBC warns: "inflation will remain sticky for the remainder of 2026."

**Geopolitical risk:** The U.S.-Iran ceasefire has collapsed. The Strait of Hormuz is running at ~13% of normal traffic. U.S. and Iran traded strikes this week. Oxford Economics warns of a "cascade" of economic consequences if the peace deal breaks — higher oil prices, pressure on AI supply chains in Asia, hawkish central banks, tighter financial conditions. However, NY Fed's Williams said he doesn't expect a sustained energy price increase. Technical talks continue.

**Dollar:** DXY at 101, stable. A weaker dollar would help tech exports; a stronger dollar would be a headwind.

**Oil:** WTI $71.50, Brent $76. Oil prices fell Friday as Trump confirmed peace talks continue despite the ceasefire being "over." If the Strait of Hormuz reopens, oil could fall to $60-70. If conflict escalates, $90-120 is possible.

## 9. Final Weekly Outlook

**Best-looking setups:**
1. **META** — AI chip cost efficiency narrative has strong momentum. The custom Iris chip plan (Sept 2026) fundamentally changes the AI infrastructure cost equation. +14% in 5 days is extended, but the fundamental story is improving.
2. **NVDA** — TSMC earnings will likely confirm the AI supercycle remains intact. Despite Meta's custom chip plans, hyperscaler spending is projected to "still more than double" (Benchmark Research).
3. **AVGO** — $30B Apple chip deal provides long-term revenue visibility. Up 11% on the week but still has room if TSMC and ASML results are strong.
4. **PLTR** — Testing a historically strong support zone ($122-135) with a business firing on all cylinders. Rackspace partnership opens regulated enterprise markets. The scaling constraint risk is real but the fundamental picture is the strongest it's ever been.

**Biggest risks:**
1. **CPI upside surprise** — If June CPI comes in hotter than expected, it would cement rate-hike expectations and hit growth stock multiples hard.
2. **TSMC guidance disappointment** — If TSMC signals any demand softening, it would trigger a broad semiconductor sell-off. The entire AI trade is priced for perfection.
3. **Iran escalation** — If U.S.-Iran talks break down completely and oil spikes toward $90-120, the macro environment would deteriorate sharply for tech.
4. **META profit-taking** — Five consecutive days of +14% gains is vulnerable to a pullback, especially on no new fundamental catalyst.
5. **Index concentration risk** — NVDA and META alone drove 0.38pp of S&P 500 gains Friday. A reversal in either would have an outsized impact.

**Setup for the first market open (Monday):**
- Futures were flat-to-slightly-positive Friday afternoon
- Asian markets rallied on the SK Hynix listing (KOSPI +4%, Nikkei +1.5%)
- Oil prices stable but elevated
- Key risk: any weekend developments in U.S.-Iran conflict
- Key opportunity: SK Hynix (SKHY) begins regular Nasdaq trading

**Key data or events that could change the outlook:**
- June CPI below consensus (especially headline below 3.7% YoY) → risk-on, rate-cut hopes
- June CPI above 4.2% YoY → risk-off, rate-hike fears
- TSMC guides above consensus → semiconductor-led rally
- TSMC guidance in line or below → semis sell off
- Warsh signals willingness to hike → tech multiples compress
- U.S.-Iran de-escalation → oil drops, macro relief rally

**5 Takeaways:**

1. **The AI supercycle faces its biggest test of the summer** — TSMC and ASML earnings this week will confirm or challenge the AI infrastructure spending thesis that has driven the market. This is the most important week for semis all quarter.

2. **CPI is the macro binary event** — The first potential monthly decline in headline CPI in months. A miss low would be a massive relief for growth stocks. A surprise high would be dangerous.

3. **SK Hynix changes the memory chip landscape** — The $26.5B IPO success validates the AI memory thesis. Regular trading starts Monday. Competition between SK Hynix and Micron will intensify, and investors now have a direct U.S.-listed pure play on HBM memory.

4. **Meta's AI cost efficiency is the sleeper story** — If Meta can build AI capacity at half the cost Wall Street modeled, it changes the competitive dynamics of the AI infrastructure buildout. It's bullish for Meta but potentially bearish for Nvidia if it signals broader hyperscaler de-commoditization.

5. **Geopolitical risk is underpriced** — The VIX at 15 when the U.S. and Iran are actively trading strikes and the Strait of Hormuz is at 13% of normal traffic is remarkable. The market is clearly prioritizing the AI capex narrative over geopolitics, but that positioning is vulnerable to a sudden shock.
