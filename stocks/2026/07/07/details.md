# Tech Stock Briefing — July 7, 2026 — Apple-Broadcom AI Chip Deal, Nasdaq Rotation, Fed Hawkishness

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market tone: Mixed / AI-led rotation.** Nasdaq futures down ~0.8% as semiconductors slip, while the Dow hit a record close above 53,000 on Monday. The divergence between mega-cap tech and cyclical/value continues.
- **Apple + Broadcom extend custom AI chip partnership through 2031** for "Baltra" server AI processor. AAPL rose 1.6%, AVGO 4-6% — a major structural re-entry for Broadcom into Apple's infrastructure stack.
- **Microsoft cuts 4,800 jobs (2.1% of workforce)** — Xbox hit hardest with 3,200 cuts. New Xbox CEO Asha Sharma calls it "the most significant restructure in Xbox history." MSFT shares fell ~1%.

- **Fed Governor Waller flagged inflation as the primary risk**, reversing his prior dovish stance. Market now prices ~25% odds of a July hike. CPI data on July 14 becomes critical.
- **Samsung Q2 operating profit surged 18-fold to 89.4T won (~$58B)** — but shares fell 6% in Seoul on "sell the news" dynamics. Memory chip stocks under pressure.
- **A new tanker strike in the Strait of Hormuz** — Iranian missiles hit two commercial vessels, setting an LNG tanker ablaze. Oil edges higher (Brent $72.29). Geopolitical risk premium revisiting markets.
- **AMD surged 6.6-9% on Monday** on Wells Fargo upgrade ($615 PT) and Turing design win. The AMD vs NVDA rotation trade continues. NVDA was flat (+0.4%).
- **PLTR rallied 24% in a 6-day win streak** on sovereign AI partnership with NVIDIA, D.A. Davidson upgrade ($175 PT), and U.S. Army NGC2 contract selection.
- **AI infrastructure spending hits record levels** — Big Tech combined 2026 CapEx of ~$715B, nearly double 2025. Amazon leads at $200B, Google and Microsoft ~$190B each.

## 8. Risk Notes

- ⚠️ **AMD valuation risk**: AMD trades at 180x trailing earnings and 285x forward earnings. The stock has re-priced as though AI GPU dominance is already achieved, but fundamentals (35% revenue growth, 11.65% operating margins) don't support the trajectory.
- ⚠️ **NVDA concentration risk**: 50% of data center revenue comes from hyperscalers actively funding custom chip alternatives. Insider net selling (16 recent transactions). The NVLink Fusion strategy keeps the interconnect sticky but concedes the GPU compute battle.
- ⚠️ **Memory pricing vulnerability**: MU up 247.8% YTD but dropped 11% in a single session. Sustained supply restraint is the thesis — if competitors flood the market, pricing power collapses.
- ⚠️ **Geopolitical binary risk**: Hormuz situation is unpredictable — escalation and de-escalation within the same week has become the pattern. Mid-August toll deadline is a specific date to watch.
- ⚠️ **AI capex ROI question**: $715B in combined Big Tech CapEx with unclear returns. Jim Chanos has raised concerns about accounting mismatches. Upcoming earnings (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META) will test the narrative.
- ⚠️ **PLTR insider selling**: Over a recent 3-month period, insiders sold 9 shares for every 1 bought. Peter Thiel sold 2M+ shares in March. CEO Karp sold $65.9M in February. Michael Burry still holds large put positions.

## 9. Bottom Line

1. **Apple-Broadcom is the week's biggest structural story.** The 2031 custom AI chip deal locks in multi-year revenue for AVGO and signals Apple's serious AI infrastructure buildout. The "Baltra" processor is a real catalyst.
2. **The AMD/NVDA rotation has room to run near-term, but AMD's valuation is stretched.** AMD at 180x earnings is pricing in perfection. NVDA at 30x with 71% growth is historically cheap by comparison. Watch for a reversal.
3. **SpaceX Nasdaq 100 inclusion today is a major liquidity event.** $35-40B in passive buying expected. This is a one-time structural flow that will affect QQQ/QQQM performance.
4. **Fed pivot is the biggest macro risk for tech.** Waller's hawkish shift and 25% July hike odds mean the CPI print on July 14 is the most important data point before earnings season.
5. **Memory chip euphoria is showing cracks.** Samsung's 18x profit surge met with a 6% stock decline. Micron's 11% single-day drop last week. The pricing power thesis is intact but the "easy money" in memory is likely behind us.
