# Research Notes — July 5, 2026

## Deeper Analysis: The Semiconductor Rotation

### Is This 2024 or the Dot-Com Era?

JPMorgan's comparison to dot-com-era divergence between hyperscaler spenders and chip stocks is the most important framing for this week. The bank's strategists (led by Nikolaos Panigirtzoglou) explicitly flagged that the growing divergence between big spenders and hardware stocks was seen in markets leading up to the dot-com crash. The bank leans toward the "bullish scenario" (hyperscalers see AI monetization improvement and catch up), but flagged that the "bearish scenario" (pullback in capex, creating a feedback loop) is a real risk.

Key stat: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft are on track to spend as much as **$725 billion** on capex this year. Goldman Sachs projects AI capex from the four could exceed Japan's GDP by end of decade.

### The Burry Critique: GPU Depreciation

The 24/7 Wall St piece on the "Michael Burry bear case" is worth taking seriously. The argument: NVDA's $119B in supply commitments and AMD's 192x trailing P/E both unravel if GPU depreciation schedules shorten by a couple of years. This is not a near-term Q2 or Q3 concern, but it's a structural debate that could keep a lid on AI hardware multiples.

Polymarket traders give NVDA only 49.5% probability of closing above $200 by end of July — and that crowd has been right 74.4% of the time across 195 resolved markets.

### NVDA Valuation Anomaly

This is the most striking data point of the weekend: NVDA at 22x forward earnings, vs KO at 26x. NVDA revenue growth 85%, KO growth 4-5%. The valuation inversion is not rational on fundamentals — it's entirely sentiment-driven. Whether this is a buying opportunity or a value trap depends on whether the AI capex cycle extends beyond 2027.

The "healthy pause" view: Even if growth slows dramatically from 85% to, say, 30%, NVDA at 22x would still be cheap. The risk: growth stops accelerating and the multiple re-rates lower in anticipation.

### AMD: Structural Risk or Overreaction?

AMD's fall was the purest single-story event of the week. The Meta connection is unique:

1. Meta committed to up to 6 GW of Instinct GPUs
2. Meta holds a warrant for ~10% of AMD's shares
3. Meta signaled it may have more AI compute than it needs
4. Northland Capital downgraded AMD to Market Perform citing over-optimistic 2027 EPS estimates

The MI450 timing and Meta's own earnings call (late July) are the next clearing events. If Meta's capex guidance remains intact or increases, the selloff was an overreaction. If Meta lowers capex, AMD has further to fall.

AMD's GPU price increase (10% on kits, driven by GDDR6 memory costs) adds another margin question.

## Plausible Bull Case

A thoughtful synthesis: The rotation out of semis and into mega-cap platforms could be the healthiest development for the AI trade since the start of 2026. It reduces concentration risk, broadens market participation, and resets valuations on the most extended names. If AI monetization starts showing in mega-cap earnings (beginning late July), the semi names that sold off into strength look cheap.
